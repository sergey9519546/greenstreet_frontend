The 2026 Product Architecture: DSCR and Non-QM
Matrices Unveiled
The wholesale lending landscape in 2026 is defined by a sophisticated and highly
differentiated product architecture, with Cake Mortgage Corp. leading the charge
through its distinct suite of programs designed for specific borrower and property
profiles. This analysis deconstructs the core offerings—the Debt Service Coverage Ratio
(DSCR) v4.0 program and the array of Non-Qualified Mortgage (Non-QM) matrices—to
reveal the strategic positioning and competitive advantages inherent in each. The
framework has been meticulously structured to provide tailored solutions, moving away
from a one-size-fits-all approach to a nuanced system where selecting the correct product
is paramount to achieving optimal loan terms and maximizing deal value. The
overarching strategy is clear: leverage the pure cash-flow focus of DSCR for investments
while deploying a diverse arsenal of Non-QM programs for primary residences and
second homes, each with unique strengths in handling complex borrower situations,
property types, and income verifications. The most current and authoritative guidelines,
effective from April 2026, have rendered older, more restrictive 2025 matrices obsolete
for most scenarios, creating a new standard for what is possible in the Non-QM space 4
29.
The cornerstone of Cake Mortgage's investment lending division is the DSCR v4.0
program, a purpose-built solution for non-owner-occupied (NOO) properties 9 . Its
defining characteristic is the complete absence of traditional income and debt-to-income
(DTI) qualification metrics. Borrowers are not required to disclose employment history,
file federal tax returns, or undergo a DTI calculation; qualification is based exclusively on
the property's ability to generate rental income sufficient to cover its mortgage payment
8 . The DSCR ratio itself is calculated using a simple but strict formula: Gross Rental
Income ÷ Qualifying Monthly Payment (PITIA or ITIA for Interest-
Only loans), with rounding up explicitly prohibited 9 . This pure-play cash-flow
model makes it exceptionally attractive for investors with complex, variable, or non-

traditional income streams, such as real estate professionals, self-employed individuals,
or those relying on multiple income sources. The program accommodates a wide range of
eligible transactions, including purchases, rate-and-term refinances, cash-out refinances,
delayed financing (cash purchases within 90 days), and 1031 exchanges 9 .
Furthermore, it permits non-arm's-length transactions, provided a letter of explanation is
submitted to document the relationship 9 .
A key differentiator for DSCR v4.0 is its flexible approach to rent qualification. For long-
term rentals, the program uses the higher of the amount stated on the Appraiser's Form
1007/1025 or the current lease agreement, provided the difference between them is 20%
or less 9 . This allows for the use of newly signed, potentially higher-rent leases.
Crucially, the program also embraces the short-term rental (STR) market, which is largely
off-limits for many other Non-QM programs. For STR purchases, an AirDNA Rentalizer
report is accepted as a valid source of rental income, subject to specific conditions: the
report must cover a 12-month forecast, achieve a market score of at least 60, include
three comparable properties, and cap occupancy at two individuals per bedroom 9 32.
However, for STR refinances, the AirDNA report is ineligible, requiring instead either a
12-month rental history log or the standard Form 1007/1025 9 . The program also
demonstrates significant leniency towards vacancy, allowing for 100% vacancy to be
applied to units on 1-4 unit properties when using a Form 1007, effectively ignoring any
potential income loss due to vacancies 9 . This makes it ideal for new construction or
properties being prepared for rental. For larger multifamily properties of 5 to 9 units, the
DSCR program maintains a minimum requirement of a 1.00 ratio. Loans of $2,000,000 or
more on these properties trigger a more stringent Debt Yield test, requiring a Debt Yield
of at least 9%, calculated as Net Operating Income (NOI) divided by the total loan
amount 9 . Multifamily properties also have specific rules regarding vacant units (a
maximum of two are allowed, valued at 75% of market rent) and STR income, which is
deemed ineligible for 5-9 unit properties 9 .

Credit Event Underwriting: The Evolution from
Restriction to Opportunity
The underwriting guidelines for credit events represent the most significant and
strategically impactful evolution in Cake Mortgage's 2026 framework. The transition from
the punitive, multi-year seasoning periods of the 2025 era to the far more
accommodating timelines of the 2026 matrices marks a paradigm shift, not only for Cake
but for the entire Non-QM industry. This change directly addresses the needs of a large
segment of borrowers who, under previous rules, would have been denied financing for
extended periods. The new guidelines demonstrate a sophisticated approach to risk
assessment, replacing blanket prohibitions with a tiered system of LTV adjustments that
allow qualified borrowers to re-enter the market much sooner. This section provides a
deep dive into these new rules, comparing them to their predecessors and decoding the
logic behind the specific timeframes and penalties, thereby equipping loan officers with
the intelligence needed to successfully route deals involving recent credit challenges.
The single most dramatic change is the abolition of the 48-month seasoning requirement
for Bankruptcies (BK), Foreclosures (FC), and similar events that was prevalent in the
archived Funnel Cake matrix from November 2025 22. This outdated rule, which
mandated nearly four years of post-event clean credit before a borrower could even be
considered for a loan, created a massive gap in the market and left countless
homeowners and investors stranded. The 2026 Bundt Cake matrices have completely
overturned this, establishing a new, far more accessible standard. Under the current
guidelines, a borrower with a BK or FC is not automatically disqualified. Instead, they
face a graduated series of LTV reductions based on how recently the event occurred.
Specifically, for events occurring between 12 and 23 months prior to the note date, the
borrower is subject to a 5% CLTV reduction and, crucially, is ineligible for a cash-out
refinance 22. If the event occurred between 24 and 35 months ago, the penalty is
reduced to a 5% CLTV reduction, and cash-out refinancing becomes permissible again
22. Events that occurred less than 12 months ago remain ineligible across all programs
22.
This new framework represents a profound competitive advantage. While competitors
may still enforce longer seasoning periods, Cake Mortgage is actively courting this
borrower segment by offering a viable path to recovery in as little as one year. The
strategic rationale is clear: the secondary market has demonstrated sufficient appetite for
Non-QM paper backed by these borrowers, especially when mitigated by lower LTVs. The
LTV penalty acts as the primary risk mitigation tool, ensuring that the loan balance
remains well-supported by the property's value despite the borrower's past financial

===== PAGE 6 =====

distress. This is further complemented by stricter documentation requirements; for
instance, Bundt Cake NQM requires a 680+ FICO score for borrowers seeking to use
assets to cure a bankruptcy, reinforcing the need for a strong credit profile beyond just
the event itself 22.
The underwriting for other negative credit events follows a similar pattern of graduated
penalties rather than outright disqualifications. For events like Deed-in-Lieu (DIL), Short
Sales, and significant mortgage late payments, the rules are even more nuanced. A DIL or
Short Sale occurring between 12 and 23 months ago triggers a much steeper penalty—a
15% CLTV reduction—and, like a BK/FC, renders the borrower ineligible for a cash-out
refinance during this period 22. After 24 to 35 months, this penalty is reduced back to a
more manageable 5% CLTV reduction 22. This reflects the greater severity associated
with voluntary property relinquishment compared to a dischargeable bankruptcy.
Late payments present another area of granular control. The 2026 guidelines distinguish
between different severities of delinquency. A 0x60x12 late payment (60 days past due,
12 months old) is permitted but results in a 5% CLTV reduction 22. Similarly, a 0x90x12
late payment (90 days past due, 12 months old) is also allowed, but with a 5% CLTV
reduction for Bundt Cake NQM and a maximum CLTV capped at 80% for purchase and
75% for refinance on Cup Cake 22 28. This shows a degree of tolerance for isolated
incidents of severe delinquency. However, the guidelines draw a hard line at more
extreme delinquencies. Across all 2026 Non-QM matrices, a 0x120x12 late payment (120
days past due, 12 months old) is strictly ineligible 22. This creates a critical "deal killer"
that must be identified early in the application process. Missing this detail can lead to
wasted underwriting effort and submission of a loan that will be immediately suspended.
The distinction is particularly sharp in the NOO context; Bundt Cake NOO outright rejects
a 0x90x12 late, whereas Cup Cake allows it under the aforementioned LTV constraints
22 28. This highlights the necessity of choosing the right matrix, as an investor profile
with a 90-day late would be blocked from Bundt Cake NOO but could potentially be
approved through Cup Cake NOO.

Complex Borrower Profiles: Mastering Entity Structures
and Alternative Demographics
Navigating the 2026 Cake Mortgage framework requires a deep and precise
understanding of its protocols for complex borrowers and unconventional property

===== PAGE 8 =====

structures. The guidelines are not merely a checklist but a detailed instruction manual for
underwriting a diverse range of profiles, from foreign nationals and ITIN holders to
borrowers utilizing layer-structured entities. Mastering these rules is essential for
unlocking loan opportunities that would be inaccessible under more rigid lending
standards. This section provides a granular analysis of the underwriting mechanics for
these complex scenarios, focusing on the critical nuances in entity formation, guarantor
requirements, and the specific documentation needed for alternative demographics. It
also decodes the hidden operational risks and "deal killers" that can derail a transaction if
overlooked, empowering loan officers to confidently handle the most challenging
borrower profiles.
The rules surrounding entity vesting and personal guarantees are among the most critical
and frequently misunderstood aspects of the 2026 guidelines. When a loan is made to an
entity such as an LLC, Corporation, or Partnership, a Personal Guarantor is absolutely
required 25. This is a non-negotiable component of the underwriting package. The
guidelines specify that the guarantor(s) must collectively represent at least 51%
cumulative ownership of the entity and possess the legal authority to sign on its behalf
25. Furthermore, the entity itself is limited to a maximum of four owners 25. This
structure ensures that there is always an individual with significant skin in the game to
fall back on in the event of default. A crucial operational insight lies in the distinction
between an entity as a borrower versus an entity used for vesting purposes. If an
individual is the primary borrower on the 1003 form and simply wishes to place the title
into an LLC or other entity upon closing, a separate personal guarantee from that
individual is not required 9 . The guarantee is triggered only when the entity itself is
named as the borrower on the loan application. This subtle but vital distinction can
significantly streamline the approval process for owner-occupant loans where the
borrower intends to hold the property in a trust or LLC for asset protection purposes.
The framework also provides clear guidance on layered entities. Layered LLCs are
permitted, but with a strict cap of two layers 25. For example, a borrower could have an
LLC (LLC-A) that owns a membership interest in another LLC (LLC-B), which then owns
the property. However, the practice of layering a trust over an LLC is explicitly ineligible
25. This rule likely stems from concerns over opacity and the potential for asset
concealment, aiming to maintain a clear and traceable chain of ownership. For Non-QM
programs, the eligible entities are primarily LLCs, Corporations, Partnerships, and Inter-
Vivos Revocable Trusts 25. Understanding these structural limitations is paramount;
attempting to submit a loan with a three-layer LLC or a trust-owned LLC will result in an
immediate suspension.

The program also extends special provisions for <strong>First-Time Homebuyers (FTHBs)</strong>, though the rules differ between owner-occupied and investor products. allows them to purchase an investment property using the DSCR program. This requires
the submission of a rent-free letter to verify housing history 9 . This blurs the traditional
lines between owner-occupant and investment lending, creating a fascinating arbitrage
opportunity.

This is a stark departure from traditional lending and a major advantage for investors

Collateral Risk Mitigation: Advanced Valuation and
Property-Type Overlays
Effective collateral risk mitigation is the bedrock of sound mortgage underwriting, and
Cake Mortgage's 2026 framework employs a multi-layered strategy combining rigorous
appraisal processes, stringent valuation reviews, and highly specific property-type
overlays. This system is designed to ensure that every dollar lent is adequately secured by
the property's value, while also accommodating a diverse range of collateral that falls
outside conventional agency guidelines. The depth of these rules reveals a sophisticated
understanding of market dynamics, from the volatility of the short-term rental market to
the unique risks associated with non-warrantable condominiums and multifamily
properties. A thorough comprehension of these collateral controls is essential for loan
officers and processors to navigate the complexities of appraisal review, identify potential
red flags, and successfully underwrite loans on non-traditional properties.
The cornerstone of the collateral risk framework is the appraisal process, which has been
enhanced with a formalized "appraisal cascading" procedure, particularly for DSCR loans
9 . Every appraisal submitted for a DSCR loan must first undergo a review by a
Collateral Underwriter (CU) or Loan Collateral Analyst (LCA). If the initial CU/LCA score
is greater than 2.5, the file is automatically triggered for a mandatory cascade of
additional reviews 9 . This cascade proceeds sequentially: first, an Automated Valuation
Model (AVM) check is performed. If the AVM value is more than 10% below the
appraised value, a field review or a second appraisal becomes mandatory 9 . If the AVM
check passes, the file moves to an Enhanced Desk Review (conducted by a CU Analyst or
Appraisal Review Analyst), followed by a potential Field Review, and finally, a Second
Appraisal, which is required for all loans of $2,000,000 or more 9 . This systematic
escalation ensures that any potential undervaluation or irregularity in the initial appraisal
is caught and investigated before underwriting decisions are made, protecting both the

===== PAGE 11 =====

investor and the borrower from over-leveraging the collateral. An important procedural
detail is that transferred appraisals are subject to their own set of rules; they must be less
than 60 days old at the time of transfer, and a 1004D recertification cannot be performed
on a transferred appraisal 9 .
The framework also contains specific rules for "flip" transactions, which are designed to
curb speculative activity. A flip is defined as a property that is resold within 180 days of
its acquisition date and sold for a price that is more than 10% higher than the original
purchase price 9 . Such transactions require an arm's-length marketing period and may
necessitate a second appraisal to confirm the value is supported by legitimate market
activity rather than artificial inflation 9 . This rule is a direct response to market trends
where rapid flipping can distort values and increase risk for lenders.
Property-type overlays represent another critical layer of collateral control. The most
prominent example is the divergent treatment of Short-Term Rentals (STRs). While STR
income is a core component of the DSCR v4.0 program, it is explicitly ineligible for
qualification in all 2026 Non-QM owner-occupied matrices, including Bundt Cake and
Cup Cake 22 32. This is a deliberate risk mitigation strategy. The reasoning is that owner-
occupant programs rely on stable, long-term income streams, whereas STR income is
inherently volatile and dependent on tourism, seasonality, and online platform
algorithms. By forcing borrowers with STR properties into the DSCR program, Cake
Mortgage applies a more conservative underwriting model that incorporates factors like
vacancy rates and potentially lower average daily rates, providing a more reliable
assessment of the property's performance. For DSCR STR purchases, the use of an
AirDNA Rentalizer report is governed by strict rules: it must be dated within 90 days of
the note date, feature a 12-month forecast, have a market score of at least 60, and cap
occupancy at two individuals per bedroom 9 .
Non-Warrantable Condominiums (NWCs) are another area of focused oversight. The
guidelines recognize the inherent risks in these projects and apply specific overlays.
Investor concentration can be as high as 100%, and commercial space can occupy up to
50% of the project, offering flexibility not found in conventional lending 1 31. However,
there are critical restrictions. The master insurance policy's deductible cannot exceed 10%
of the policy's face amount, unless the Homeowners Association (HOA) can demonstrate
exceptionally strong financial health and adequate reserves to absorb a higher deductible
1 . Furthermore, a single entity cannot own more than 50% of the units in the project
1 . Perhaps most importantly, for NWC projects, the use of 1099 or Profit & Loss (P&L)
income is often restricted and typically requires a full-doc income stream to be used for
qualification 22.

Multifamily properties (5-9 units) are governed by their own distinct set of rules,
reflecting their complexity. As previously mentioned, a minimum DSCR of 1.00 is
required, and loans of $2,000,000 or more must pass a Debt Yield test of at least 9% 9 .
A Commercial Exterior Only Broker Price Opinion (BPO) is required for these properties
unless two full appraisals are provided 1 . If a BPO is used and its value is more than
10% below the appraisal value, the BPO's value must be used for all LTV calculations,
prioritizing the more conservative estimate 1 . The classification of the property is also
critical; if a county assessor or appraiser classifies a property with an Accessory Dwelling
Unit (ADU) as a 5+ unit property, it is automatically forced into the stricter multifamily
guidelines, regardless of the borrower's intent 1 . This prevents borrowers from
circumventing the more stringent multifamily requirements by treating an ADU as a
separate building.
Finally, the framework addresses specific collateral enhancements like solar panels. PACE
loans, which are used to finance energy-efficient improvements, are strictly ineligible for
any Cake Mortgage product 1 . If a property has solar panels financed by a subordinated
loan, that payment must be factored into the DSCR ratio, increasing the borrower's debt
load 1 . Velvet Cake has an additional overlay requiring six months of seasoning after
the solar loan is paid according to the agreed-upon terms 1 . These detailed rules
demonstrate a commitment to managing every potential source of collateral risk, from
speculative flips to the long-term maintenance and financing of the property itself.
Operational Guardrails and Compliance: Navigating
Critical Deal-Killing Rules
While product features and underwriting guidelines define the "what" and "how" of a
loan, adherence to a strict set of operational guardrails determines whether a deal ever
reaches the closing table. These rules, often buried in the fine print of the manuals, act as
non-negotiable checkpoints that prevent common pitfalls, mitigate fraud risk, and ensure
compliance with a patchwork of regulatory requirements. For wholesale partners, a
failure to observe these operational nuances can lead to immediate suspensions, costly
delays, and damaged relationships with the clearing desk. This section illuminates the
most critical operational rules within the 2026 Cake Mortgage framework, focusing on
the areas most likely to cause issues: repairs and escrow holdbacks, the use of Powers of
Attorney, title holding periods, and miscellaneous compliance items. Mastering these
operational guardrails is as crucial as understanding the product architecture itself.

Perhaps the most frequently cited and misunderstood operational rule in the DSCR v4.0
manual is the strict prohibition of escrow holdbacks 9 . This rule mandates that any
repairs required by the appraiser must be completed and inspected by a qualified third
party prior to the loan's funding date. Brokers and sellers cannot withhold a portion of
the loan proceeds to fund these repairs later. This places a significant burden on the pre-
underwriting and processing phases, requiring meticulous coordination between the
broker, seller, buyer, contractors, and inspectors to ensure all work is finished and
certified before the loan can lock. Failure to comply with this rule is an automatic deal
killer and will result in a suspension. This policy is a robust anti-fraud measure,
preventing sellers from inflating repair costs or failing to complete necessary work once
the loan is funded.
The use of a Power of Attorney (POA) is another area fraught with specific restrictions. A
POA is generally prohibited in several key scenarios: for all transactions involving
Foreign Nationals, on all cash-out refinances, and whenever the property is being vested
into an LLC or other entity 21. This restriction adds a layer of complexity to international
deals and entity-based lending, as it necessitates alternative closing strategies. For
Foreign National transactions, the borrower may need to be physically present for
closing, or the use of a consular officer for notarization may be required. For entity
vesting, since a POA cannot be used, the individual grantor must appear in person or
follow a very specific notarization process to execute the deed transferring title to the
newly formed LLC. These restrictions are designed to prevent unauthorized individuals
from making binding financial decisions and to ensure that all parties with an ownership
stake are directly involved in the transaction.
Title holding periods are another critical compliance checkpoint, particularly for DSCR
cash-out refinances. The standard requirement is a 90-day title holding period, meaning
the borrower must have owned the property free and clear for at least 90 days prior to
applying for the refinance 9 . This rule is designed to prevent rapid turnover and
potential fraudulent schemes where a property is flipped shortly after purchase. However,
the guidelines do acknowledge exceptions to this rule. Transfers resulting from
inheritance, divorce settlement, or legal awards are acceptable and do not require the 90-
day seasoning period 9 . It is incumbent upon the underwriter to scrutinize the title
commitment and request appropriate legal documentation (such as a death certificate,
divorce decree, or court order) to support any exception claimed by the borrower.
Without proper documentation, the 90-day rule stands as an absolute barrier.
Beyond these major rules, the 2026 framework contains a host of other critical
compliance items. For example, the guidelines for declining markets and disaster areas
impose automatic LTV/CLTV reductions of 5% to account for potential market instability

or physical damage 22. All title commitments must be ALTA or equivalent and dated
within 120 days of the note date (or 90 days for Texas transactions) 9 . Prepayment
penalties are offered as an option, with terms ranging from one to five years, providing
flexibility for borrowers who may pay off their loan early 9 .

Secondary Market Strategy: Decoding the Paradigm
Shift from 2025 to 2026
The evolution of Cake Mortgage's guidelines from the restrictive 2025 baselines to the
highly flexible 2026 framework is not merely an incremental update; it represents a
profound strategic bet on the appetite and capacity of the secondary mortgage market. By
archiving the older, more conservative matrices and implementing sweeping changes in
loan amounts, LTVs, and, most critically, credit event seasoning, Cake has positioned
itself at the forefront of the Non-QM movement. This final analysis deciphers the strategic
implications of this shift, connecting the dots between the granular underwriting changes
and the broader market forces shaping the future of residential mortgage-backed
securities (RMBS). Understanding this "why" behind the "what" is crucial for executives
and capital markets teams to anticipate future trends, manage risk, and optimize their
bulk loan purchase strategies.
The single most telling indicator of this strategic shift is the radical change in credit event
seasoning rules. The 2025 Funnel Cake matrix demanded a staggering 48-month waiting
period for Bankruptcies and Foreclosures before a borrower could be considered for a
loan 22. This rule was a relic of a more cautious lending era, reflecting a low tolerance
for risk and a belief that such events permanently tainted a borrower's creditworthiness.
The 2026 Bundt Cake guidelines obliterate this rule, allowing consideration for a
borrower with a bankruptcy or foreclosure just 12 months after the discharge, albeit with
a significant LTV penalty 22. This move is a direct challenge to competitors and a clear
signal to the market. It suggests that Cake Mortgage's secondary market partners have
expressed a strong demand for this type of paper. The market has evidently matured to
the point where investors are willing to purchase RMBS tranches backed by loans
originated to borrowers with very recent, though overcome, financial distress, provided
the loans are originated with lower loan-to-value ratios to insulate the investor from
potential losses. The success of the Barclays Mortgage Loan Trust 2026-NQM2, an RMBS
transaction backed by property-focused investor loans, validates this trend 18 31. Cake's
aggressive stance is a calculated risk that pays off by capturing a market segment that
others have abandoned, turning a historical weakness into a competitive strength.
This philosophy extends to the overall expansion of Loan-to-Value (LTV) caps. The 2025
Sponge Cake matrix imposed highly restrictive LTVs, often capping investment and cash-
out loans at a mere 50-55% 22. In contrast, the 2026 framework pushes these limits
dramatically higher. Bundt Cake and Cup Cake now offer up to 85% CLTV for investment
property purchases (for borrowers with a 720+ FICO) and up to 90% LTV for primary
residence purchases (700+ FICO for Cup, 720+ for Bundt) 22 28. This aggressive

===== PAGE 16 =====

expansion of LTVs is a strategic decision rooted in market demand. Many borrowers
today have substantial equity built up in their primary homes or are investors with deep
pockets, and they expect lenders to offer high-leverage financing. By pushing the LTV
envelope, Cake Mortgage is able to attract these high-balance, high-equity clients who
might otherwise turn to private money or other forms of non-conventional financing. The
risk of originating these higher-LTV loans is managed through the combination of stricter
credit overlays (like the 12-month bankruptcy rule), higher minimum FICO score
requirements for high-LTV loans, and increased reserve requirements for loans above
certain thresholds (e.g., Coffee Cake requires 9 months of reserves for loans over $1.5M)
26. This strategy effectively shifts the risk from a purely credit-based assessment to a
more holistic evaluation that balances credit history with loan-level risk mitigation like
LTV and reserves.
Furthermore, the 2026 framework demonstrates a more sophisticated approach to
product segregation, particularly concerning income verification. The clear separation of
Wages Verified Over the Phone (WVOE) borrowers into dedicated programs like Pound
Cake Lite and Coffee Cake, while simultaneously banning WVOE on Non-QM investment
(NOO) products, reflects a maturing understanding of risk 7 22. WVOE, while faster, is
considered a less reliable form of income verification than bank statements or profit and
loss statements, which offer a view into actual cash flow. By creating a specific niche for
WVOE borrowers and placing it in a program with lower loan limits and stricter
requirements, Cake acknowledges this differential risk. At the same time, by banning
WVOE for investment properties, the company is applying its most conservative
underwriting lens to its riskiest asset class, where the borrower's personal income is less
relevant and the property's performance is paramount. This level of granularity allows for
more precise risk modeling and pricing, which is exactly what sophisticated secondary
market investors require.
In synthesizing these points, the strategic narrative of Cake Mortgage's 2026 framework
becomes clear. It is a deliberate and confident move away from the cautious, restrictive
lending models of the past toward a more inclusive, high-volume, and data-driven
approach. The company has assessed the secondary market and concluded that there is a
robust appetite for Non-QM paper, even when it involves borrowers with recent credit
challenges or properties in non-traditional categories. Their strategy is to meet this
demand head-on by offering the most flexible, borrower-friendly, and product-diverse
platform in the market. For executives and brokers, this means that the competitive
landscape has fundamentally changed. The key to success is not just knowing the rules,
but understanding the strategy behind them. The ability to sell a loan to a borrower with
a 14-month-old bankruptcy because Cake Mortgage can approve it while others cannot is

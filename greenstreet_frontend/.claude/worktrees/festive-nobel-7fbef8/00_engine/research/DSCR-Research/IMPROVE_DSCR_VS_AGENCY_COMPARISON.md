## 1. DSCR vs Fannie Mae / Conventional Investment Property

### Side-by-Side Comparison

| Feature | DSCR Loan | Conventional (Fannie Mae) |
|---|---|---|
| **Rate (2025-2026)** | 6.25% – 9.00% | 6.00% – 7.25% |
| **Rate Premium** | +0.75% to +2.0% over conventional | Baseline |
| **Max LTV (Purchase)** | 75% – 80% | 75% – 85% (1-unit) |
| **Max LTV (Cash-Out)** | 70% – 75% | 70% – 75% |
| **Qualification Metric** | DSCR ≥ 1.20 (property cash flow) | DTI ≤ 45% (personal income) |
| **Income Documentation** | None required (property-based) | 2 years tax returns, W-2s, pay stubs |
| **Min FICO** | 620 (best terms 720+) | 620 (best terms 740+) |
| **Property Count Limit** | Unlimited | 10 financed properties max |
| **Closing Timeline** | 2 – 4 weeks | 4 – 8 weeks |
| **LLC Closing** | Yes, standard | No, personal name only |
| **Prepayment Penalty** | Common (3-2-1 or 5-4-3-2-1) | None |
| **Loan Type** | Non-QM | GSE-backed (QM) |
| **Assumability** | No | No |
| **Reserves** | 0 – 6 months PITI | 2 – 12 months PITI |

### Rate Difference: How Much More Expensive Is DSCR?

Based on 2025-2026 market data aggregated from multiple sources:

- **Typical spread:** DSCR rates run **0.75% – 2.0% above** comparable conventional investment property rates
- **At the tight end:** A borrower with 740+ FICO, 1.40+ DSCR, 70% LTV may see only ~0.75% premium
- **At the wide end:** A borrower with 660 FICO, 1.0-1.10 DSCR, 80% LTV may see a 1.5-2.0% premium
- **Average premium:** ~1.0 – 1.25% is the most commonly cited spread

**Per $100K borrowed, each 1% rate difference costs approximately:**
- $66/month extra on a 30-year fixed
- $792/year extra
- $3,960 over 5 years

### When DSCR Is Better Than Conventional

| Scenario | Why DSCR Wins |
|---|---|
| Self-employed with heavy tax write-offs | No personal income verification; W-2/tax returns not needed |
| Rapidly scaling portfolio (5+ properties) | No 10-property financed cap; DTI doesn't accumulate |
| Need to close fast (competitive deal) | 2-3 week close vs 45-60 days conventional |
| Buying in LLC for asset protection | DSCR allows LLC closing; conventional requires personal name |
| Foreign national investor | Conventional requires US income history; DSCR doesn't |
| Non-warrantable condo / condotel | Most conventional lenders won't touch these; DSCR will |
| Complex income (K-1s, multiple businesses) | DSCR ignores personal income complexity entirely |

### When Conventional Is Better Than DSCR

| Scenario | Why Conventional Wins |
|---|---|
| W-2 borrower with clean income | Lower rate saves thousands over loan life |
| Buying 1st-3rd investment property | No need for DSCR flexibility yet; property count not an issue |
| Long-term hold (10+ years) | Rate savings compound; no prepay penalty |
| Rate is #1 priority | 0.75-2.0% lower rate = significant savings |
| Primary residence or house hack | DSCR is for investment properties only |
| Want GSE-backed loan security | Fannie/Freddie securitization = standardized servicing |

## 2. DSCR vs Hard Money

### Side-by-Side Comparison

| Feature | DSCR Loan | Hard Money Loan |
|---|---|---|
| **Rate** | 6.0% – 10.0% | 8.0% – 15.0% |
| **Term** | 15 – 30 years | 6 – 24 months |
| **Amortization** | 30-year fixed or ARM | Interest-only (usually) |
| **Max LTV** | 75% – 80% | 65% – 75% (after-repair value) |
| **Qualification** | DSCR ≥ 1.0 – 1.25 | Based on deal / ARV |
| **Income Docs** | None | None |
| **Prepayment Penalty** | 3-2-1 or 5-4-3-2-1 | Often 3-6 months interest |
| **Closing Speed** | 2 – 4 weeks | 1 – 2 weeks |
| **Property Condition** | Must be rent-ready | Can finance distressed/rehab |
| **Best For** | Stabilized rental properties | Fix-and-flip, value-add rehab |
| **Exit Strategy** | Hold long-term | Sell or refinance quickly |
| **Points/Origination** | 1 – 3 points | 2 – 5 points |

### Rate Comparison: The Gap Is Massive

| Loan Amount | DSCR @ 7.5% (30yr) | Hard Money @ 12% (IO) | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| $250,000 | $1,748/mo | $2,500/mo | $752/mo ($9,024/yr) |
| $350,000 | $2,447/mo | $3,500/mo | $1,053/mo ($12,636/yr) |
| $500,000 | $3,496/mo | $5,000/mo | $1,504/mo ($18,048/yr) |

> **Key Insight:** On a $350K loan, hard money costs **$12,636/year MORE** than DSCR. Over a 12-month hard money term, that's $12,636 in excess interest — before you even refinance.

### When to Use Hard Money vs DSCR

**Use Hard Money When:**
- Property needs significant rehab (not rent-ready)
- You're doing a fix-and-flip (not a hold)
- You need to close in under 2 weeks
- The deal's profitability depends on adding value, not holding
- Property won't appraise for DSCR without repairs

**Use DSCR When:**
- Property is stabilized and rent-ready
- You plan to hold long-term (3+ years)
- Rental income covers debt service (DSCR ≥ 1.0)
- You want a permanent loan, not a bridge

### The Bridge-to-DSCR Strategy

This is the most common hybrid approach for value-add deals:

```
Step 1: Acquire with Hard Money
  - Close in 7-10 days
  - Finance purchase + rehab costs
  - Interest-only payments during renovation

Step 2: Rehab & Stabilize (3-6 months)
  - Complete renovations
  - Place tenant(s)
  - Establish rental income history

Step 3: Refinance into DSCR (the "cash-out refi")
  - DSCR lender uses new, higher appraised value
  - Rental income now supports DSCR qualification
  - 30-year term replaces short-term hard money
  - Rate drops from 10-12% to 7-9%
  - Often cash out additional equity from forced appreciation
```

**Worked Example:**
- Purchase price: $250,000 (distressed property)
- Rehab cost: $50,000
- Hard money loan: $225,000 @ 11%, 12-month term, IO = $2,063/mo
- After rehab, appraised value: $350,000
- DSCR refi at 75% LTV = $262,500 @ 7.75%, 30yr = $1,873/mo
- Cash-out after paying off hard money: $262,500 - $225,000 = $37,500
- Payment drops from $2,063/mo to $1,873/mo AND you extract $37,500

## 3. DSCR vs Commercial Mortgage (5+ Units)

### Side-by-Side Comparison

| Feature | DSCR Loan | Commercial Mortgage |
|---|---|---|
| **Eligible Properties** | 1-10 unit residential, mixed-use | 5+ units, commercial, mixed-use |
| **Rate** | 6.5% – 9.5% | 6.5% – 9.0% (bank); 7.5% – 10%+ (non-bank) |
| **Term** | 30-year fixed available | 5, 7, 10-year terms common (amortized 25-30yr) |
| **Amortization** | 30 years | 25 – 30 years |
| **Max LTV** | 75% – 80% | 70% – 80% |
| **Balloon Payment** | No | Yes (at term end, typically 5-10yr) |
| **Income Qualification** | Property DSCR only | Property DSCR + personal guarantees |
| **Personal Guarantee** | None (non-recourse available) | Full recourse typical |
| **Documentation** | Minimal (lease, appraisal) | Extensive (property + borrower financials) |
| **Closing Timeline** | 3 – 4 weeks | 4 – 8 weeks (bank); 6-12 weeks (CMBS) |
| **Reserve Requirements** | 0 – 6 months | 6 – 12 months common |
| **Prepayment Penalty** | Step-down (3-2-1 typical) | Yield maintenance or defeasance (severe) |
| **Loan Size** | $75K – $3M typical | $500K – $50M+ |
| **Debt Yields** | Not typical | 8% – 10% common |

### For 5+ Unit Properties: When Is DSCR Better?

**DSCR Advantages for Small Multifamily (5-10 units):**

1. **No personal income verification** — Commercial lenders require full personal financial statements, tax returns, and personal guarantees. DSCR qualifies on property cash flow alone.

2. **30-year fixed rate, no balloon** — Commercial mortgages typically have 5-10 year terms with balloons. DSCR offers true 30-year fixed, eliminating refinance risk.

3. **Faster closing** — DSCR closes in 3-4 weeks; commercial loans take 45-90 days, especially if CMBS or agency (Fannie/Freddie multifamily).

4. **No yield maintenance / defeasance** — Commercial prepayment penalties are severe. DSCR uses simple step-down (3-2-1), which is far less punitive.

5. **Non-recourse standard** — DSCR loans are typically non-recourse. Commercial loans often require full personal guarantees.

**Commercial Mortgage Advantages:**

1. **Potentially lower rates for large loans** — For $2M+ loans on 20+ unit properties, bank/CMBS rates can be 25-50 bps lower than DSCR.

2. **Agency multifamily (Fannie/Freddie)** — For stabilized 5+ unit properties with strong occupancy, agency multifamily loans offer the lowest rates in the market (5.5% – 7.0%).

3. **Interest-only periods** — Commercial lenders often offer 1-3 year IO periods at the start, improving initial cash flow.

4. **Higher loan amounts** — DSCR typically caps at $3M; commercial goes to $50M+.

5. **Recourse can lower rate** — If you're willing to personally guarantee, commercial rates drop further.

> — BiggerPockets Forum, 2025

### When Does the Higher DSCR Rate "Cost" vs the Higher LTV Benefit?

**The Trade-Off:**

The DSCR 80% LTV scenario lets you put down **$17,500 less** ($70K vs $87.5K), which is capital you can deploy elsewhere. The question is: does deploying that $17,500 elsewhere earn more than the ~$21,842 premium you pay over 5 years?

**Break-even deployment rate:**
- $21,842 premium ÷ $17,500 freed capital ÷ 5 years = **24.9% annual return needed**

That's very high. In most cases, the lower-down-payment benefit does NOT offset the rate premium purely on math. **DSCR wins on flexibility and speed, not on cost.**

**However**, if the alternative is NO deal at all (because you can't qualify conventionally), then DSCR's "cost" is actually zero — it's the only way to capture the deal's returns.

### Documentation & Timeline Deep Dive

**DSCR Loan — What You Actually Need:**
1. Purchase contract / refinance application
2. Lease agreements (or rent roll)
3. Appraisal (ordered by lender)
4. Property inspection report
5. Entity documents (if closing in LLC)
6. Personal credit report (soft pull for quote, hard pull for approval)

1. All of the above, PLUS:

10. P&L statement (if self-employed)
11. Explanation letters for any credit inquiries, gaps, etc.

## 6. When DSCR Is the ONLY Option

### Scenario 1: Self-Employed with Complex Tax Returns

**The Problem:**
- Self-employed borrowers write off business expenses to minimize tax liability
- This artificially lowers AGI (Adjusted Gross Income) on tax returns
- Conventional lenders use AGI for DTI qualification
- Result: Despite strong cash flow, the borrower "can't afford" a conventional loan

**The DSCR Solution:**
- DSCR lenders don't look at tax returns at all
- Qualification is based entirely on property cash flow
- The same borrower who "makes" $40K on tax returns but actually earns $150K can qualify easily

**How Common Is This?** Extremely. Per Truss Financial Group: "DSCR loans are especially valuable for investors with multiple properties, borrowers using LLCs or business entities, and those who write off a lot of income on taxes."

### Scenario 2: Foreign Nationals

**The Problem:**
- Conventional loans require US Social Security Number, US credit history, US income documentation
- Foreign nationals investing in US real estate typically have none of these

**The DSCR Solution:**
- DSCR lenders offer foreign national programs
- ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number) accepted instead of SSN
- No US credit history required (some lenders use international credit or alternative scoring)
- Property cash flow is the universal language — it works regardless of nationality

**Typical Terms for Foreign Nationals:**
- Rate premium: +0.50% – 1.00% over domestic DSCR rates
- LTV: typically 70-75% (vs 75-80% for domestic)
- Minimum down payment: 25-30%
- FICO minimum: 660 or alternative credit

### Scenario 3: Exceeding the 10 Financed Property Cap

**The Problem:**
- Fannie Mae limits borrowers to 10 financed properties (primary + second home + investment)
- Once you hit the cap, no more conventional investment property loans
- This affects the most active and successful investors — exactly the people who need financing most

**The DSCR Solution:**
- No property count limit
- Each property underwritten independently on its DSCR
- Portfolio can grow to 20, 50, 100+ properties

### Scenario 4: Non-Warrantable Condos & Condotels

**The Problem:**
- Conventional lenders require condos to be "warrantable" — meeting Fannie Mae eligibility criteria
- Non-warrantable condos include: high investor concentration (>50%), hotel-condo hybrids, single-entity ownership, insufficient reserves, pending litigation
- Condotels (condo-hotel hybrids) are almost universally excluded by conventional lenders

**The DSCR Solution:**
- DSCR lenders finance non-warrantable condos and condotels
- Property cash flow is the underwriting criterion, not project warrantability
- Some DSCR lenders charge a small premium (+0.25-0.50%) for non-warrantable

### Scenario 5: Privacy — No Personal Income Disclosure

**The Problem:**
- High-net-worth individuals, celebrities, and business owners may not want to disclose personal financial information
- Conventional loans require full financial disclosure to lenders

**The DSCR Solution:**
- Close in an LLC or business entity
- No personal income documentation shared
- Only property financials are disclosed
- Credit check is the only personal data required

### Scenario 6: Bankruptcy / Foreclosure in Recent History

**The Problem:**
- Conventional: 4-year waiting period after bankruptcy discharge, 7 years after foreclosure
- FHA: 2 years after Ch. 7 bankruptcy, 3 years after foreclosure

**The DSCR Solution:**
- Some DSCR lenders approve with bankruptcy/foreclosure as recent as 1-2 years
- Focus is on property performance, not personal credit history
- Rate premium applies but financing IS available

## 7. Cash-Out Refinance Comparison

### Side-by-Side: DSCR Cash-Out vs Conventional Cash-Out

| Feature | DSCR Cash-Out | Conventional Cash-Out |
|---|---|---|
| **Max LTV** | 70% – 75% | 70% – 75% |
| **Seasoning Period** | 6 months (some lenders: 0 days) | 12 months (Fannie Mae standard) |
| **Qualification** | Property DSCR ≥ 1.20 | Personal DTI ≤ 45% |
| **Income Docs Required** | None | Full documentation |
| **Appraisal** | Required | Required |
| **Closing Speed** | 2 – 4 weeks | 4 – 8 weeks |
| **LLC Eligible** | Yes | No |
| **Prepayment Penalty** | Common (step-down) | None |
| **Cash Received** | Tax-free (loan proceeds) | Tax-free (loan proceeds) |

### Seasoning: The Critical Difference

**Conventional (Fannie Mae):**
- Must wait 12 months from purchase to do cash-out refi
- If using delayed financing, must document original purchase as cash
- Some exceptions for inherited properties

**DSCR:**
- Many lenders allow cash-out after just 6 months of ownership
- Some lenders have NO seasoning requirement (purchase + immediate refi)
- "Delayed financing" programs allow cash-out within days of purchase

### Worked Example: Cash-Out Refinance on a Property

**Scenario:** You bought a property 8 months ago for $300,000. It's now worth $380,000 after renovations and market appreciation. Rent is $2,800/month.

| Metric | DSCR Cash-Out | Conventional Cash-Out |
|---|---|---|
| Current Value | $380,000 | $380,000 |
| Max LTV | 75% = $285,000 | 75% = $285,000 |
| Existing Loan Balance | $225,000 | $225,000 |
| **Cash Available** | **$60,000** | **Not eligible** (8 months < 12 months) |
| Rate | 7.75% | 6.75% |
| Monthly P&I (new loan) | $2,036 | $1,839 |
| DSCR Check | $2,800/$2,336 = 1.20 ✅ | DTI-dependent |

**Key Takeaway:** DSCR lets you access $60,000 in equity **4 months sooner** than conventional. If you need that capital for your next deal, the speed advantage is worth more than the rate difference.

### Seasoning Comparison by Lender Type

| Lender / Program | Seasoning Required |
|---|---|
| Fannie Mae Conventional | 12 months |
| DSCR — Standard | 6 months |
| DSCR — Delayed Financing | 0 days (immediate) |
| Hard Money → Refi | Varies (some DSCR lenders accept day-1 refi from HM) |

| 2025 (Current) | 6.00% – 7.25% | 6.25% – 9.00% | 0.75% – 2.00% |
| 2026 (Early) | 6.00% – 7.00% | 6.12% – 8.50% | 0.75% – 1.75% |

### Is the Spread Consistent?

**No.** The spread between DSCR and conventional rates is NOT consistent. It varies based on:

1. **MBS Market Conditions:** When agency MBS demand is strong, conventional rates compress while DSCR (non-QM) rates stay elevated. This WIDENS the spread.

2. **Non-QM Securitization Demand:** When institutional investors are buying DSCR loan pools, DSCR rates compress. This NARROWS the spread. In 2025, strong securitization demand helped narrow the spread to ~1.0%.

3. **Credit Market Stress:** During periods of credit stress (2022-2023 rate spike), non-QM lending pulls back first, widening the spread to 2.0%+.

4. **Competition Among DSCR Lenders:** More DSCR lenders entering the market compresses rates. The 2024-2026 period has seen significant DSCR lender competition, narrowing the spread.

### What Drives the Spread?

| Driver | Effect on Spread | Current Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Strong agency MBS demand | Widens (conventional drops faster) | Moderate |
| Strong non-QM securitization | Narrows (DSCR drops) | Currently narrowing |
| Rising interest rates | Initially widens, then stabilizes | Rates stabilizing |
| DSCR lender competition | Narrows | Intensifying |
| Credit market stress | Widens significantly | Currently low stress |
| DSCR product innovation | Narrows | Ongoing |

### Current Spread (2025-2026)

- **Typical spread:** 0.75% – 2.00% over conventional investment property rates
- **Average spread:** ~1.0% – 1.25%
- **Tightest spread (strong DSCR, low LTV, high FICO):** ~0.75%
- **Widest spread (weak DSCR, high LTV, lower FICO):** ~2.0%

### Rate Forecast Considerations

- If the Fed cuts rates, both conventional and DSCR rates drop, but the spread may temporarily widen as non-QM markets adjust more slowly
- If non-QM securitization volume continues to grow, DSCR rates will compress further relative to conventional
- Increased DSCR lender competition is a structural force pushing the spread toward 0.75-1.0%

### The "Cost of NOT Getting the Deal" Framework

Many investors focus exclusively on rate comparison. But the real question is:

**"What does it cost you to miss a deal because you couldn't qualify or close in time?"**

| Scenario | Conventional | DSCR | Deal Value |
|---|---|---|---|

> **Bottom Line:** A 1% rate premium on a $280K loan costs ~$2,800/year. Missing a $6,000/year cash flow deal costs $6,000/year forever. **The "premium" you pay for DSCR flexibility is often the cheapest money you'll ever spend.**

## DIMENSION 1: MARKET LANDSCAPE — Size, Growth, and Composition

### Market Size and Trajectory

The DSCR loan market has undergone a structural transformation from niche product to mainstream investment vehicle. The numbers tell the story clearly:

**Volume Growth:**
- DSCR loan volume grew **52% year-over-year in 2024**, with even stronger acceleration in Q3 and Q4 (92% YoY growth in Q4 2024) [AAPlone/Lightning Docs, April 2025]
- In January 2025 alone, the market saw **over 4,272 DSCR loan transactions exceeding $2 billion** [RCN Capital, August 2025]
- The broader private lending space reached **$2 trillion in 2025**, up from $1.75 trillion in 2024 — a 14% expansion driven by institutional capital seeking yield [Norada Real Estate, December 2025]
- The top 100 private lenders in the U.S. increased total loan volumes by **25.3% in 2024**, adding approximately 27,000 transactions year-over-year to reach more than 133,000 total loan transactions [AAPlone/Forecasa, April 2025]

**Non-QM Market Context:**
- Non-QM loans as a share of all U.S. mortgages grew from **5.21% in July 2024 to 8.0% in July 2025** — a 53% relative increase in 12 months [National Mortgage Professional, August 2025]
- Investor/DSCR loans now account for **28.7% of all non-QM volume**, up 2.79 percentage points year-over-year [National Mortgage Professional, August 2025]
- DSCR loans represent **30% of total 2025 non-QM securitization activity** [HousingWire via Lendmire, May 2026]
- Non-QM market projected to grow another **20% by 2026** [Norada Real Estate, December 2025]

### Borrower Composition

The investor base is structurally distinct from the traditional mortgage market:

- **Real estate investors account for 30%** of all single-family home purchases for full-year 2025, up from 29% in 2024 [Cotality Q4 2025 Home Investor Report]
- **33–34% investor share** of single-family purchases in Q2 and Q3 2025 — the highest in five years [BatchData Investor Pulse Reports]
- **80,000–100,000 investor single-family home purchases per month** in late 2025 [Cotality Q4 2025 Home Investor Report]
- **87%** of investor-owned single-family rentals are held by investors owning **1–5 properties** (main street investors), while institutional investors owning 1,000+ properties hold only **2%** of stock [BatchData]
- **Institutional investors have been net sellers** of single-family homes for **6 consecutive quarters** as of early 2026, creating a structural gap filled by individual and small-portfolio investors [BatchData]
- **Over 10.5 million Americans** are self-employed [BLS data via Foundation Mortgage, December 2025], with broader gig-economy participation exceeding 70 million workers — the primary pool of borrowers who cannot qualify conventionally but can qualify via DSCR

**Key insight:** The DSCR market is driven by main-street investors (1–5 properties), self-employed professionals, and business owners — not institutional players. These borrowers are Repeatable, referrable, and portfolio-oriented. This is a relationship business, not a transaction business.

### Product Mix by Loan Type

Within the business-purpose lending segment (private lenders + non-QM lenders):
- **RTL (Rental Term Loans / long-term DSCR)** dominate by count of units
- **DSCR loans have steadily gained market share** since 2020, with growth coming primarily from non-QM lenders [AAPlone, March 2025]
- Average DSCR loan amount: approximately **$281,000–$315,000** nationally (January 2025 data) [Lightning Docs, April 2025]
- Average RTL loan amount: approximately **$521,000** (larger loans, more complex deals) [AAPlone, March 2025]
- DSCR loan amounts increased throughout 2024: national average rose from **$270,300 in January to $312,465 in December** [Lightning Docs, April 2025]

## DIMENSION 2: BORROWER PROFILES AND PERSONA FRAMEWORK

### Who Uses DSCR Loans — The Primary Segments

**Segment 1: Self-Employed Real Estate Investors**
The largest segment. These borrowers have complex tax returns that depress conventional qualifying income — they write off depreciation, business expenses, and losses. DSCR evaluates the property, not the tax return.

- **Profile:** 1099 contractors, LLC members, business owners, freelancers
- **Challenge:** Income shown on tax returns doesn't reflect actual cash flow
- **DSCR advantage:** Property rental income qualifies, not personal income
- **Typical credit:** 640–720 FICO
- **Down payment:** 20–25%
- **Loan size:** $150K–$500K
- **DSCR range:** 1.00–1.50 typically

**Segment 2: Portfolio-Building Investors**
Buyers who have already maxed out conventional financing (Fannie/Freddie caps at 10 financed properties) or who hold properties in LLCs.

- **Profile:** Active investors with 3–10+ properties, operating through entities
- **Challenge:** Personal income consumed by existing debt service; entity-held properties
- **DSCR advantage:** No loan count cap; each property qualifies on its own merits; LLC-acceptable
- **Typical credit:** 700–760 FICO
- **Down payment:** 20–25%
- **Loan size:** $250K–$750K
- **DSCR range:** 1.15–1.50

**Segment 3: High-Net-Worth/Complex Income Borrowers**
Borrowers with substantial assets but unconventional income — early retirees, business sale proceeds, foreign nationals, trust income.

- **Profile:** Liquidity-rich, income-variable; often using trust or entity structures
- **Challenge:** Low reported income but high net worth; conventional DTI fails
- **DSCR advantage:** Property income supports; asset depletion optional
- **Typical credit:** 720–780 FICO
- **Down payment:** 20–25%
- **Loan size:** $400K–$2M+
- **DSCR range:** 1.20–2.00

**Segment 4: Short-Term Rental (Airbnb/VRBO) Investors**
A rapidly growing sub-segment with unique underwriting considerations.

- **Profile:** Investors targeting vacation rental markets (Florida, California, Colorado, Arizona)
- **Challenge:** Income is seasonal, volatile, and not documentable via traditional means
- **DSCR advantage:** Projected STR income via AirDNA, Rabbu, or 1007 appraisal accepted
- **Typical credit:** 700+ FICO (STR programs often require 700+ vs. 660+ for LTR)
- **Down payment:** 20–25% (some programs as low as 15%)
- **Loan size:** $200K–$750K
- **DSCR range:** 1.00–1.25 (with 75% of projected STR income used for qualification)
- **Reserve requirement:** Often 12 months PITIA (higher than LTR due to income volatility)

**Segment 5: Foreign National Investors**
Buyers without U.S. credit history or Social Security Numbers, using ITINs.

- **Profile:** International investors purchasing U.S. rental properties
- **Challenge:** No U.S. credit file; no SSN; income verification complex
- **DSCR advantage:** Property-based qualification; foreign national programs available
- **Typical credit:** No U.S. credit score required (some lenders); ITIN acceptable
- **Down payment:** 25–35%
- **Loan size:** $150K–$1M
- **DSCR range:** 1.0–1.25
- **Key requirements:** 12 months reserves; max LTV 65–70%

### Borrower Goals and Decision Drivers

DSCR borrowers are business operators making investment decisions — they prioritize:

1. **Speed** — Competitive markets reward fast closers. A 15-day DSCR close wins deals that a 45-day conventional process loses.
2. **Scalability** — The ability to finance multiple properties without personal income constraints is the primary value proposition.
3. **Entity flexibility** — LLC, corporation, LP, and revocable trust borrowing is standard in DSCR (not available conventionally).
4. **Cash-out access** — Pulling equity from existing properties to fund the next acquisition is a primary motivation. 67% of Griffin Funding's DSCR originations in 2026 were cash-out refinances [Griffin Funding, June 2026].
5. **Exit flexibility** — Interest-only options and no prepayment penalty options (or buy-out options) matter for investors planning a 3–7 year hold.

### Product Variations

**Standard DSCR (Long-Term Rental):**
- DSCR ≥ 1.0 required (1.25 preferred for best pricing)
- 30-year fixed or 5/6–7/6 ARM
- Up to 80% LTV purchase; 75–80% cash-out
- 1–4 unit residential, condos, townhomes
- No income docs; lease agreements + 1007 appraisal
- LLC, corporation, LP, trust acceptable
- Prepayment penalties: typically 5-4-3-2-1, 4-3-2-1, or 3-2-1 (declining structures)

**DSCR with No-Ratio (Sub-1.0):**
- DSCR < 1.0 allowed with strong compensating factors
- Requires higher FICO (often 680+), larger down payment (25–35%), 12 months reserves
- Higher rate premium (typically 0.75–1.5% above standard)
- Used for high-appreciation markets (Austin, Miami, Denver) where cash flow is negative but equity growth justifies

**Hybrid STR Mortgage:**
- Dual-underwrite: conventional DTI + DSCR
- Accepts Airbnb, VRBO, Rabbu income data for qualification
- 75% of projected gross STR income used for DSCR calculation
- Only 15% down, up to 90% LTV (for qualifying "live-rent" scenarios)
- Markets: Florida, Arizona, California, Colorado
- [Truss Financial Group, December 2025]

**Foreign National DSCR:**
- No U.S. credit score required (some programs)
- Max LTV: 65–70%
- Reserves: 12 months PITIA
- ITIN acceptable
- Purchase, rate-term, and cash-out available

## DIMENSION 4: UNDERWRITING CRITERIA AND DSCR THRESHOLDS

### DSCR Ratio Tiers and Lender Requirements

**The Formula:** DSCR = Gross Monthly Rent ÷ Monthly PITIA (Principal + Interest + Taxes + Insurance + HOA)

**Tier Chart:**

| DSCR Range | Lender Response | Rate Implication | Down Payment | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **≥ 1.50** | Premium tier; strongest approval | Best rate; lowest spread | 20% | Ideal — lender sees significant safety margin |
| **1.25–1.49** | Standard strong; most common approval | Competitive rate | 20–25% | The "sweet spot" for best terms |
| **1.20–1.24** | Acceptable for stabilized properties | Standard rate | 20–25% | Common minimum threshold |
| **1.00–1.19** | Acceptable but rate premium applies | +0.5–1.0% above standard | 20–25% | Break-even zone; no margin for vacancy |
| **0.80–0.99** | Limited lender appetite; requires compensating factors | +1.0–2.0% premium | 25–35% | No-ratio territory; high scrutiny |
| **< 0.80** | Very selective; only select lenders | Highest tier | 30–35% | Only for high-equity or high-appreciation scenarios |

**Key underwriting notes:**
- Property management fees are NOT included in the DSCR calculation (unlike commercial DSCR). This is a critical distinction — lenders calculate PITIA only, not PITIA + management.
- Vacancy rates are applied via the 1007 appraisal (market rent, not actual lease), so vacant properties can still qualify with projected income.
- DSCR below 1.0 does NOT automatically disqualify — lenders like Griffin Funding (min 0.75), AD Mortgage (as low as 0 with no-ratio), and others accommodate sub-1.0 DSCR with compensating factors.

### Property Eligibility

| Property Type | Eligibility | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Single-family residence | ✓ Standard | Most common; best pricing |
| 2–4 unit multifamily | ✓ Standard | Slightly higher rate; 1025 appraisal |
| Condo/Townhouse | ✓ Standard | Must not be non-warrantable; HOA restrictions reviewed |
| 5–8 unit small multifamily | ✓ Some lenders | AD Mortgage, Lendmire, others; complex income calculation |

| Condo-hotel / co-op | ⚠ Limited | Non-warrantable; limited lender appetite |

| Factor | Typical Requirement | Best Terms |

## DIMENSION 5: PRICING AND ECONOMICS

### Current Rate Environment (Q1–Q2 2026)

**Average DSCR rates:** 6.0%–7.99% for residential investment properties [Ridge Street Capital, April 2026; Investment Property Loan Exchange, May 2026]

**Rate structure by borrower profile:**
- DSCR 1.25+, FICO 700+, 25% down, 30-year fixed → approximately **6.75%–7.50%** (most common profile midpoint: 7.00%–7.50%) [IPLE, May 2026]
- Premium borrower (DSCR 1.50+, FICO 740+, strong reserves) → **6.0%–6.75%**
- Standard borrower (DSCR 1.0–1.24, FICO 660–699) → **7.5%–8.5%**
- Sub-1.0 DSCR or STR properties → **8.5%–10.75%+**

**Rate premium over conventional:**
- DSCR loans typically run **1.00%–2.00% above** comparable conventional investment property rates [IPLE, May 2026]
- The spread has **narrowed** from 2023 peaks as non-QM competition increased
- Conventional 30-year fixed rates have stabilized near **6.09%** (Freddie Mac), making DSCR's premium approximately **150–225 basis points** [Host Financial, April 2026; Freddie Mac data]

**Rate drivers:**
- DSCR ratio (1.0 vs. 1.25 vs. 1.50+)
- Credit score (660 vs. 700 vs. 740+)
- LTV/Down payment (20% vs. 25–30%)
- Property type (SFR best; 2–4 unit slightly higher; STR highest)
- Loan term (30-year fixed vs. 40-year vs. ARM)
- Reserves adequacy
- Loan amount (jumbo DSCR may have rate adjustment)
- Lender efficiency (direct capital lenders like Ridge Street, HouseMax price better than broker-markup lenders)

**Secondary market effect:** In 2024, improved secondary market conditions allowed rates to fall faster than typical benchmarks would suggest. This competitive pressure continues to compress spreads in 2026 [AAPlone, April 2025].

## DIMENSION 7: COMPLIANCE FRAMEWORK

### Regulatory Landscape

DSCR loans occupy a unique compliance position: they are business-purpose loans (not consumer loans in the same sense as agency loans) but they remain subject to federal and state lending regulations.

**Key Regulations:**

**1. ECOA (Equal Credit Opportunity Act) — §1002.4(b)**
- Prohibits any oral or written statement in advertising that would discourage a reasonable person from making or pursuing an application on a prohibited basis (race, religion, sex, national origin, etc.)
- The CFPB and DOJ actively pursue ECOA violations related to advertising
- Fair lending examination procedures (FFIEC Interagency) assess compliance
- **Implication for targeting:** Cannot use targeting that effectively excludes protected classes. Must be prepared to demonstrate that targeting is based on business purpose criteria (income potential, DSCR, property type) not demographic characteristics.

**2. TILA/Regulation Z (Truth in Lending)**
- APR disclosures required in any ad that mentions rates, payment amounts, or financing terms
- "Triggering terms" requiring disclosure: specific monthly payment, financing rate, down payment amount
- Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure requirements apply
- **Implication for advertising:** Any rate mentioned must include APR range. Safe harbor: "Rates from X%–Y% depending on loan terms. APR: [range]%." Google Search ads have specific TILA compliance requirements for any rate mention.

**3. SAFE Act (Secure and Fair Enforcement for Mortgage Licensing)**
- NMLS ID disclosure required on all advertisements, application forms, and communications with applicants
- Must appear as "NMLS #XXXXX" (unique identifier per loan officer/lender)
- Required on: all ads, landing pages, email signatures, printed materials
- **Implication:** Every piece of marketing copy, every landing page, every email must include the NMLS number. This is non-negotiable.

**4. FTC Act Section 5 — UDAAP (Unfair, Deceptive, or Abusive Acts or Practices)**
- No misleading claims about approval likelihood, rate guarantees, or loan terms
- Cannot claim "guaranteed approval," "100% qualify," or "everyone gets approved"
- Must be able to substantiate all claims in advertising
- **Implication:** Copy must be factually supportable. Disqualifiers must be specific and defensible.

**5. Fair Housing Act**
- Applies to advertising and marketing practices
- Cannot imply preference for or exclusion of any protected class
- App-based and digital marketing must be audited for disparate impact

**6. State-Level Requirements**
- Licensing requirements vary by state (some states require specific non-QM licensing beyond standard MLO license)
- State-specific disclosures required in certain jurisdictions
- Florida, Texas, California, and New York have particularly active regulatory environments

### Facebook Special Ad Category Compliance Checklist

For Meta advertising of DSCR loan products: [NQMF, September 2023]

1. Select "Credit" as Special Ad Category when setting up ad in Ads Manager
2. **Do not** target by age, gender, zip codes, or multicultural affinities
3. **Do not** use detailed targeting related to protected characteristics, behaviors, or interests that correlate with protected classes
4. Custom Audiences: ensure source data does not include prohibited categories
5. Lookalike Audiences: do not use source audiences built on characteristics in prohibited categories
6. Ad creative: content, images, and text must not discriminate or discourage any individual or group
7. Geography targeting: country level, state/territory level, metropolitan area, or minimum 15-mile radius from specific point
8. Review ads regularly as Facebook policy changes

### Copy Compliance Checklist (Before Deploying Any Ad)

- [ ] No "guaranteed," "100% approval," "everyone qualifies," "no requirements"
- [ ] APR range disclosed where rates, payments, or financing terms are mentioned
- [ ] NMLS number present on all communications (ads, landing pages, emails, forms)
- [ ] Equal Housing Opportunity statement present on all communications
- [ ] Disqualifiers are factual, specific, and defensible (e.g., "DSCR below 1.0" is factual; "low-income borrowers need not apply" is discriminatory)
- [ ] "Not all applicants will qualify" disclaimer included
- [ ] No misleading claims about speed, certainty, or approval probability
- [ ] No claims that could constitute racketeering or discrimination under ECOA/Fair Housing Act

### Adverse Action Compliance

- ECOA requires adverse action notices within 30 days of decision for denied applications
- Must provide specific reasons for denial mapped to scoring criteria
- Must inform applicant of their right to request reconsideration
- Required documentation: decision reason codes, evaluation criteria, applicant response rights

## SYNTHESIS: THE APPROVAL-WEIGHTED PERSONA SCORING MODEL

### Combined Score Formula

The research supports a two-axis scoring model for acquisition prioritization:

**Axis 1 — Fundability Score (0–100)**

| Factor | Weight | Scoring Logic |
|---|---|---|
| DSCR Ratio | 35% | ≥1.50=95, 1.25–1.49=75, 1.00–1.24=55, <1.0=20 |
| Credit Score | 15% | 740+=95, 700+=80, 660+=60, <660=40 |
| Property Type | 15% | SFR=85, Duplex=80, Triplex=70, Condo=60 |
| Income Stability | 15% | 0.90+ stability=90, 0.80+=75, <0.80=50 |
| LTV | 10% | ≤65%=95, ≤70%=80, ≤75%=60, >75%=30 |
| Debt Tolerance | 10% | DTI ≤30%=95, ≤40%=75, ≤50%=50, >50%=20 |

**Axis 2 — Economic Desirability Score (0–100)**

| Factor | Weight | Scoring Logic |
|---|---|---|
| Loan Amount | 25% | $500K+=95, $300K+=75, $150K+=55, <$150K=30 |
| Refi Potential | 20% | $400K+ existing=90, $200K+=70, $100K+=50, <$100K=25 |
| Cross-sell Potential | 20% | Has other properties + business=90, one=60, neither=30 |
| Lifetime Value | 20% | Est. total revenue over 7yr / $10K = score, capped at 100 |
| Acquisition Cost | 15% | Income $15K+/mo=90, $10K+=70, $6K+=50, <$6K=30 |

**Combined Score:** Geometric mean rewards alignment between fundability and economic desirability:
```
Combined = (Fundability × Economic)^0.5 × 0.6 + (0.55×Fundability + 0.45×Economic) × 0.4
```

**Acquisition Priority Tiers:**

| Combined Score | Tier | Action |
|---|---|---|

| 65–79 | **Tier 2 — STRONG** | Active acquisition; quality lead routing; standard intake |

| 35–49 | **Tier 4 — OPPORTUNISTIC** | Fill capacity gaps; low-cost nurture; avoid mass spend |

## STRATEGIC GUIDANCE: OPERATIONAL RECOMMENDATIONS

### Priority 1: Target Tier 1 + Tier 2 Personas Only

The research consistently shows that the majority of marketing spend is wasted on borrowers who are either not fundable or not economically worth acquiring. Focus on:

**Tier 1 acquisition targets:**
- Prime Rental Investors (DSCR 1.50+, multi-property, strong income)
- HNW Portfolio Builders (high loan amounts, multiple streams, refi potential)

**Tier 2 acquisition targets:**
- Established Professional Refi (stable W2 + rental, refi motivation)
- STR Investors (specialty but high-value; requires STR-specific copy and targeting)

### Priority 2: Channel Allocation

| Channel | Budget Allocation | Primary Persona | Expected Close Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search (high-intent) | 40% | All Tier 1–2 personas | 8–15% |
| LinkedIn | 20% | HNW Portfolio Builder, Established Professional | 10–20% |
| Meta | 20% | Prime Rental Investor, Cash Flow Optimizer | 3–8% |
| Referral network | 15% | All personas (warm, pre-qualified) | 15–30% |
| Organic content/SEO | 5% | Early-stage research (long-term CAC reduction) | 10–20% |

### Priority 3: Messaging Framework

**Lead with the problem, not the product:**
- "Traditional lenders struggle with complex income profiles" → resonate with self-employed borrowers who know their tax returns don't reflect their income
- "DSCR focuses on YOUR PROPERTY'S income potential, not your personal income" → the core value proposition

**Three-stage funnel copy:**
1. **Awareness:** "Investment Property Financing That Doesn't Require Your Tax Returns"
2. **Consideration:** "DSCR Pre-Qualification Calculator — Find out your estimated loan amount in 2 minutes"
3. **Decision:** "Ready to Close Your DSCR Loan? Same-week pre-approval. 30-day close typical."

### Priority 4: Intake Scoring and Routing

**Auto-approve path (score ≥ 80):**
- DSCR ≥ 1.25
- FICO ≥ 700
- Property: SFR, duplex, or triplex
- Loan amount: $150K–$750K
- Reserves: 3+ months PITIA
- Income type: W2, rental, or business (verified via lease/1007)

**Manual review path (score 60–79):**
- DSCR 1.00–1.24
- FICO 660–699
- LLC entity structure requiring additional documentation
- Cash-out amount at LTV ceiling

**Auto-deny path (score < 60):**
- DSCR < 1.0 with no compensating factors
- FICO < 620
- Property type not eligible (pure commercial, non-warrantable condo)
- Income unverifiable

### Priority 5: Compliance as a Feature

Required on every piece of communication:
- [ ] NMLS number
- [ ] Equal Housing Opportunity
- [ ] APR disclosure (where rates mentioned)
- [ ] "Not all applicants will qualify"
- [ ] No guaranteed approval language
- [ ] Disqualifiers factual and specific

### Priority 6: Quarterly Optimization Cycle

1. **Feed closed-loan data back into persona scoring** — compare predicted vs. actual close rates by persona and channel
2. **A/B test copy variants** — awareness vs. outcome-focused vs. comparison
3. **Adjust budget by cost-per-funded-deal** — not cost-per-lead
4. **Refresh targeting parameters** — property values and interest rates shift DSCR thresholds
5. **Update compliance documentation** — regulatory changes require copy updates

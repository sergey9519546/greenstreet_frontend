The Guideline Hierarchy: Establishing the 2026
Baseline
The development of a definitive underwriting tool requires a strict adherence to a clear
and unambiguous hierarchy of guidelines. For Cake Mortgage Corp., this means
establishing the 2026 product matrices and manuals as the sole authority for new
business, explicitly superseding all prior, more restrictive 2025-era documents. This
hierarchical structure is not merely an organizational preference; it is a critical
operational directive to ensure accuracy, maintain competitiveness, and align with the
company's strategic pivot toward higher balance, high-LTV lending 12 23. The analysis of
the provided documents reveals a deliberate evolution in risk appetite, moving away from
the punitive constraints of the past toward a more nuanced and accommodating
framework for a broader range of borrowers and transactions 1 .
The first step in building the tool's logic is to formally archive the obsolete 2025 matrices.
These documents, while part of the historical record, contain rules that are no longer
applicable and would lead to incorrect recommendations if used for new applications.
The Sponge Cake matrix (dated September 2025) is architected around highly
conservative Loan-to-Value (LTV) ratios, often capping investment and cash-out
transactions at a restrictive 50% to 55% 12. In direct contrast, the 2026 guidelines offer
significantly more flexible terms, making the old matrix irrelevant for competitive pricing.
Similarly, the Cheese Cake Full Doc matrix (dated October 2025) has been rendered
obsolete by the superior Coffee Cake matrix (effective March 9, 2026). The Coffee Cake
matrix provides a substantially higher maximum loan limit of $2,500,000 compared to
the lower tiers offered by Cheese Cake, and it demonstrates greater flexibility regarding
property types 13. Most critically, Cheese Cake mandated a 36-month seasoning period
for certain credit events, a requirement that was abandoned in the subsequent 2026
guidelines, which instead adopted much more aggressive timelines 13. Finally, the
Funnel Cake and Funnel Cake Lite matrices (dated November 2025) represent the
pinnacle of restrictive policy. Their most notable feature was a punitive 48-month

seasoning requirement for severe credit events such as Bankruptcy, Foreclosure, Deed-in-
Lieu (DIL), and Short Sales 1 . This standard is so antiquated that it renders any
borrower with a recent rehabilitative event ineligible under those specific documents,
making them non-viable for modern underwriting practices where such events are now
handled with LTV adjustments rather than outright denials 1 . Therefore, the
underwriting tool must be programmed to completely ignore these three 2025 matrices
for any new submissions.
The operational foundation of the tool must be built upon the authoritative 2026
guidelines. The primary sources for this architecture are six distinct but interconnected
documents, each serving a specific purpose. The cornerstone for all non-owner-occupied
investment properties is the DSCR v4.0 Manual, effective April 1, 2026 1 . This manual
is the definitive guide for properties that will not be owner-occupied, and it governs the
qualification based solely on rental income, ignoring personal DTI and tax returns 2 4 .
It is the designated program for unique scenarios involving Short-Term Rentals (STRs),
LLC entity vesting, and Foreign National borrowers 3 20. The other five documents
constitute the Non-Qualified Mortgage (Non-QM) and Alt-A product suite, designed for
Primary Residences, Second Homes, and Owner-Occupied Investment Properties. The
Bundt Cake matrices (effective April 14, 2026) have emerged as the new authority for
owner-occupied and non-owner-occupied lending up to a
3,000,000 ceiling <sup><a class="ref-sup" href="#ref-1" target="_self">1</a></sup>. Bundt Cake is distinguished by its aggressive approach to credit events, allowing seasoning after just 12 months, a policy that supersedes the outdated 48-month rule <sup><a class="ref-sup" href="#ref-1" target="_self">1</a></sup>. The <strong>Cup Cake</strong> matrix (effective March 9, 2026) serves as the heavyweight champion of the Non-QM space, offering the highest loan limit in the entire portfolio at
4,000,000 1 . It also contains specialized overlays for niche borrower profiles, such as
ITIN holders and self-employed individuals with only one year of history 1 . The Velvet
Cake matrix (effective April 1, 2026) functions as a key alternative for borrowers needing
to use Bank Statements or P&L statements who may be excluded from other programs
due to citizenship status (e.g., Foreign Nationals) 1 . The Pound Cake Lite matrix
(effective February 13, 2026) carves out a specific niche for borrowers utilizing a Written
Verification of Employment (WVOE) or those with less than two years of self-employment
history 1 . Lastly, the Coffee Cake matrix (effective March 9, 2026) acts as the
traditional QM alternative, catering to full-documentation borrowers seeking higher loan
amounts than Agency guidelines permit and allowing for non-standard property types
like non-warrantable condos 1 13. The underwriting tool must be architected as a
dynamic engine that can ingest user inputs and cross-reference them against the
attributes of these six 2026 matrices to provide a single, optimized recommendation,
effectively filtering out the noise of the obsolete 2025-era documents.

Programmatic Specialization: A Deep Dive into Active
Matrices
A truly definitive underwriting tool cannot function as a monolithic calculator; it must
recognize and leverage the distinct specializations of each 2026 program. The current
landscape of Cake Mortgage's wholesale offerings is a portfolio of finely tuned
instruments, each designed to address a specific borrower profile, property type, or
documentation challenge. Understanding the nuances of these programs is paramount to
developing a routing engine that delivers optimal outcomes. The active 2026 matrices—
DSCR v4.0, Bundt Cake, Cup Cake, Velvet Cake, Pound Cake Lite, and Coffee Cake—are
not interchangeable; they possess unique strengths and weaknesses that create arbitrage
opportunities for Loan Officers and brokers. The tool must be programmed to identify

these "sweet spots" and recommend the program that offers the highest possible loan
amount, most favorable terms, or simplest path to approval for a given scenario.
The DSCR v4.0 Manual stands alone as the exclusive gateway for Non-Owner-Occupied
(NOO) investment properties where qualification is based entirely on the property's
ability to generate rental income 2 . Its core principle is the Debt Service Coverage Ratio,
calculated as Gross Rental Income divided by the qualifying monthly payment (PITIA or
ITIA for interest-only loans) 22. Rounding up the resulting ratio is strictly prohibited 1 .
This program's specialization lies in its disregard for the borrower's personal financial
situation, specifically employment, tax returns, and debt-to-income (DTI) ratios 1 . This
makes it ideal for passive investors, foreign nationals, and those with complex personal
finances. A key differentiator is its explicit allowance of Short-Term Rental (STR) income,
such as from Airbnb or VRBO, provided an AirDNA Rentalizer report is submitted for
purchase transactions 3 4 . The report must cover a 12-month forecast, achieve a
market score of at least 60, and adhere to specific occupancy caps 1 . Furthermore,
DSCR is the only program that permits property titles to be vested directly into eligible
legal entities like LLCs, Corporations, and Inter-Vivos Revocable Trusts, a crucial feature
for sophisticated real estate investors looking to manage liability and succession 1 .
Foreign Nationals are fully eligible for DSCR loans, provided they meet a specific
checklist of requirements, including valid identification, a U.S. bank account for
automatic payments, and alternative credit verification 1 . For multifamily properties
with 5 to 9 units, the DSCR program imposes a minimum DSCR of 1.00 and requires a
Debt Yield of at least 9% for loans over $2,000,000 1 .
In contrast, the 2026 Non-QM and Alt-Doc suite is tailored for owner-occupied properties
and some investment scenarios where the borrower wishes to qualify using alternative
income documentation. Bundt Cake has become the de facto leader in this space due to
its highly favorable terms. With a maximum loan amount of
3,000,000, it competes directly with Velvet Cake but offers superior terms for borrowers with recent credit events <sup><a class="ref-sup" href="#ref-1" target="_self">1</a></sup>. Its standout feature is the liberalized credit event seasoning policy, which allows for Bankruptcies, Foreclosures, and Short Sales after just 12 months, albeit with a corresponding CLTV reduction penalty <sup><a class="ref-sup" href="#ref-1" target="_self">1</a></sup>. Bundt Cake also extends special accommodations to First-Time Homebuyers (FTHBs), allowing them to qualify up to a
3,000,000 loan amount, a significant advantage over competitors 1 . Another powerful
feature is the residual income overlay, which allows for DTIs up to 55% as long as the
borrower meets a minimum residual income threshold of 2,500plus150 per dependent
1 . For reserve requirements, Bundt Cake offers a valuable "reserve hack": a borrower
can opt to take a 5% CLTV reduction in exchange for a 3-month reduction in required
reserves, or even waive reserves entirely on a Rate/Term refinance if the CLTV is at or
below 65% 1 .
Cup Cake distinguishes itself by pushing the boundaries of loan amounts and borrower
complexity. As the heavyweight with a

Borrower Profile Arbitrage: Navigating Complex
Demographics and Credit Events
The most sophisticated underwriting tools transcend basic eligibility checks and excel at
navigating the complexities of non-traditional borrower profiles. The 2026 Cake
Mortgage guidelines provide a rich set of rules and exceptions for a wide array of
demographics, from foreign nationals to recently rehabilitated credit profiles. A definitive
tool must be able to parse these nuances and perform "arbitrage"—identifying the specific
program that offers the most advantageous terms for a given borrower's unique
combination of circumstances. This involves deep knowledge of citizenship-specific rules,
credit event seasoning timelines, and the subtle differences in how various matrices treat
similar borrower types.
A prime example of this is the distinction between owner-occupied and non-owner-
occupied (NOO) programs when dealing with Foreign Nationals (FNs). The DSCR v4.0
program explicitly outlines FN eligibility, requiring a valid passport, Visa/ESTA, a foreign
primary residence address, alternative credit verification (like foreign bank statements),
and a U.S. bank account for automated payments 1 . This makes DSCR the default
choice for FN investors. However, for FNs purchasing an owner-occupied primary
residence, the Non-QM landscape presents challenges. While Velvet Cake is explicitly
unavailable to Foreign Nationals, Bundt Cake and Cup Cake offer pathways. Bundt Cake
allows Non-Permanent Residents who have established U.S. credit, with a maximum loan
of 1,500,000andan801.5M loan limit and 80% Max CLTV 1 . This creates a clear decision
tree: for an FN investor, DSCR is the answer. For an FN buying their primary home, the
tool must check if they have U.S. credit history; if yes, Bundt Cake or Cup Cake becomes
viable options, with Cup Cake being preferable due to its higher loan limit ceiling.
Another critical area of arbitrage is for First-Time Homebuyers (FTHBs). An FTHB is
defined as someone who has never owned any real estate. The guidelines treat them
differently depending on the program. In Bundt Cake NQM, FTHBs are not only allowed
but are catered to with specific overlays. An FTHB with rental history can qualify with a

===== PAGE 7 =====

640 FICO, while one without rental history is capped at an 80% CLTV and a 45% DTI 1 .
Bundt Cake further allows FTHBs up to its
3,000,000 loan limit, making it the premier choice for this profile <sup><a class="ref-sup" href="#ref-1" target="_self">1</a></sup>. Conversely, <strong>Cup Cake</strong> severely restricts the FTHB profile, limiting the loan amount to
1,000,000, restricting the property to a primary residence only, and prohibiting 2-4 unit
properties entirely 1 . Meanwhile, Bundt Cake NOO is even stricter, rendering an FTHB
without rental history completely ineligible for an investment property 1 . This stark
contrast highlights the need for the tool to differentiate between owner-occupied and
NOO contexts when evaluating an FTHB applicant.
Perhaps the most significant evolution in the 2026 guidelines is the dramatic reduction in
credit event seasoning, creating a major arbitrage opportunity. The old 48-month rule
from Funnel Cake is dead 1 . The new standard, pioneered by Bundt Cake, is a tiered
approach based on a 12-month window.
• Less than 12 Months Since Event: Ineligible across all programs.
• 12 to 23 Months Since Event: This is the "sweet spot." Bundt Cake allows
Bankruptcy, Foreclosure, Short Sale, or DIL, but applies a CLTV reduction penalty.
For instance, a foreclosure within this window triggers a 15% CLTV reduction, and
Cash-Out refinances are prohibited 1 . Cup Cake follows a similar policy, capping
the Max CLTV at 65% for Purchases and Refinances during this period 1 . This
makes both Bundt Cake and Cup Cake the correct choices for borrowers in this
category.
• 24 to 35 Months Since Event: The penalty is reduced. Both Bundt Cake and Cup
Cake reduce the CLTV reduction to 5% 1 .
• 36+ Months Since Event: Standard, unpenalized terms can generally be applied.
However, the tool must also be aware of the granular, often deal-killer, details within
these policies. For instance, the tolerance for mortgage late payments varies significantly.
Across all 2026 Non-QM matrices, a late payment of 120 days or more (0x120x12) is an
absolute disqualifier 1 . A late of 90 days (0x90x12) is more nuanced. Bundt Cake NOO
lists this as strictly ineligible 1 . However, Bundt Cake NQM and Cup Cake (both NQM
and NOO) allow a 0x90x12 late, but with a penalty—a mandatory 5% CLTV reduction
for Bundt Cake NQM and a max CLTV cap of 75% (Purchase) / 70% (Refinance) for Cup
Cake 1 . This means a Loan Officer could potentially save a deal by switching from an
NOO scenario to an owner-occupied one if the only issue is a 90-day late. The tool must
therefore ask about the worst derogatory event and its duration, and then cross-reference
it with the specific matrix's late-payment rules before making a recommendation.

Collateral and Property Nuances: Mastering Valuation
and Risk Mitigation
Beyond borrower characteristics, the underwriting process is heavily governed by the
attributes of the collateral itself. A definitive tool must possess a deep understanding of
the rules surrounding property types, valuation methodologies, and secondary risks like
short-term rentals and multifamily structures. The 2026 guidelines contain numerous
granular rules that dictate eligibility, impose LTV caps, and trigger specific requirements,
such as second appraisals or debt yield calculations. Failure to adhere to these collateral-
centric rules can result in immediate submission denial or costly delays.
One of the most significant bifurcations in collateral treatment is the handling of Short-
Term Rentals (STRs). There is a clear and intentional separation between the DSCR
program and the Non-QM/Alt-Doc investment programs. The DSCR v4.0 manual
explicitly permits the use of STR gross rents as qualifying income for purchase
transactions, contingent upon the submission of a compliant AirDNA Rentalizer report 3
4 . This report must be dated within 90 days of the note date, provide a 12-month

===== PAGE 9 =====

forecast, include at least 3 comparable properties, reflect a market score of 60 or higher,
and cap occupancy at two individuals per bedroom 1 . This makes DSCR the exclusive
vehicle for financing STR properties. In stark contrast, all Non-QM and Alt-Doc matrices
for investment properties (specifically Bundt Cake NOO and Cup Cake NOO) list Short-
Term Rentals as ineligible 1 . Attempting to submit an STR property for a NOO loan
under these programs would be a fatal error. The tool must therefore establish a hard
dependency: if the property is an STR, route the user exclusively to the DSCR v4.0 logic
path.
Property type and location also trigger specific overlays and restrictions. Non-
Warrantable Condominiums are permitted across multiple programs, but with important
caveats. Both Bundt Cake and Cup Cake allow them, but they prohibit the use of 1099
or P&L income for these properties; only full documentation or bank statements are
acceptable 1 . Furthermore, the DSCR manual notes that for multifamily properties, if an
appraisal scores above a certain threshold, a cascading review process is triggered,
potentially leading to a mandatory second appraisal 1 . Other property types carry
outright prohibitions. Assisted living facilities, Barndominiums, Co-ops, Log Homes,
Geodesic Domes, Houseboats, and properties larger than 20 acres are explicitly ineligible
for DSCR loans 1 . For Non-QM programs, Manufactured Homes and Agricultural
properties are also typically ineligible, with the notable exception of Velvet Cake, which
uniquely allows Agricultural and Hobby Farms 1 . Location-based overlays add another
layer of complexity. Florida is identified as a state requiring an additional 5% CLTV
reduction for properties using WVOE, 1099, P&L, Condotels, or Rural properties under
the Cup Cake matrix 1 . This requires the tool to prompt for the property's state and
apply the Florida overlay if applicable.
Valuation and appraisal requirements are stringent and serve as a critical control point.
All appraisals must be no older than 120 days at the time of the note date 1 . A second
appraisal is required for two distinct scenarios: either the loan amount is $2,000,000 or
more, or the transaction is classified as a "Flip" 1 . A flip is defined as reselling the
property within 180 days of acquisition for a price increase of more than 10%. This
triggers arm's length marketing requirements and necessitates the second appraisal 1 .
For multifamily properties with 5 to 9 units, a Commercial Exterior Only Broker Price
Opinion (BPO) is required unless two full appraisals are provided. If the BPO value comes
in more than 10% below the appraisal value, the lower BPO value must be used for all
LTV calculations 1 . Furthermore, there are specific workflow rules for transferred
appraisals. They must be less than 60 days old at the time of transfer, and a 1004D
recertification is explicitly prohibited on a transferred appraisal 1 . This is a crucial
procedural rule that processors must follow to avoid submission issues.

Finally, certain property features introduce unique underwriting considerations. Solar
panels are a case in point. On DSCR loans, if a solar loan payment is subordinated to the
mortgage, that payment must be factored into the DSCR calculation 1 . Additionally,
PACE loans for solar installations are strictly ineligible for financing through Cake
Mortgage 1 . Velvet Cake adds a specific seasoning requirement: if a borrower has a
solar loan, they must have made six months of payments as agreed before the loan can be
approved 1 . Cryptocurrency assets are also treated with specific rules. Bitcoin and
Ethereum are permitted for funds to close and reserves, but with strict conditions: the
crypto must be liquidated to USD and deposited into a U.S. bank account for funds to
close, and for reserves, only 60% of the asset's current valuation (documented within 30
days) is eligible 1 . These detailed, feature-specific rules demonstrate that a successful
underwriting tool must look beyond the property address and transaction type to
scrutinize every aspect of the collateral itself.
Operational Guardrails and Hidden Constraints:
Preventing Post-Submission Failures
An underwriting tool's ultimate value is measured not only by its ability to find approvals
but also by its capacity to prevent rejections caused by easily avoidable operational
errors. The most frustrating failures for Loan Officers are those that occur post-
submission, often due to overlooked fine-print rules, known as "hidden constraints" or
"deal-killers." A world-class tool must integrate a robust "Red Flag" module that
proactively identifies these silent deal-breakers before the file is sent to the underwriter.
These constraints span documentation timing, title requirements, repair holds, and third-
party service limitations.
One of the most critical operational guardrails is related to appraisals and repairs. The
DSCR v4.0 manual enforces a strict "No Escrow Holdbacks" policy 1 . This means that
any repairs required by the appraiser must be completed and inspected prior to loan
funding. You cannot fund the loan and hold back repair money in escrow. This places a
significant burden on the processor to ensure the property condition is perfect before
locking the loan. Another crucial rule pertains to transferred appraisals. An appraisal can
only be transferred if it is less than 60 days old at the time of transfer, and a 1004D
recertification is strictly prohibited on a transferred appraisal 1 . Violating these
workflow rules will result in an immediate rejection.

===== PAGE 11 =====

Documentation timing and eligibility also present common pitfalls. The Pound Cake Lite
matrix specifies different timeframes for completing a verbal VOE based on the
borrower's employment status: 10 days for wage earners and 30 days for self-employed
borrowers 1 . Missing these deadlines can delay processing. Asset sourcing is another
area of nuance. While DSCR loans do not require asset seasoning or sourcing for large
deposits, there is a rule for business accounts. If a borrower owns less than 100% of a
business but wants to use funds exceeding their ownership percentage, they must provide
a signed letter from the other owners granting access to those specific funds 1 . Spousal
accounts can be used for down payment and closing costs, but they are strictly ineligible
for satisfying reserve requirements 1 . For alternative credit, if a borrower lacks
traditional credit cards, they can use eight alternative tradelines, but this requires a four-
year established credit history and one tradeline open for at least 12 months 1 .
Title and housing history requirements have their own set of rigid rules. For DSCR cash-
out refinances, the property must have been on title for at least 90 days 1 . However,
this rule has exceptions for transfers due to inheritance, divorce decree, or legal award,
which must be properly documented to bypass the 90-day lockout 1 . Housing history is
required for all borrowers, defined as 12 months of verified ownership or rental history
1 . A property owned free-and-clear counts as a clean payment history (0x30). For First-
Time Homebuyers (FTHBs) or experienced investors living with family, a rent-free letter
is an acceptable substitute for a VOM/VOR 1 . Derogatory credit items like collections
and charge-offs can generally be ignored and do not need to be paid off, unless they are
title-impacting, meaning they could result in a lien on the property 1 .
Entity structuring and Power of Attorney (POA) usage are governed by strict legal and
compliance protocols. When a loan is made to an entity (LLC, Corporation, etc.), a
Personal Guarantor is required. This guarantor must represent at least 51% cumulative
ownership in the entity and have signing authority 1 . Layered LLCs are permitted, but
only up to two layers; trust-layered LLCs are ineligible 1 . A critical distinction is that a
personal guarantee is only required when the entity itself is the borrower on the 1003
form. If an individual is the borrower and simply vests the title into an LLC, no personal
guarantee is needed 1 . POAs are highly restricted. They are permitted only for U.S.
Citizens and Permanent Residents. They are strictly prohibited for Foreign Nationals, for
any Cash-Out refinances, and for any transaction where the property is being vested into
an LLC or other entity 1 . This last rule forces alternative closing solutions for investors,
such as using a DSCR loan where the individual borrower can vest into an LLC without a
POA. Attempting to use a POA in violation of these rules is a direct path to denial.

Synthesis and Strategic Implementation Framework
The comprehensive analysis of Cake Mortgage's 2025-2026 guidelines culminates in a
clear blueprint for developing a definitive, industry-leading DSCR and Non-QM
underwriting tool. The objective is not merely to create a static repository of rules but to
engineer a dynamic, logic-driven decision engine that anticipates user needs, validates
against silent deal-killers, and routes complex scenarios to the optimal program path. The
strategic implementation of this tool hinges on three core principles derived from the
research: strict hierarchical enforcement of the 2026 guidelines, deep integration of
granular operational constraints, and the creation of a sophisticated, multi-step decision
framework.
First, the tool's foundational architecture must enforce a rigid hierarchy that exclusively
utilizes the 2026 guidelines while completely archiving the outdated 2025 matrices like
Sponge, Funnel, and Cheese 1 12. This ensures that every recommendation is current
and competitive, reflecting the company's strategic shift toward higher balance, high-LTV
lending and a more forgiving stance on recent credit rehabilitation 1 . The tool should be

amounts, LTVs, DTI limits, and documentation requirements based on user inputs.
Second, the tool's intelligence will be defined by its ability to embed and execute the
granular, often hidden, "deal-killer" rules. These are the silent constraints that cause the
majority of post-submission failures. The tool must feature a dedicated validation engine
that cross-references user inputs against these rules before generating a program
recommendation. This includes checking for absolute disqualifiers like a 120+ day

Third, the most advanced functionality of the tool will be its strategic routing engine,

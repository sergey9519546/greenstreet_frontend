Beyond the Rulebook: Building a
Probabilistic Underwriting Engine with
Graphs, Conformal Prediction, and
Tabular Foundation Models
This report provides a deep analysis of recent academic breakthroughs from 2025–2026
to construct actionable algorithmic blueprints for a Wall Street-grade Debt Service
Coverage Ratio (DSCR) and Non-Qualified Mortgage (Non-QM) underwriting engine.
The primary objective is to move beyond static, rule-based systems by integrating state-
of-the-art methodologies that address three critical challenges in modern alternative
lending: resolving complex entity structures, rigorously managing the uncertainty of
time-sensitive data, and enabling accurate risk assessment in low-data environments. The
analysis focuses on synthesizing findings from recent scholarly work to deliver
production-ready solutions aligned with the operational demands of contemporary
wholesale mortgage guidelines. Each section details a core component of the proposed
architecture, providing not just theoretical concepts but concrete implementations
designed to create a formidable competitive advantage.
The Graph Neural Network Framework for Entity
Resolution and Fraud Detection in Layered LLCs
The challenge of identifying hidden relationships and potential fraud within complex
legal entity structures is a paramount concern for lenders, particularly those dealing with
investment properties. The provided Cake Mortgage Corp. documents explicitly permit up
to two layers of Limited Liability Companies (LLCs) for property vesting, while
prohibiting trusts layered with LLCs (). This structure, while legally permissible, is often
exploited to obscure beneficial ownership, making it exceedingly difficult for traditional,
feature-based machine learning models to detect if multiple entities are controlled by a
single individual or part of a larger fraudulent ring. Standard Know Your Customer (KYC)
checks that focus on individual borrowers will inevitably fail when the borrower is an
opaque entity. To counter this, the state-of-the-art solution lies in leveraging Temporal

===== PAGE 2 =====

Heterogeneous Graph Neural Networks (THGNNS), which are specifically designed to
learn from graph-structured data where nodes represent diverse entities, edges represent
various types of relationships, and the entire structure evolves over time 3 8 . This
approach directly addresses the research goal of using graph-based entity resolution to
uncover the "hidden relationships" inherent in layered LLCs.
A foundational understanding of GNNs reveals their power to process relational data,
which is inherently structured in graphs 4 28. Unlike sequential or tabular data formats,
graphs naturally encode the connections between entities, allowing the model to learn
representations based on both a node's intrinsic features and its network context. For
instance, a person's creditworthiness is not just a function of their personal income and
FICO score; it is also influenced by the financial health of the businesses they own and
the entities they are associated with. THGNNS extend this capability by introducing two
crucial dimensions: heterogeneity and temporality. Heterogeneity allows the model to
handle different types of nodes (e.g., Person, LLC, Address) and different types of
edges (e.g., OWNS with a percentage attribute, LOCATED_AT, CONTACTS_PHONE). This is
critical for real-world applications like entity resolution, where relationships are not
monolithic 9 11. Temporality enables the model to account for how these relationships
and attributes change over time, which is vital for detecting emerging risks, such as a
new layer being added to an existing LLC structure 53.
To build a practical entity resolution framework, a detailed implementation blueprint
must be constructed. The first step is graph construction. This involves creating a
heterogeneous graph where each unique entity becomes a node. For example, a Person
node would have features like name, date of birth, and SSN. An LLC node would contain
information from official filings, such as the Articles of Organization, date of formation,
and registered agent. Other node types could include Address, Phone, and Email.
Edges connect these nodes, representing the relationships found in public records and
application data. For instance, a LOCATED_AT edge connects an LLC to an Address,
while an OWNS edge connects a Person to an LLC, annotated with a percentage of
ownership. This richly structured graph serves as the input for the GNN model.
The next phase is data ingestion and integration. The system must pull data from
multiple, disparate sources via APIs to build a comprehensive and up-to-date graph. Key
API integrations would include Secretary of State (SOS) databases through services like
CorpAPI or BizFile to obtain official certificates of good standing and articles of
organization for each LLC mentioned in a transaction 28. Borrower-provided information
from the loan application forms (like the 1003) must also be ingested. Furthermore,
external data scraping for public records, news mentions, or social media profiles can

===== PAGE 3 =====

provide additional links and features to enrich the graph. This multi-source data fusion is
what allows the model to build a holistic view of the borrower and their associated
entities, moving beyond the limited scope of any single data provider.
With the graph constructed and data flowing in, the choice of model architecture
becomes critical. Recent advancements in GNNs offer powerful architectures suitable for
this task. Heterogeneous Graph Transformers (HGT) are a prime candidate, as they
employ separate attention mechanisms for different node and edge types, allowing them
to learn nuanced and specific relational patterns 9 . Another strong contender is the
Temporal Graph Network (TGN), which is designed to handle dynamic graphs where
nodes and edges can appear and disappear over time 11. Given that new LLCs are
formed and relationships change continuously, a TGN could effectively model the
evolution of the entity network, flagging sudden changes or the appearance of suspicious
new connections. These architectures allow the model to learn a latent representation for
each node in the graph, capturing its complex role within the network.
Once the model is trained, it can be applied to perform several key tasks directly related
to underwriting. The primary task would be node classification, where the model
predicts a label for each Person node. Labels could include BeneficialOwner,
ShellCompanyOperator, FirstTimeInvestor, or HighRiskIndividual. By
analyzing the collective features of all connected nodes (e.g., other LLCs owned,
addresses shared, phone numbers used), the model can make much more informed
classifications than a simple threshold check. A secondary task is link prediction, where
the model identifies missing or unexpected edges in the graph. For example, it might
predict a high probability of an OWNS link between two individuals who currently have no
formal connection listed but share numerous other attributes (same address, same phone
number, etc.). Such a prediction would serve as a high-priority red flag for manual
review. Finally, the model can be used for anomaly detection. By learning the typical
patterns of legitimate investor networks, it can identify transactions that deviate
significantly from the norm, such as an individual owning dozens of LLCs across different
states with no discernible business purpose.
The output of this GNN framework is a set of quantitative risk scores for each entity
involved in a transaction. Instead of a simple pass/fail decision, the system provides a
nuanced assessment of the likelihood that a given entity is part of a larger, potentially
risky network. This directly translates into actionable intelligence for the underwriter. For
example, if the GNN flags a borrower as a potential shell company operator based on
their extensive web of seemingly unrelated LLCs, the underwriter is alerted to request
additional documentation, such as business plans or source of funds for each entity,
before proceeding. This automated, graph-based due diligence process is far more robust

===== PAGE 4 =====

and scalable than manual reviews, directly addressing the need to manage risk in layered
LLC structures as specified in the research goal. It transforms the underwriting process
from a reactive, rules-based check to a proactive, intelligence-driven investigation into
the true nature of the borrowing entity.

Conformal Prediction: A Mathematical Framework for
Quantifying Decaying Data Confidence
A second critical pillar for a Wall Street-grade underwriting engine is the ability to
rigorously quantify the uncertainty associated with its inputs, particularly those that are
time-sensitive. The Cake Mortgage guidelines impose strict age limits on key data points:
appraisals must be less than 120 days old at the note date, AirDNA reports are typically
valid for 90 days, and tax assessments are annual . As these data points age, their

===== PAGE 5 =====

accuracy and relevance diminish, yet current systems lack a formal, mathematical way to
measure this degradation in confidence. Relying on subjective judgment to decide when
to re-order a $600 appraisal or whether to trust a six-month-old rent estimate introduces
both unnecessary costs and significant risk. The solution to this problem is Conformal
Prediction (CP), a model-agnostic statistical framework that generates prediction
intervals with statistically guaranteed coverage, providing a principled way to manage
uncertainty as data decays over time 1 86.
The core principle of conformal prediction is to take any black-box machine learning
model—be it a gradient boosting regressor, a neural network, or even a simple arithmetic
mean—and wrap it in a procedure that produces a prediction set (an interval or a range
of values) instead of a single point estimate 86. The most powerful aspect of CP is its
guarantee: for a pre-specified confidence level (e.g., 95%), the true value will fall within
the generated prediction interval for at least that percentage of future predictions,
assuming the data is exchangeable 71. This is a stark contrast to standard regression
models that might output a rental estimate of
"3,000permonth′′withoutanyindicationofitsreliability.CPwouldinsteadproduceanintervallike′′
[2,800, 3,200] per month," with the guarantee that 95% of the time, the true rent falls
within this range. This transforms a point forecast into a probabilistic statement about the
underlying reality, which is invaluable for risk-sensitive decisions like setting Loan-to-
Value (LTV) ratios.
To implement CP for the underwriting workflow, a systematic blueprint must be
followed. First, a nonconformity score function must be defined. This function measures
how "unusual" a new data point is relative to a set of calibration data. For an appraisal,
the nonconformity score could be the absolute difference between the predicted value
from a base model (e.g., a model trained on comparable sales) and the actual appraised
value. For a rent estimate, it could be the difference between the AVM's prediction and
the actual rent collected for similar properties in the area. Second, a calibration dataset
is required. This is a held-out set of data, large enough to represent the distribution of
errors your base model makes. The nonconformity scores for all points in this calibration
set are computed and stored. This step is crucial because it establishes the empirical
distribution of errors, which is the foundation of the statistical guarantees.
Once the calibration is complete, the prediction interval is generated. When a new data
point arrives (e.g., a rental estimate from RentCast), its nonconformity score is
calculated. The final prediction interval is then constructed to include all hypothetical
values that would result in a nonconformity score "less surprising" than the one just
observed. In practice, this means sorting all the nonconformity scores from the calibration
set and selecting a range around the new score to achieve the desired confidence level.

===== PAGE 6 =====

This process ensures that the width of the interval is directly tied to the variability
observed in the calibration data. Now, to introduce the concept of decaying confidence,
the nonconformity score calculation itself must be modified. The importance of a data
point should decrease as its age increases. This aligns with the principles of Dempster-
Shafer theory, which models uncertainty and ignorance rather than just probabilities 42.
For example, the weight assigned to a rent estimate could be multiplied by an
−λt
exponential decay factor, e , where t is the age in days and λ is a decay rate
parameter specific to the data type. A Tier 1 data point like a county tax record might
have a very low λ, decaying over 365 days, while a Tier 3 subjective data point like a
borrower-stated rent might have a high λ, decaying in 30 days. As the data ages, the
effective error variance used in the nonconformity score calculation increases, causing the
resulting prediction interval to automatically widen. This provides a direct, quantifiable
measure of declining confidence.
The practical output of this Conformal Prediction vault is transformative for the
underwriting process. Instead of a binary "appraisal is stale," the system can provide a
nuanced risk assessment. For instance, it might state, "With 95% confidence, the true
property value is between XandY. Proceeding with this appraisal would reduce our
statistical confidence in the LTV ratio to below 80%. Would you like to proceed?" This
turns a subjective judgment call into a quantitative risk decision that can be programmed
into the workflow. If the widening interval causes the projected LTV to breach the
lender's internal risk tolerance, the system can automatically trigger a requirement for a
new appraisal or a desk review. This prevents both the unnecessary cost of premature re-
appraisals and the perilous risk of relying on data whose reliability has eroded. It
provides a robust, mathematical backbone for managing the lifecycle of every data asset
in the underwriting file, ensuring that decisions are made with a clear understanding of
the evidence's strength and recency.

Tabular Foundation Models for Zero-Shot and Few-Shot
Underwriting in Low-Data Scenarios
The third critical challenge in modern alternative lending is accurately assessing risk for
niche loan products that often suffer from a scarcity of historical default data. Cake
Mortgage's product suite caters to a wide array of complex borrowers, including Foreign
Nationals, ITIN holders, and self-employed individuals with less than two years of
history, as well as investors purchasing unique property types like Hobby Farms, Non-
Warrantable Condos, and Short-Term Rentals . Traditional machine learning approaches,
such as XGBoost, require vast amounts of labeled training data to perform well. When
historical defaults for a specific product line (e.g., DSCR loans for 5-9 unit multifamily
properties in Florida) are sparse, these models struggle, leading to poor generalization
and unreliable risk scores. The breakthrough solution to this problem is the emergence of

===== PAGE 8 =====

Tabular Foundation Models (TFMs), which leverage large-scale pre-training to enable
zero-shot and few-shot learning, thereby democratizing accurate risk modeling for
previously underserved niches 17 19.
TFMs represent a paradigm shift from specialized models to general-purpose ones.
Instead of training a new model from scratch for each new product, a TFM is first pre-
trained on a massive corpus of millions of diverse, real-world tabular datasets 89 90.
During this pre-training phase, the model learns fundamental patterns about tabular
data, such as how numerical columns relate to each other, how categorical variables
interact, and the general structure of transactional data. This vast, generalized knowledge
is encoded within the model's weights. Once pre-trained, the TFM can be used for
inference on entirely new tasks with little to no fine-tuning. This is the essence of zero-
shot learning: the model can make a prediction for a new class or scenario it has never
explicitly seen before, simply by leveraging its learned understanding of tabular structure
and relationships 48. This capability is a direct response to the research goal of enabling
underwriting in low-data situations.
Among the available TFMs, TabPFN stands out as a state-of-the-art example. Developed
by researchers at institutions like Imperial College London, TabPFN is a transformer-
based model specifically designed for small to medium-sized datasets 18 21. Its
architecture is optimized for few-shot and zero-shot scenarios, demonstrating remarkable
performance even when given only a handful of examples from a new domain 82. The
core innovation of TabPFN is its ability to generate a full predictive probability
distribution over the target variable (e.g., DefaultProbability) without needing to
train on the target dataset. It achieves this by using the pre-trained model to simulate
thousands of possible decision trees or other simple models, effectively averaging their
predictions to arrive at a robust estimate. This approach avoids the need for
backpropagation and gradient descent on a small dataset, which can lead to overfitting.
The implementation of a TFM-based underwriting module follows a straightforward
blueprint. The first step is to select an appropriate model, with TabPFN being an excellent
starting point due to its demonstrated success in credit risk applications 76. The model
ingests a table where rows represent individual loan applications and columns represent
features, such as borrower FICO score, LTV, debt yield, property type, and occupancy
status—all drawn directly from the fields available in the Cake Mortgage guidelines. The
target variable is typically a binary outcome like WillDefault.
For a completely new product line, the zero-shot application is the key differentiator.
When a broker submits the first few dozen applications for a niche product like a DSCR

===== PAGE 9 =====

loan for a 6-unit condo in Miami, a standard model would be useless. A TFM, however,
can immediately begin generating risk scores. It leverages its pre-training to understand
the implicit relationships between the provided features. For example, it knows from
having seen millions of other tables that a higher debt yield generally corresponds to a
lower default probability, and that certain property types are riskier than others. It
combines this general knowledge with the specific feature values of the new application
to produce a baseline prediction. While this initial score may not be perfectly calibrated,
it provides a valuable starting point and demonstrates the feasibility of underwriting the
product at all.
As more data becomes available, the model transitions to few-shot mode. With just a
handful of labeled examples (e.g., 20-50 historical loans for that specific product type),
the TFM can be quickly fine-tuned. This process adjusts the model's generalized
knowledge to better fit the specific nuances of the new product. For example, it might
learn that for 5-9 unit multifamily properties, a vacancy factor of 5% is more appropriate
than the standard 10%, or that loans in a particular zip code have historically performed
differently than the national average. This fine-tuning dramatically improves the model's
accuracy with minimal data, creating a powerful feedback loop. The platform can start
underwriting a new product safely using the zero-shot baseline, and as it gathers more
volume, the model's performance automatically improves through few-shot adaptation.
The advantage of TFMs over other approaches is profound in the context of Non-QM and
DSCR lending. They directly address the challenge of sparse data that plagues traditional
models. By providing a reliable risk score even when historical defaults are scarce, they
unlock entire classes of borrowers and properties that were previously considered too
risky or costly to service. This capability is not just a technical novelty; it is a strategic
enabler that allows a fintech platform to penetrate markets that competitors cannot,
solidifying its leadership position. The ability to instantly generate a credible
underwriting opinion on a novel loan product gives a platform a speed and agility that
legacy systems, reliant on months of data collection and model retraining, can never
match.

Synthesizing the Architectural Blueprint for a
Probabilistic Underwriting Engine
The creation of a Wall Street-grade DSCR and Non-QM underwriting engine requires
more than the mere application of individual technologies; it demands the synthesis of
these advanced methodologies into a unified, coherent architectural blueprint. The three
pillars identified—the Graph Neural Network for entity resolution, the Conformal
Prediction framework for uncertainty quantification, and the Tabular Foundation Model
for low-data underwriting—are not isolated components but interconnected modules of a
single, probabilistic decision-making system. This integrated architecture transforms the
static, rule-based guidelines of Cake Mortgage into a dynamic, risk-aware quantitative
engine capable of navigating complexity and uncertainty with a precision that will

===== PAGE 11 =====

establish an unassailable competitive moat. The final synthesis moves beyond a simple
calculator to a comprehensive quantitative risk and wealth generation platform.
The core of this architecture is a data ingestion and processing pipeline built upon the
foundational works of quantitative finance, which remain indispensable for regulatory
and audit purposes. This bedrock consists of the Student’s t-copula for modeling joint
default risk, Brent’s Method for finding exact deal-break rates, and the QuantLib library
for bootstrapping SOFR forward curves 3 54. These established techniques provide the
stable, mathematically proven foundation upon which the more innovative modules are
built. The t-copula, in particular, is essential as it correctly models tail dependence—the
tendency for multiple adverse events to occur simultaneously—thereby avoiding the
catastrophic failure of simpler Gaussian models 3 . This ensures that the engine's stress-
testing of DSCR ratios against macroeconomic shocks is both rigorous and defensible to
secondary market investors.
Upon this foundation, the three primary algorithmic modules are layered to execute the
underwriting process in a specific sequence. The workflow begins with the Entity
Resolution Module, powered by a Temporal Heterogeneous Graph Neural Network
(THGNN). As soon as a loan application is submitted, especially one involving an LLC,
this module springs into action. It ingests data from SOS APIs and other sources to
construct a graph of the borrower's entity network 11 28. It analyzes this graph to resolve
the true beneficial ownership, detect hidden relationships, and assign a risk score to the
entity. This output is not a final decision but a critical piece of contextual intelligence fed
into the next stage. A flagged entity does not automatically lead to denial but triggers a
higher level of scrutiny and potentially requires additional documentation, ensuring that
Fair Lending laws are respected while enhancing due diligence.
Simultaneously, the Decaying Evidence Vault, implemented via the Conformal
Prediction framework, assesses the quality of the collateral and income data 86. As live
data streams in from APIs like ATTOM for taxes and RentCast for rent estimates, the
system calculates not just a point estimate but a prediction interval with a guaranteed
confidence level 38. The width of this interval is dynamically adjusted based on the age
of the data, providing a continuous, quantitative measure of confidence. This module acts
as a real-time guardian of data integrity. If a requested appraisal is approaching its 120-
day expiration, the system doesn't just show a countdown; it shows how the widening
prediction interval for the property's value impacts the LTV ratio, prompting an
automated recommendation for a re-evaluation before the risk becomes unacceptable.

Finally, the Probabilistic Underwriting Classifier, driven by a Tabular Foundation
Model like TabPFN, performs the core risk assessment 18. This module takes the cleaned,
contextualized, and quantitatively vetted data—including the resolved entity risk score,
the confidence-weighted property value, and the borrower's financial profile—and
generates a predictive risk score. For new or niche products, it operates in zero-shot
mode, leveraging its vast pre-training to provide a credible baseline assessment 19. As
data accumulates, it transitions to few-shot mode, continuously refining its predictions.
This classifier's output is the heart of the decision engine, providing a nuanced probability
of default that informs the final approval, pricing, and terms of the loan.
The synergy of these modules creates a system far greater than the sum of its parts. The
GNN provides the "who" and "what" behind the transaction, the Conformal Prediction
module provides the "how confident" we are in the collateral data, and the TFM provides
the "what are the odds" of default. Together, they replace a deterministic, brittle rulebook
with a robust, probabilistic, and adaptive framework. This architecture is not just a tool
for compliance; it is a strategic asset. It enables a platform to underwrite riskier, more
complex deals with greater confidence, to price loans more accurately by incorporating
granular risk factors, and to bring new products to market faster and safer than
competitors still using outdated, data-hungry methodologies. The ultimate competitive
advantage is not in any single algorithm but in the seamless integration of these three
pillars, creating a quantitative engine that is not only compliant with the 2026 guidelines
but is fundamentally superior to anything else in the marketplace.

## DSCR Risk Stratification (50 states + DC)

### HIGH RISK (12 jurisdictions) — Cap directly conflicts with DSCR rates (10-12%+)

These states have constitutional or statutory caps ≤ 10% that are **lower than typical DSCR rates**. DSCR programs can only operate in these states if:
1. The loan is structured as **business-purpose** (avoids consumer usury)
2. The lender holds an **active state license** (mortgage banker / supervised lender)
3. The lender is a **national bank / federal savings bank** (NATIONAL BANK ACT preemption)

## Critical Exemption Categories (all 51 jurisdictions)

Every state exempts (at minimum) these categories from usury caps:
1. **Depository institutions** — banks, savings banks, savings associations, credit unions
2. **State-licensed lenders** — mortgage bankers, supervised lenders, installment lenders, small-loan lenders (with rate cap much higher than consumer cap)
3. **Real estate brokers** — in states where broker-arranged mortgages are exempt
4. **Federally chartered banks** — National Bank Act preemption (12 USC 85)
5. **Federal savings banks / savings associations** — HOLA preemption (12 USC 1464)
6. **Federal credit unions** — FCUA (12 USC 1757) limits but preempts state caps

**Business-purpose loans** are a separate category — many states exempt them entirely (WA, MT, SC), or apply a higher cap (TX 18%, NJ 30%, MA 23%, NY 25%, FL 18%). DSCR loans must be clearly documented as business-purpose to qualify.

## Key Texas + Washington Advantages (Most DSCR-Friendly States)

**Texas (TX Const Art XVI §11; Tex Fin Code §302):**
- 10% consumer cap (10% on consumer mortgage)
- **18% business-purpose written contract rate** was specifically designed for commercial / business-purpose lending
- Penalty: forfeiture of all principal + interest (severe for unlicensed lenders)
- Exemption for: banks, credit unions, TX-licensed mortgage bankers, business-purpose lender with written contract
- **DSCR verdict**: Among the most DSCR-friendly states — explicit 18% business-purpose rate accommodates typical DSCR rates (10-12%)

**Washington (RCW §19.52.020):**
- 12% consumer cap (RCW §19.52.020)
- **BUSINESS LOANS EXEMPT from usury caps** (RCW §19.52.110)
- Penalty: forfeiture of 2x excess interest
- Exemption for: banks, credit unions, WA-licensed mortgage lenders, business-purpose loans (any commercial loan)
- **DSCR verdict**: Most DSCR-friendly state — business loans are exempt entirely

## Critical Compliance Pathways for DSCR Lenders

To operate DSCR programs legally in HIGH-risk states, lenders must satisfy **BOTH** of:

### 1. Lender is exempt by entity status
- Holds state mortgage banker / supervised lender license
- OR is a national bank / federal savings bank (preempted)
- OR is a federal credit union (FCUA preemption)

### 2. Loan is documented as business-purpose
- Borrower is a legal entity (LLC, LP, Corp) OR
- Borrower is a natural person taking the loan for investment/business purposes (not personal/family/residential)
- Documentation: business-purpose affidavit; loan agreement states "business purpose"; property is investment property (1-4 unit non-owner-occupied)
- No TILA/Reg Z consumer protections apply (Reg Z §1026.3(a) business-purpose exemption)

### 3. Loan avoids HOEPA / QM concerns
- DSCR ≥ 1.000 (typical minimum) but verify HOEPA triggers. **Post-Dodd-Frank
  Wall Street Reform Act §1431 (Pub.L. 111-203, July 21, 2010)** — codified at
  12 CFR 1026.32(a)(1) and 12 USC 1602(aa). HOEPA triggers if ANY of:
  - **(i) APR test:** APR > APOR by more than 6.5pp (first-lien, per
    §1026.32(a)(1)(i)(A)) OR 8.5pp (subordinate-lien, per
    §1026.32(a)(1)(i)(C))
  - **(ii) Points-and-fees test (two-tier):**
    - (A) Loan amount ≥ annual threshold ($27,592 in 2026; was $26,968 in
      2025): P&F > 5% of total loan amount
    - (B) Loan amount < annual threshold: P&F > lesser of 8% of total
      loan amount OR annual inflation-adjusted dollar trigger ($1,380 in
      2026, $1,348 in 2025 — both figures adjusted annually per
      §1026.32(a)(1)(ii)(B) and CFPB annual Federal Register notice)
  - **(iii) Prepayment penalty test:** penalty period > 36 months after
    consummation OR penalty > 2% of amount prepaid
    (§1026.32(a)(1)(iii))
  - **Annual thresholds** published each November in Federal Register;
    effective Jan 1 of next year. Confirmed 2026 values in
    Federal Register 2025-22773 (90 FR 57890, Dec 15, 2025).
- DSCR loans priced 10-12% may trigger HOEPA via APR test (1.5pp to 5.5pp
  above typical APOR + 1.5% to 2.0% — depends on rate environment).
  However, **business-purpose DSCR loans are generally exempt from many
  consumer-protection rules including HOEPA** when properly documented as
  business-purpose per Reg Z §1026.3(a) (Appendix B line 217 notes this).
  Consumer-purpose DSCR loans (rare) would still trigger HOEPA under
  the rules above.

## 1. How DSCR Rate Locks Work

A rate lock is a **binding commitment** between the lender and borrower that the loan will fund at the specified rate, points, and terms — provided the loan closes before the lock expiration date and the loan characteristics match what was locked.

### What IS locked:
| Element | Notes |
|---|---|
| Interest rate | The note rate on the loan |
| Points | Discount points and/or origination points quoted at time of lock |
| PPP structure | Prepayment penalty structure (5/4/3/2/1, 3/2/1, no-PPP) |
| Loan amount | Changes during underwriting may require a re-lock |

### What is NOT locked:
| Element | Notes |
|---|---|
| Non-lender closing costs | Title, taxes, recording fees |
| Third-party fees | Appraisal, credit report, flood cert |
| Rate if material characteristics change | LTV change (low appraisal), DSCR change (low 1007 rent) |

### Key Mechanism: Best-Efforts vs. Mandatory Delivery

- **Best-efforts delivery** (most common for DSCR): The lender commits to delivering the locked loan to their secondary-market pipeline. If the loan doesn't close, the lender eats the hedging cost. This is why extensions have a price — the lender's hedge is expiring.
- **Mandatory delivery**: The broker/lender guarantees delivery of a certain volume of locked loans. Miss the window and penalties are severe. Rare for individual DSCR borrowers; more common for high-volume broker channels.

## 2. Typical Lock Periods

**Industry standard DSCR lock periods and pricing** (verified from DSCR Authority, May 2026):

| Lock Period | Rate Premium vs. 30-Day | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| **15 days** | -0.00 to -0.125% | Existing files already in underwriting; very fast closes |
| **30 days** | **Baseline** | Clean files, established lender relationships, urban properties |
| **45 days** | +0.00 to +0.125% | **Standard — the most common choice for purchases** |
| **60 days** | +0.125–0.25% | Complex files, rural appraisals, new entity formation, known delays |
| **90 days** | +0.25–0.50% | Long lead time, new construction, out-of-state investors |

### The 45-Day Reality Check

DSCR close timelines average **30–45 days**. If you lock for 30 days and:
- The appraisal takes 12 days instead of 7
- The inspector finds an issue requiring a 1004D
- Conditions take 3 rounds instead of 2

…you are paying for an extension. **Budget for 45 days on any non-trivial file.**

## 3. Lock Deposit / Upfront Cost

### General Rule: No Upfront Deposit on Standard Locks

Most DSCR rate locks have **no upfront fee** — the lock cost is built into the rate or points. This is different from some conventional lenders that charge a non-refundable lock deposit.

### Exceptions:
| Scenario | Upfront Cost | Refundable? |
|---|---|---|
| Standard 30- or 45-day lock | **$0** — built into pricing | N/A |
| 60-day lock (some lenders) | 0.125–0.25 points | Credited back at closing |
| 90-day lock (most lenders) | 0.25–0.50 points | Credited back at closing; forfeited if loan doesn't close |
| Float-down option | 0.125–0.25 points | Non-refundable option premium |

### Why No Deposit?

DSCR lenders operate on a **best-efforts** delivery model. The lender absorbs the hedging cost internally and prices it into the rate. This differs from some agency lenders that require a $500–$1,500 non-refundable lock deposit to ensure borrower commitment. DSCR lenders rely on the borrower's signed lock agreement and the property under way as commitment enough.

## 4. Lock Extension Costs

When the close does not happen before the lock expiration, the borrower has three options:

### Option 1: Extend the Lock (Most Common)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| **Typical extension cost** | **0.125–0.25% per 15 days** |
| **On a $350,000 loan** | **$437.50 – $875 per 15-day extension** |
| **Cumulative** | Yes — three 15-day extensions = paid three times |
| **Typical extension period** | 15 or 30 days |
| **Maximum extensions** | Varies by lender; typically 2–3 extensions max |

### Extension Cost Examples (at 0.25% per 15 days):

| Loan Amount | 15-Day Extension | 30-Day Extension |
|---|---|---|
| $200,000 | $500 | $1,000 |
| $350,000 | $875 | $1,750 |
| $500,000 | $1,250 | $2,500 |
| $750,000 | $1,875 | $3,750 |
| $1,000,000 | $2,500 | $5,000 |

### Option 2: Re-Lock at Market

- If rates have **fallen** since your original lock → this is better than paying extension fees
- If rates have **risen** since your original lock → this is painful — you lose the locked rate
- The re-lock is treated as a new lock with a new expiration

### Option 3: Seek a New Lender

- If the original lender is causing the delay (slow underwriting, lost documents)
- Resets the entire timeline (new appraisal, new application)
- Usually a **last resort** — costs more time and money

### Managing Extension Cost Exposure:

1. **Order insurance before week 2** — don't wait for the lender to ask
2. **Respond to conditions within 24 hours** — most delays are borrower-caused
3. **Pre-gather entity documents** before applying
4. **Know the appraisal queue time** in your specific market and lock accordingly
5. **If the delay is lender-caused, negotiate** — some lenders will share extension costs

## 5. Float-Down Options

Some DSCR lenders offer a **float-down clause** — a contractual option to re-lock at a lower rate if market rates fall during the lock period by more than a specified threshold.

### Typical Float-Down Terms:

| Parameter | Typical Range |
|---|---|
| **Upfront cost** | 0.125–0.25 points at time of lock |
| **Minimum improvement threshold** | 0.25–0.50% (rates must fall by this much to trigger) |
| **Frequency** | Typically **one** re-lock opportunity |
| **Window** | Must occur within the last 10–15 days of the lock period |
| **New rate** | Current market rate at time of exercise (not locked rate minus threshold) |

### Float-Down Math Example:

```
$350,000 loan. Lock at 7.00% on a 45-day lock.
Float-down costs 0.125 points = $437.50 upfront.
Trigger: rates must fall 0.375% to exercise.

If rates fall to 6.625% (0.375% below lock):
  Monthly P&I at 7.00%:  $2,328
  Monthly P&I at 6.625%: $2,241
  Monthly savings:         $87
  Break-even on $437.50:   5 months

If rates fall by less than 0.375%, the float-down does NOT trigger.
The $437.50 was an option premium you did not exercise — essentially a sunk cost.
```

### When Float-Down Is Worth It:

- When locking at rates near **cycle highs** with reasonable basis to believe rates may fall (Fed signaling cuts, trend data)
- NOT as a hedge against your own indecision
- NOT in a stable or rising rate environment

### Lender-by-Lender Float-Down Availability:

| Lender | Float-Down? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Kiavi | **No** (standard) | No float-down on standard DSCR product |
| Angel Oak | **Case-by-case** | Available on select programs with LO approval |
| Griffin Funding | **Limited** | Available on some ARM products; rare on fixed-rate DSCR |
| Lima One Capital | **No** | Standard best-efforts lock only |
| Easy Street Capital | **No** | No float-down on core programs |
| Ridge Street Capital | **No** | Daily rate sheets; re-lock at market if beneficial |
| Visio Lending | **No** | Standard lock; no float-down |
| LendSure | **Case-by-case** | Available on some wholesale channel products |
| Defy Mortgage | **Yes** (limited) | Offers float-down on 45+ day locks with 0.25-point premium |

## 6. When to Lock — Timing Best Practices

### The Baseline Reality

**DSCR rates move daily.** The mortgage market has no memory — last week's rates do not predict this week's. Most investors should **lock promptly** when they receive a quote they can make their deal work at, rather than speculating on rate direction.

### When to Lock Immediately:

| Scenario | Reason |
|---|---|
| Purchase transaction with hard close date | Missing close = breach of contract, earnest money at risk |
| Rate within 0.25% of your deal's break-even | Small rate improvement doesn't justify risk |
| First-time DSCR investor | You lack experience reading rate trends |
| Rates near historical lows | Regression to mean risk is asymmetric (rates rise faster than they fall) |
| Loan near a DSCR/FICO/LTV tier boundary | A small rate increase could push you into a worse pricing tier |

### When Floating Might Make Sense:

| Scenario | Reason |
|---|---|
| Fed has signaled rate cuts and short-term rates declining | Fundamental tailwind for lower rates |
| 10-Year Treasury falling consistently for 3+ weeks | Trend confirmation |
| 45–60 day close timeline with significant rate volatility compression | Time buffer exists |
| Rate-and-term refinance (no purchase deadline) | Timeline flexibility |
| Cash-out refinance still in early stages | No external deadline pressure |

### When Floating Rarely Makes Sense:

- You have a purchase contract with a hard close date
- Rates are near historical lows
- You are a first-time investor without rate-reading experience
- The rate difference between current market and your break-even is less than 0.25%

### The Honest Assessment

> Professional mortgage traders with full-time access to markets fail to consistently predict 30-day rate direction. For most borrowers, **locking when you have a rate that works for your deal economics** is the right decision — the insurance value of certainty outweighs the speculative upside of floating.

## 7. What Happens When a Lock Expires

### Timeline of Expiration Events:

```
Day 0:     Lock expires
           ↓
Day 1-3:   Lender issues "lock expired" notice
           ↓
Option A:  Borrower requests extension (0.125-0.25% per 15 days)
           → New lock expiration date set
           → Same rate and terms maintained
           
Option B:  Borrower re-locks at current market rates
           → If rates rose: borrower pays higher rate
           → If rates fell: borrower benefits from lower rate
           → New lock period begins (30 or 45 days)
           
Option C:  Borrower walks away
           → No penalty for not closing (in most states)
           → Appraisal fee lost ($500-$800)
           → Any upfront lock deposit forfeited
           → For purchases: potential breach of contract with seller
```

### Key Implications:

1. **For purchases**: An expired lock can jeopardize the purchase contract. The seller may issue a "notice to perform" and potentially keep earnest money if you cannot close.

2. **For refinances**: Less catastrophic — you control the timeline. Re-lock at market or extend.

3. **Lender repricing**: Once a lock expires, the lender must re-price the loan at current market. The original rate is gone — it cannot be "held" informally.

4. **No grace period**: Most lenders offer **zero days** of grace after lock expiration. The lock expires at 11:59 PM on the expiration date.

## 8. Optimal Day / Timing for Locking

### Day-of-Week Pattern:

| Day | Rate Tendency | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | Slightly lower | Weekend economic data often benign; less volatility |
| **Tuesday–Thursday** | Variable | Driven by economic data releases (jobs, CPI, GDP) |
| **Friday** | Slightly higher | Lenders widen spreads before weekend to hedge uncertainty |

### Monthly Pattern:

| Timing | Rate Tendency | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Early month** | Slightly better | New month = fresh lender quotas; some pricing flexibility |
| **Mid-month** | Neutral | Standard market-driven pricing |
| **End of month** | Potentially better | Lenders trying to hit volume targets may sharpen pricing |

### Macro Event Calendar — Lock Around These:

| Event | Impact | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **Fed meeting (8x/year)** | High — rates can move 0.125-0.375% on decision day | Lock BEFORE the meeting if rates are acceptable |
| **Jobs report (1st Friday)** | High — NFP can move rates 0.125-0.25% | Lock early in the week before Friday release |
| **CPI report (mid-month)** | High — inflation data drives bond yields | Lock BEFORE CPI if possible |
| **Treasury auctions** | Moderate — supply impacts MBS spreads | Less critical but worth monitoring |

### The Golden Rule of Lock Timing:

**Lock when your deal works, not when you think rates will improve.** A 0.125% rate difference on a $350,000 loan is $29/month. That's not worth risking a blown purchase deadline or a 0.50% rate spike.

## 9. What Breaks a Rate Lock

A rate lock breaks — meaning it must be re-priced — when a **material loan characteristic** changes between lock and closing.

### Changes That Break a Lock:

| Change | How It Happens | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Loan amount changes** | Appraisal comes in different → different LTV | Re-price at new LTV tier; some lenders allow 5% variance |
| **DSCR changes** | 1007 market rent comes in lower → different DSCR tier | Re-price at new DSCR tier |
| **Property type changes** | Locked on SFR; underwriting discovers it's a duplex | Re-price for correct property type |
| **Vesting changes** | Locked personal name; changed to LLC after lock | Re-lock required; may affect pricing |
| **Borrower changes** | Adding/removing co-borrower or guarantor | Full re-underwrite; re-lock required |

### Changes That Typically Do NOT Break a Lock:

| Change | Notes |
|---|---|
| Appraisal within expected range | Value supports locked LTV |
| Minor rate-sheet repricing within a single tier | Same pricing tier maintained |
| Closing date change within lock period | No impact if within lock window |

### Proactive Management:

If any material change occurs, **notify your loan officer immediately**. Surprises discovered at CTC (clear-to-close) trigger last-minute repricing, which is far more disruptive than handling the conversation proactively during underwriting.

## 10. Purchase vs. Refinance Lock Strategy

### Purchase Transaction

| Factor | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Lock period** | 45 days minimum; 60 days for rural/slow-appraisal markets |
| **When to lock** | At application — don't float with a hard close date |
| **Risk of expiration** | High — breach of contract, lost earnest money |
| **Extension strategy** | Budget for one potential extension; factor into deal math |

### Cash-Out Refinance

| Factor | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Lock period** | 30–45 days is usually sufficient |
| **When to lock** | Float for 2–3 weeks while appraisal is ordered; lock once appraisal confirms DSCR/LTV |
| **Risk of expiration** | Low — no external deadline |
| **Extension strategy** | Extend only if rates have risen; otherwise re-lock at market |

### Rate-and-Term Refinance

| Factor | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Lock period** | 30 days often sufficient for clean files |
| **When to lock** | Float until rate improvement justifies costs, then lock |
| **Risk of expiration** | Minimal — you control the timeline entirely |
| **Extension strategy** | Rarely needed; can walk away and re-apply if rates move unfavorably |

## 13. Common Myths Debunked

### Myth 1: "My rate is locked, so nothing can change."
**Reality:** Your rate is locked assuming the loan closes as quoted. If the appraisal comes in low (changing LTV), the 1007 rent comes in low (changing DSCR tier), or title issues delay the close beyond the lock expiration, your rate can change.

### Myth 2: "I should wait until I have everything ready to lock."
**Reality:** If you wait until the appraisal is in and conditions are cleared to lock, you have already been floating for 3 weeks and the market moved while you were waiting. Most borrowers lock at application, which is the right strategy for purchases.

### Myth 3: "The lender controls the rate movement — if rates go up, it's their fault."
**Reality:** DSCR lenders set rates daily based on the secondary market. They do not control rate movements. The lender is not your adversary in rate-lock strategy — they want the loan to close as much as you do.

### Myth 4: "A longer lock is always safer."
**Reality:** Longer locks cost more (0.00–0.50% premium depending on length). If you pay 0.25% more for a 60-day lock when a 45-day lock would have been sufficient, you paid for protection you did not need. Match the lock to the realistic close timeline.

### Myth 5: "I can cancel my lock and re-lock at a better rate anytime."
**Reality:** Most lenders do not allow you to cancel a lock and re-lock with them at a lower rate. You would need to let the lock expire or switch lenders entirely. Some lenders have "worst-case" pricing — if you re-lock after expiration, you get the higher of your original rate or current market.

## 14. Actionable Checklist

### Before Locking:
- [ ] Confirm the quote works for your deal economics (cash flow, break-even)
- [ ] Verify the lock period matches your realistic close timeline (budget for 45 days minimum on purchases)
- [ ] Ask about float-down availability and cost
- [ ] Get the lock confirmation in writing (email or document with rate, points, PPP, lock expiration)
- [ ] Check the economic calendar — avoid locking on days with major data releases (Fed, NFP, CPI)

### During the Lock Period:
- [ ] Order insurance within the first week
- [ ] Respond to all conditions within 24 hours
- [ ] Track the lock expiration date — don't let it sneak up on you
- [ ] If rates fall significantly, inquire about float-down or re-lock options
- [ ] If delays occur, notify your LO immediately — don't wait until the lock expires

### If an Extension Is Needed:
- [ ] Confirm extension cost in writing before agreeing
- [ ] Get a new lock expiration date documented
- [ ] If the delay is lender-caused, negotiate cost-sharing
- [ ] Consider whether re-locking at market might be better (if rates have fallen)
- [ ] Only extend once — if you need a second extension, there's a systemic problem

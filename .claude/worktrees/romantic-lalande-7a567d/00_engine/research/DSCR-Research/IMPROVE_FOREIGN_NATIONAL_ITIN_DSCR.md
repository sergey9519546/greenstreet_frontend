## 1. MARKET OVERVIEW

Foreign nationals represent one of the fastest-growing borrower segments in DSCR lending. The core value proposition is simple: **DSCR loans qualify on property cash flow, not personal income or U.S. credit history** — making them uniquely suited for international investors who lack W-2 income, SSNs, or FICO scores.

### Key Market Drivers
- **$67B+** in foreign investment in U.S. residential real estate annually (NAR data)
- **LATAM, Europe, and Asia** are primary source regions for foreign DSCR borrowers
- **DSCR eliminates the #1 barrier**: U.S. income verification and credit history
- **Digital-first lenders** (Waltz, Milo) are purpose-built for this segment
- **Traditional lenders reject** most foreign nationals due to lack of FICO/SSN/W-2

### Market Size Estimate
- Approximately **10-15% of all DSCR originations** involve foreign national or ITIN borrowers
- Foreign national DSCR volume estimated at **$8-12B annually**
- Average foreign national DSCR loan size: **$250K-$400K**

## 3. ITIN BORROWER DSCR

### 3.1 What is an ITIN?

An **Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN)** is a tax processing number issued by the IRS to individuals who are required to have a U.S. taxpayer identification number but are not eligible for a Social Security Number (SSN). ITINs are issued regardless of immigration status.

- **Format:** 9-digit number starting with "9" (e.g., 9XX-XX-XXXX)
- **Purpose:** Federal tax reporting and filing
- **Eligibility:** Non-resident aliens, resident aliens, spouses/dependents of visa holders
- **NOT proof of:** Work authorization, immigration status, or eligibility for Social Security

### 3.2 Can ITIN Holders Get DSCR Loans?

**Yes.** ITIN holders can qualify for DSCR loans from select lenders. This is a growing but still niche segment within the non-QM market.

**Key distinction:** An ITIN is NOT the same as a foreign national. ITIN holders may:
- Reside in the U.S. legally (visa holders, residents)
- Have U.S. bank accounts and credit history
- File U.S. tax returns using their ITIN
- Have established rental property portfolios

### 3.3 Which Lenders Accept ITIN?

| Lender | ITIN Accepted? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **LendSure** | Yes | ITIN accepted in lieu of SSN for foreign national program |
| **Waltz** | Yes | Accepts ITIN; also accepts foreign passport without SSN |
| **Milo** | Yes | Accepts ITIN for DSCR and crypto-backed mortgages |
| **DSCR Authority** | Yes (matches) | Has ITIN-specific lender matching |
| **Select wholesale lenders** | Varies | Some non-QM wholesale lenders accept ITIN |

### 3.4 How Does the ITIN DSCR Process Differ from SSN?

| Step | SSN Borrower | ITIN Borrower |
|---|---|---|
| **Credit Check** | Tri-merge FICO pull | Foreign credit report OR alternative credit (rent payments, utility bills) |
| **Identification** | SSN + Driver's License | ITIN + Passport + Visa (if applicable) |
| **Entity Formation** | LLC with EIN | LLC with EIN (same process — EIN does not require SSN) |
| **Bank Account** | Standard | May require ITIN or foreign passport; some banks accept ITIN for business accounts |
| **Reserves** | 3-6 months | 6-12 months (more conservative) |
| **Rate Premium** | Base rate | +0.50% to +1.00% over SSN DSCR rates |
| **LTV** | Up to 80% | Up to 75% (some lenders cap at 70%) |

### 3.5 Credit History Requirements for ITIN Borrowers

- **No U.S. FICO required** by most DSCR lenders (property income-based qualification)
- **Foreign credit reports accepted** by LendSure and others (3 active trade lines, 2-year history)
- **Alternative credit** may be accepted: rent payment history, utility bills, bank account history
- **Foreign-based checking and savings accounts** may count as trade lines (LendSure)
- **Credit letters from home country** lenders/banks accepted
- **If ITIN holder has established U.S. credit**, they may qualify at better rates/LTVs

## 4. ENTITY REQUIREMENTS FOR FOREIGN INVESTORS

### 4.1 Why an Entity is Required

**DSCR loans for foreign nationals are almost always required to close in a U.S.-based LLC.** This is because:

1. **Business-purpose loan classification** — DSCR loans are business-purpose loans on non-owner-occupied investment properties
2. **Asset protection** — LLC separates personal assets from property liabilities
3. **Lender requirement** — Most DSCR lenders require entity vesting for foreign nationals
4. **Tax efficiency** — Pass-through taxation and depreciation benefits
5. **Portfolio scalability** — Multiple properties easier to manage under entity structure
6. **Privacy** — Owner's name kept off public records

> **~99% of DSCR loans close in an entity** — most in a single-member LLC (DSCR Authority)

### 4.2 Best Entity Structure for Foreign Investors

| Entity Type | Recommendation | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **US Single-Member LLC** | **BEST OPTION** | Pass-through taxation, liability protection, simple, accepted by all DSCR lenders | Must be U.S.-registered |
| **US Multi-Member LLC** | Good for partners | Same as SMLLC + partnership flexibility | More complex operating agreement |
| **US C-Corp** | NOT recommended | Limited use cases | Double taxation, FIRPTA implications on stock sale |
| **Foreign LLC/Corp** | NOT accepted by most lenders | Home country familiarity | Most DSCR lenders require U.S.-domiciled entity |
| **S-Corp election** | NOT for rental property | Creates unnecessary payroll complexity | Converts tax-advantaged rental income to wage income |

### 4.3 State Selection for LLC Formation

| State | Advantages | Common For |
|---|---|---|
| **Delaware** | Business-friendly law, Court of Chancery, privacy, no state income tax for non-residents | Most popular for foreign nationals |
| **Wyoming** | Lowest fees, strong privacy, no state income tax, charging order protection | Asset protection focus |
| **Nevada** | No state income tax, strong privacy, no franchise tax | Privacy-focused investors |
| **Property state** | Simpler — no foreign entity registration needed | Investors with single-state portfolio |

**Key consideration:** If your LLC is formed in a different state than the property, you need **Foreign Entity Registration** in the property's state. For example, a Delaware LLC purchasing in Florida must register as a foreign entity in Florida.

### 4.4 Can a Foreign Entity Directly Own Property?

**Technically yes, but practically problematic for DSCR lending:**

- **Most DSCR lenders will NOT lend to a foreign entity** (e.g., UK Ltd, SA de CV, GmbH)
- **U.S.-registered entity required** — the entity must be domiciled in the U.S. with a domestic registered agent
- **LendSure:** "LLCs or corporations must be domiciled in the U.S. with domestic agent for service of process"
- **Waltz:** Requires U.S.-based business entity (LLC); provides Investor Kit for formation
- **Foreign parent ownership of U.S. LLC is allowed** — the U.S. LLC can be wholly owned by a foreign person or entity

### 4.5 Entity Documentation Required

| Document | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Articles of Organization/Formation | Proves entity exists legally | Filed with state |
| LLC Operating Agreement | Identifies members, ownership %, borrowing authority | **Most scrutinized document** — must explicitly authorize mortgage debt |
| EIN Confirmation Letter | Entity's tax ID (equivalent of SSN for business) | Free from IRS, obtained online in minutes |
| Certificate of Good Standing | Confirms entity is active and compliant | Required if LLC registered >1 year; must be dated within 90 days of closing |
| Borrowing Resolution | Written authorization for specific loan | Some lenders require separate document; others accept operating agreement language |
| Foreign Entity Registration | If LLC formed in different state than property | Required for multi-state operations |

### 4.6 Tax Implications of Entity Structure

#### For Foreign Investors Owning Through U.S. LLC:

1. **Income Tax:** 
   - Single-member LLC = "disregarded entity" for tax purposes
   - Rental income flows to owner's personal return (Form 1040-NR for non-residents)
   - Effectively Connected Income (ECI) subject to graduated U.S. tax rates
   - Can deduct depreciation, property taxes, mortgage interest, operating expenses

2. **Estate Tax:**
   - Non-resident alien estate tax exemption: **only $60,000** (vs. $13.99M for U.S. citizens)
   - Above $60K, estate tax rate climbs to **40%**
   - **LLC ownership can help** — owning LLC interests (vs. direct real estate) may change situs analysis

3. **FIRPTA:** See Section 6 below

4. **FDIIPT (Foreign-Domestic International Investment Property Tax):**
   - Not a separate tax — refers to the combined impact of FIRPTA + income tax + estate tax on foreign-held U.S. real property

### 5.3 Credit History Alternatives

Since many foreign nationals lack U.S. FICO scores, lenders accept:

1. **Foreign Credit Reports** — From credit bureaus in home country
2. **Credit Letters** — From banks or lenders in home country documenting payment history
3. **Trade Line Verification** — 3 active trade lines with 2-year history
4. **Bank Account History** — Foreign-based checking and savings may count as trade lines (LendSure)
5. **No Credit Required** — Waltz explicitly states "No FICO credit requirement for foreign nationals"

### 5.4 Are Foreign Appraisals Accepted?

**No.** U.S. appraisals are always required. Lenders order appraisals through U.S.-licensed appraisers in compliance with USPAP and lender guidelines. Foreign property valuations are not relevant to U.S. DSCR lending.

### 5.5 How is Income Verified?

**For DSCR loans, personal income is NOT verified.** The qualification is based entirely on the property's rental income. However:

- **Source of funds** IS verified (for AML/KYC compliance)
- Lenders review bank statements to confirm the borrower has sufficient funds for down payment and reserves
- **Waltz requires:** Financial statements, bank statements, income source information, and other relevant paperwork
- **LendSure requires:** No foreign income documentation or tax returns for DSCR program; closing funds must be in U.S.-domiciled account

## 6. FIRPTA & TAX WITHHOLDING

### 6.1 What is FIRPTA?

The **Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act of 1980 (FIRPTA)** is a federal tax law that ensures foreign persons pay U.S. tax on gains from the sale of U.S. real property interests (USRPIs).

- **IRC Sections:** §897 and §1445
- **Purpose:** Prevent non-U.S. persons from avoiding U.S. capital gains on the sale of U.S. real property
- **Mechanism:** Makes the **buyer** the withholding agent, with personal liability if withholding is not done correctly

### 6.2 How FIRPTA Works

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Default withholding rate** | **15% of the gross sale price** (not the gain) |
| **Applies to** | Dispositions of U.S. Real Property Interests (USRPIs) by foreign persons |
| **Who withholds** | The buyer (withholding agent) |
| **When** | At closing |
| **What is a USRPI** | Direct ownership in U.S. real estate, or interests in U.S. Real Property Holding Companies (USRPHCs) |
| **USRPHC definition** | U.S. corporation where fair market value of USRPIs ≥ 50% of worldwide assets |

**Critical point:** FIRPTA withholding is computed on the **gross sale price**, not the seller's gain. This means even if the seller has no gain or a small gain, 15% of the entire sale price is withheld.

### 6.3 Combined Withholding Impact

| Jurisdiction | Withholding Rate | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Federal (FIRPTA)** | 15% | Gross sale price |
| **New York State** | +10.9% | Non-resident tax on gain |
| **Combined (NY example)** | ~26% | Of sale proceeds tied up at closing |
| **California** | Additional state withholding | Varies |
| **Other states** | Varies | Some have additional non-resident withholding |

> "FIRPTA can tie up roughly 26% of sale proceeds at closing" — Dilendorf Law Firm (NY example)

### 6.4 FIRPTA Exceptions and Exemptions

| Exception | Details | Relevance to DSCR |
|---|---|---|
| **Personal Residence Exemption** | No withholding if sale price ≤ $300,000 AND buyer intends to use as personal residence for ≥50% of occupancy in first 2 years | **NOT applicable** — DSCR properties are investment properties |
| **Reduced Rate (10%)** | Sale between $300,001 and $1,000,000 where buyer uses as personal residence | **NOT applicable** — investment properties |
| **Withholding Certificate** | IRS can issue certificate reducing or eliminating withholding based on actual tax liability | **YES — primary strategy for DSCR investors** |
| **Non-Foreign Status Affidavit** | If seller can prove U.S. citizenship or residency (Green Card, Substantial Presence Test) | **YES — if foreign national becomes U.S. resident** |
| **U.S. Corporation Certification** | Corporation can certify it is not a USRPHC | **Complex — C-Corp structure has FIRPTA implications** |
| **Publicly Traded Stock/Partnership** | Exemption for publicly traded entities | **Not applicable** for typical DSCR investor |

### 6.5 Withholding Certificate Strategy

The **most important FIRPTA planning tool** for DSCR investors:

1. **Apply to IRS** for a withholding certificate before or at closing (Form 8288-B)
2. **IRS determines** the maximum tax liability and issues certificate with reduced (or zero) withholding amount
3. **Processing time:** Was guaranteed 90 days pre-COVID; no longer guaranteed — **apply early**
4. **Benefit:** Can reduce 15% gross withholding to the actual tax owed on the gain
5. **Example:** If selling a $500K property with only $50K gain, withholding certificate could reduce withholding from $75K (15% of $500K) to ~$10K-$15K (tax on $50K gain)

### 6.6 How FIRPTA Affects Refinance Strategy

| Scenario | FIRPTA Impact | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **Rate & Term Refinance** | No FIRPTA withholding (not a disposition) | Refinance freely — no FIRPTA concern |
| **Cash-Out Refinance** | No FIRPTA withholding (not a disposition) | Cash-out proceeds are not subject to FIRPTA |
| **Property Sale** | 15% withholding on gross sale price | Apply for withholding certificate early; structure through U.S. LLC |
| **Entity Restructuring** | May trigger FIRPTA if it constitutes a disposition | Consult tax advisor before restructuring |
| **1031 Exchange** | May defer FIRPTA if exchange qualifies | Foreign persons CAN do 1031 exchanges for U.S. property |

### 6.7 Can FIRPTA Be Avoided or Reduced?

**FIRPTA cannot be entirely avoided**, but withholding can be reduced or eliminated with proper planning:

1. **Pre-investment structuring** — Entity structure decisions made before acquisition determine FIRPTA exposure
2. **Withholding certificate** — Apply to IRS to reduce withholding to actual tax liability
3. **Become U.S. tax resident** — Green Card or Substantial Presence Test eliminates FIRPTA
4. **Treaty benefits** — Some tax treaties may reduce U.S. tax on real property gains
5. **U.S. LLC structure** — While LLC interests can be USRPIs, proper structuring may provide planning opportunities
6. **Installment sales** — May spread gain recognition and withholding over time

> "FIRPTA cannot be avoided, but withholding can be reduced or eliminated with proper planning before the investment is made" — Dilendorf Law Firm

### 6.8 Three Tax Risks for Foreign DSCR Investors

1. **Income Tax** — Rental income and capital gains taxed at graduated rates on net ECI
2. **Estate Tax** — Only $60,000 exemption for non-resident aliens (vs. $13.99M for citizens); 40% rate above threshold
3. **FIRPTA Withholding** — 15% of gross sale price at disposition

## 7. COUNTRY-SPECIFIC RESTRICTIONS (OFAC, AML)

### 7.1 OFAC Sanctions

The **Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC)** administers U.S. economic sanctions programs. All U.S. lenders are required to screen borrowers against OFAC lists.

**Key OFAC lists relevant to DSCR lending:**
- **SDN List (Specially Designated Nationals)** — Individuals and entities blocked from U.S. transactions
- **Sectoral Sanctions** — Restrictions on specific sectors in certain countries
- **Country-based sanctions** — Comprehensive or targeted sanctions on specific nations

### 7.2 Comprehensively Sanctioned Countries

Lending to individuals from these countries is **prohibited or severely restricted:**

| Country | Sanction Level | DSCR Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Russia** | Extensive sanctions (2022+) | Highly restricted; individual screening required |
| **Iran** | Comprehensive sanctions | Virtually impossible to lend |
| **North Korea (DPRK)** | Comprehensive sanctions | Prohibited |
| **Syria** | Comprehensive sanctions | Prohibited |
| **Cuba** | Comprehensive sanctions | Prohibited |
| **Myanmar** | Targeted sanctions | Highly restricted |
| **Venezuela** | Sectoral sanctions | Government-affiliated individuals blocked |

### 7.3 DSCR Lender Country Restrictions

Beyond OFAC, individual DSCR lenders may have their own country restrictions:

- **No universal "preferred countries" list** — varies by lender
- **Common restricted countries:** Those under OFAC sanctions, high-risk AML jurisdictions
- **Common preferred countries:** EU members, UK, Canada, Australia, Japan, South Korea, UAE, Brazil, Mexico
- **LATAM focus:** Waltz specifically targets Latin American investors (Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, Argentina)
- **Lender risk tolerance varies:** Some lenders accept any non-sanctioned country; others maintain "approved" lists

### 7.4 Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Requirements for DSCR

AML compliance is a **significant factor** in foreign national DSCR lending:

| Requirement | Impact on Foreign National DSCR |
|---|---|
| **KYC (Know Your Customer)** | Full identity verification required — passport, address, source of wealth |
| **Source of Funds Verification** | Lenders must verify where down payment and closing costs originated |
| **Beneficial Ownership** | LLC members must be disclosed; no anonymous ownership |
| **Currency Transaction Reporting** | Transactions >$10,000 in currency must be reported |
| **Suspicious Activity Reporting** | Lenders file SARs for suspicious transactions |
| **Patriot Act Compliance** | All lenders required to have CIP (Customer Identification Program) |
| **FinCEN Beneficial Ownership Rule** | 25%+ owners must be identified |

### 7.5 Practical AML Implications

- **Wire transfers from foreign accounts** are subject to enhanced scrutiny
- **Large cash deposits** are red flags — lenders prefer documented wire transfers
- **Source of funds trail** must be clear and documentable
- **Crypto-originated funds** are increasingly accepted (LendSure accepts crypto for reserves) but carry additional documentation requirements
- **Multiple entity layers** (foreign parent → U.S. LLC → property) may trigger enhanced due diligence

### 10.2 Rate Adjuster Logic

```typescript
function calculateForeignNationalRateAdjusters(profile: ForeignNationalProfile): RateAdjuster[] {
  const adjusters: RateAdjuster[] = [];
  
  // Foreign national baseline premium
  if (profile.idType !== 'SSN') {
    adjusters.push({
      name: 'FOREIGN_NATIONAL_PREMIUM',
      basisPoints: profile.idType === 'ITIN' ? 75 : 125, // 0.75% for ITIN, 1.25% for passport-only
      reason: 'Non-SSN borrower risk premium'
    });
  }
  
  // No U.S. credit
  if (!profile.hasUsFico) {
    adjusters.push({
      name: 'NO_US_CREDIT',
      basisPoints: 50, // 0.50%
      reason: 'No U.S. FICO score available'
    });
  }
  
  // No visa
  if (!profile.visaType && !profile.hasUsResidency) {
    adjusters.push({
      name: 'NO_VISA',
      basisPoints: 50, // 0.50%
      reason: 'No U.S. visa — investor-only program'
    });
  }
  
  // Country risk tier
  const countryRiskPremiums: Record<string, number> = {
    'PREFERRED': 0,
    'STANDARD': 25,  // 0.25%
    'ELEVATED': 75,  // 0.75%
    'RESTRICTED': 200, // 2.00%
    'PROHIBITED': Infinity // Cannot lend
  };
  adjusters.push({
    name: 'COUNTRY_RISK',
    basisPoints: countryRiskPremiums[profile.countryRiskTier],
    reason: `Country risk tier: ${profile.countryRiskTier}`
  });
  
  // Foreign entity (not US LLC)
  if (profile.entityType === 'FOREIGN_ENTITY') {
    adjusters.push({
      name: 'FOREIGN_ENTITY_PREMIUM',
      basisPoints: 100, // 1.00%
      reason: 'Non-U.S. entity borrower'
    });
  }
  
  return adjusters;
}
```

### 10.3 LTV Cap Logic

```typescript
function calculateForeignNationalMaxLTV(profile: ForeignNationalProfile): {
  purchase: number;
  rateAndTerm: number;
  cashOut: number;
} {
  let baseLTV = { purchase: 80, rateAndTerm: 80, cashOut: 75 }; // Domestic defaults
  
  // Adjust for borrower type
  if (profile.idType === 'ITIN') {
    baseLTV = { purchase: 75, rateAndTerm: 75, cashOut: 70 };
  } else if (profile.idType === 'PASSPORT_ONLY') {
    if (profile.visaType) {
      baseLTV = { purchase: 75, rateAndTerm: 75, cashOut: 70 };
    } else {
      baseLTV = { purchase: 70, rateAndTerm: 70, cashOut: 60 }; // No visa = lower LTV
    }
  }
  
  // Further reduce for foreign entity
  if (profile.entityType === 'FOREIGN_ENTITY') {
    baseLTV = { 
      purchase: Math.min(baseLTV.purchase, 70), 
      rateAndTerm: Math.min(baseLTV.rateAndTerm, 70), 
      cashOut: Math.min(baseLTV.cashOut, 60) 
    };
  }
  
  // Further reduce for elevated country risk
  if (profile.countryRiskTier === 'ELEVATED') {
    baseLTV = { 
      purchase: baseLTV.purchase - 5, 
      rateAndTerm: baseLTV.rateAndTerm - 5, 
      cashOut: baseLTV.cashOut - 5 
    };
  }
  
  return baseLTV;
}
```

### 10.6 FIRPTA Impact Calculator

```typescript
interface FIRPTACalculation {
  salePrice: number;
  adjustedBasis: number;
  gain: number;
  federalWithholdingRate: number; // 15% default
  stateWithholdingRate: number; // varies by state
  federalWithholdingAmount: number;
  stateWithholdingAmount: number;
  totalWithholding: number;
  estimatedTaxOnGain: number;
  potentialRefund: number;
  withholdingCertificateRecommended: boolean;
}

function calculateFIRPTAImpact(
  salePrice: number,
  adjustedBasis: number,
  state: string,
  isUsResident: boolean
): FIRPTACalculation {
  if (isUsResident) {
    return {
      salePrice, adjustedBasis,
      gain: salePrice - adjustedBasis,
      federalWithholdingRate: 0,
      stateWithholdingRate: 0,
      federalWithholdingAmount: 0,
      stateWithholdingAmount: 0,
      totalWithholding: 0,
      estimatedTaxOnGain: 0,
      potentialRefund: 0,
      withholdingCertificateRecommended: false
    };
  }
  
  const gain = salePrice - adjustedBasis;
  const federalWithholding = salePrice * 0.15; // 15% of GROSS
  
  // State withholding varies
  const stateRates: Record<string, number> = {
    'NY': 0.109, // 10.9% non-resident
    'CA': 0.033, // 3.3% non-resident
    'HI': 0.05,  // 5%
    // Most other states: 0% or minimal
  };
  const stateRate = stateRates[state] || 0;
  const stateWithholding = gain * stateRate; // State usually withholds on gain, not gross
  
  const estimatedTaxRate = 0.20; // 20% capital gains + 3.8% NIIT ≈ ~24%, simplified to 20%
  const estimatedTax = gain * estimatedTaxRate;
  
  return {
    salePrice,
    adjustedBasis,
    gain,
    federalWithholdingRate: 0.15,
    stateWithholdingRate: stateRate,
    federalWithholdingAmount: federalWithholding,
    stateWithholdingAmount: stateWithholding,
    totalWithholding: federalWithholding + stateWithholding,
    estimatedTaxOnGain: estimatedTax,
    potentialRefund: Math.max(0, federalWithholding + stateWithholding - estimatedTax),
    withholdingCertificateRecommended: federalWithholding > estimatedTax * 1.5
  };
}
```

## APPENDIX C: KEY TAKEAWAYS FOR PLATFORM

1. **Foreign national DSCR is a massive growth segment** — build it right and capture market share
2. **Entity formation is the #1 friction point** — integrate LLC setup (like Waltz Investor Kit)

4. **OFAC/country screening must be automated** — cannot originate loans to sanctioned persons

8. **Source of funds verification is mandatory** — AML compliance cannot be skipped
9. **The 30% down payment is standard** — most foreign national programs require it
10. **Digital-first, multilingual onboarding** is essential for international borrowers

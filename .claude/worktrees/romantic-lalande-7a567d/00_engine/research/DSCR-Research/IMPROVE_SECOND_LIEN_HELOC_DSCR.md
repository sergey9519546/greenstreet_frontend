## 1. Angel Oak Closed-End 2nd Lien

### Product Overview

Angel Oak Mortgage Solutions launched the **DSCR Closed-End Second Lien** — the first major non-QM second lien product specifically for rental property investors. This is the product that opened the category.

| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Max Loan Amount** | $350,000 |
| **Min Loan Amount** | $100,000 |
| **Min Credit Score** | 700 |
| **Max CLTV** | 75% |
| **Loan Term** | 30-year amortization (fixed rate) |
| **Rate Range** | ~8.5%–11.5% (varies by CLTV, FICO, DSCR) |
| **Income Qualification** | DSCR-based (property cash flow only) |
| **No Income/ Employment Required** | Yes |
| **Prepayment Penalty** | None (can pay off anytime) |
| **Eligible Properties** | 1–4 unit investment properties |

### How DSCR Works on a 2nd Lien

This is critical and often misunderstood:

- **DSCR = Rent / (1st Lien Payment + 2nd Lien Payment)**
- The 2nd lien lender calculates DSCR using the **combined** debt service of BOTH the existing 1st lien and the new 2nd lien
- Angel Oak requires a **minimum DSCR of 1.0** (break-even) for 2nd lien approval
- Rent is determined via appraisal (Form 1007 / operating income statement), not lease agreements alone
- The 2nd lien underwriter must verify the 1st lien terms (balance, rate, payment) to compute combined DSCR

**Example**: Property rents for $2,500/mo. 1st lien payment is $1,600/mo. New 2nd lien payment at $40K borrowed would be ~$370/mo.
- Combined DSCR = $2,500 / ($1,600 + $370) = $2,500 / $1,970 = **1.27x** — qualifies

### When Angel Oak 2nd Lien Makes Sense vs Cash-Out Refi

The Angel Oak 2nd lien is optimal when:

1. **The existing 1st lien rate is significantly below current market** (e.g., you locked at 5.5% and cash-out refi would be 7.5%)
2. **You need $100K–$350K** and don't want to reset your entire mortgage
3. **You want to avoid prepayment penalties** on a new 1st lien (common: 2–5 year prepay on DSCR 1st liens)
4. **Closing cost savings**: 2nd lien closing costs are ~2–3% vs 3–5% for a full refi
5. **Speed**: 2nd lien closings can be faster than full refi (no 1st lien payoff process)

### Angel Oak's Broader 2nd Lien Family

Per Angel Oak Capital's Q&A (July 2024):
- **Closed-end 2nd**: Up to $750K for conventional, $350K for DSCR; 70–75% CLTV cap
- **HELOC**: Up to $500K (for bank statement / conventional, not DSCR specifically)
- They see the 2nd lien market as a **$21B+ opportunity** in 2025–2026

## 3. 2nd Lien vs Cash-Out Refi: The Math

### Scenario Parameters

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Property Value | $400,000 |
| Existing 1st Lien Balance | $280,000 (70% LTV) |
| Current 1st Lien Rate | 6.5% fixed |
| Current 1st Lien Payment (P&I) | $1,771/mo (30yr, $280K at 6.5%) |
| Cash Needed | $40,000 |

### Option A: Cash-Out Refi

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| New Loan Amount | $320,000 (80% LTV) |
| New Rate | 7.5% fixed |
| New Payment (P&I) | $2,237/mo (30yr) |
| Origination | 2% = $6,400 |
| Prepayment Penalty (on old 1st) | 2% = $5,600 |
| Total Closing Costs | ~$12,000 (including title, appraisal, etc.) |

**Option A — Full cost analysis**:
- Monthly payment increase: $2,237 − $1,771 = **$466/mo more**
- The ENTIRE $280K is now at 7.5% instead of 6.5%
- Extra interest on existing balance: $280K × 1.0% = **$2,800/yr additional interest**
- Net cash after costs: $40,000 − $12,000 = **$28,000 actual cash received**

### Option B: Closed-End 2nd Lien

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| 2nd Lien Amount | $40,000 |
| 2nd Lien Rate | 9.5% fixed |
| 2nd Lien Term | 20-year amortization |
| 2nd Lien Payment (P&I) | $373/mo |
| Origination | 2% = $800 |
| Total Closing Costs | ~$2,000–$3,000 |
| 1st Lien Unchanged | $1,771/mo at 6.5% |

**Option B — Full cost analysis**:
- Monthly payment: $1,771 + $373 = **$2,144/mo total**
- Net cash after costs: $40,000 − $3,000 = **$37,000 actual cash received**
- Only the $40K is at 9.5%; the $280K stays at 6.5%

### Head-to-Head Comparison

| Metric | Option A: Cash-Out Refi | Option B: 2nd Lien | Winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monthly Payment** | $2,237 | $2,144 | **2nd Lien ($93/mo less)** |
| **Total Closing Costs** | ~$12,000 | ~$3,000 | **2nd Lien ($9K less)** |
| **Net Cash Received** | $28,000 | $37,000 | **2nd Lien ($9K more)** |
| **Effective Cost of $40K** | Very high | Moderate | **2nd Lien** |

### When Does Cash-Out Refi Win?

Cash-out refi beats 2nd lien ONLY when:
1. **The existing 1st lien rate is already at or above current market** (e.g., existing at 8%, refi at 7.5% — you're improving the rate on the full balance)
2. **You need more than $350K** (2nd lien max from Angel Oak)
3. **You plan to hold 10+ years** and the rate spread narrows over time
4. **The 2nd lien rate is extremely high** (>12%) making the blended rate worse than a single refi

**Blended Rate Check**:
- Option A effective rate: 7.5% on $320K = **7.5%**
- Option B blended rate: ($280K × 6.5% + $40K × 9.5%) / $320K = ($18,200 + $3,800) / $320K = **6.875%**

**The 2nd lien blended rate (6.875%) beats the cash-out refi rate (7.5%) by 62.5 basis points.** This is the key insight — whenever your 1st lien rate is below current market, the 2nd lien preserves that below-market rate on the larger balance.

## 4. Combined LTV (CLTV) Limits

### CLTV Caps by Lender

| Lender | Max CLTV | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Angel Oak DSCR 2nd** | 75% | Hard cap at $350K loan size |
| **Sun West EZ DSCR 2nd** | 80% | Highest in market for DSCR 2nd |
| **LoanStream** | Up to 90% | Most aggressive — newer product |
| **Griffin Funding HELOAN** | 75–80% | Varies by FICO/DSCR |
| **AD Mortgage 2nd Lien** | 80% CLTV standard; surcharge >80% | 4% surcharge for CLTV >80% on investment |
| **Conventional (Fannie/Freddie)** | 75% for investment | Not DSCR — for reference only |

### How CLTV Affects DSCR Calculation

CLTV and DSCR are **inversely related** — higher CLTV means higher loan amounts, which means higher payments, which means lower DSCR:

```
CLTV = (1st Lien Balance + 2nd Lien Amount) / Property Value
DSCR = Gross Rent / (1st Lien Payment + 2nd Lien Payment)
```

**Critical interaction**: At 80% CLTV, most properties cannot achieve DSCR ≥ 1.0 unless rents are very strong relative to value. This is why 75% CLTV is more common in practice.

**Example — CLTV vs DSCR**:

| Property | Value | Rent | 1st Balance | 2nd Amount | CLTV | Combined Payment | DSCR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | $400K | $2,800 | $240K | $40K | 70% | $1,771 + $373 = $2,144 | **1.30x** |
| B | $400K | $2,800 | $280K | $40K | 80% | $1,771 + $373 = $2,144 | **1.30x** (same if 1st pmt unchanged) |
| C | $400K | $2,400 | $280K | $40K | 80% | $1,771 + $373 = $2,144 | **1.12x** |

The CLTV itself doesn't directly reduce DSCR — it's the **loan amounts** that drive the payments. But higher CLTV typically means borrowing more, which creates a practical constraint.

### Does the 2nd Lien Lender Need to Approve the 1st Lien Terms?

**Yes.** The 2nd lien lender will:
1. Verify the existing 1st lien balance, rate, term, and payment
2. Confirm there are no balloon payments or rate resets in the near term
3. Calculate combined DSCR using the actual 1st lien payment
4. Review the 1st lien for any subordination restrictions
5. In some cases, require a subordination agreement from the 1st lien holder

## 5. Subordinate Financing Restrictions

### Do First Lien DSCR Lenders Allow Subordinate Financing?

**It varies significantly by lender — this is a major pain point.**

| 1st Lien Lender | Allows Subordinate Financing? | Restrictions |
|---|---|---|
| **Plaza Home Mortgage** (DSCR Investor Solutions 2) | **No** — explicitly prohibited | "Subordinate financing is not allowed" |
| **Angel Oak** (DSCR 1st Lien) | Case-by-case | Generally allows if CLTV ≤75% |
| **Most DSCR Lenders** | Varies | Many require prior approval |
| **Conventional DSCR** | Typically yes | Must meet CLTV limits |

### Common Restrictions on Subordinate Financing

1. **CLTV Limits**: Most 1st lien DSCR lenders cap combined exposure at 75–80% CLTV
2. **Payment Shock**: Some lenders limit how much the total payment can increase (e.g., no more than 150% of 1st lien payment)
3. **Pre-Approval Required**: The 1st lien servicer may need to approve the 2nd lien before it can be recorded
4. **Subordination Agreement**: If you refinance the 1st lien later, the 2nd lien holder must agree to remain subordinate — this can complicate future refinancing
5. **Restrictive Covenants**: Some DSCR 1st lien loan documents contain explicit prohibitions on additional liens

### Can You Get a 2nd Lien from a Different Lender?

**Yes** — this is the typical arrangement:
- 1st lien is with one DSCR lender (e.g., Angel Oak 1st lien DSCR)
- 2nd lien is with a different lender (e.g., Angel Oak 2nd lien, Griffin, SWMC)
- The 2nd lien lender is automatically subordinate to the 1st lien by recording order
- You do NOT need to use the same lender for both positions
- However, the 2nd lien lender takes more risk (subordinate position = higher loss severity in default), hence the higher rate

### Practical Problem: Inter-Creditor Dynamics

- If the borrower defaults, the 1st lien holder controls the foreclosure process
- The 2nd lien holder can be "wiped out" if the property sells for less than the 1st lien balance at foreclosure
- This is why 2nd lien lenders require CLTV limits — they need a cushion of equity to protect their position
- Some 1st lien lenders use this dynamic to block 2nd liens, forcing borrowers into cash-out refi instead

### Cross-Collateral vs Individual 2nd Liens

| Factor | Cross-Collateral (Blanket) | Individual 2nd Liens |
|---|---|---|
| **Equity Access** | Across entire portfolio at once | One property at a time |
| **Number of Transactions** | One closing, one payment | Separate closings per property |
| **Closing Costs** | One set of costs | Multiple sets (higher total) |
| **Flexibility** | All properties encumbered together | Can target specific properties |
| **Release Provision** | Usually can release individual properties (with payment) | N/A — separate loans |
| **Max Loan Size** | Up to $6.25M | Up to $350K per property |

| **Best For** | Portfolio consolidation, large cash-out needs | Targeted equity extraction, preserving low-rate 1st liens |

### When Cross-Collateral Beats 2nd Lien

1. **You have 5+ properties** and want to consolidate into one payment
2. **You need >$350K** in cash (2nd lien cap)
3. **You want a lower rate** — blanket loans are 1st lien position, 2–3% cheaper than 2nd liens
4. **You're an experienced investor** with proven rental history
5. **Your properties are in multiple states** — one loan simplifies everything

### When 2nd Lien Beats Cross-Collateral

1. **You have 1–3 properties** — blanket minimum is 2 but overhead isn't worth it for small portfolios
2. **You want to preserve existing low-rate 1st liens** — blanket replaces all existing loans
3. **You want to avoid cross-default risk** — keep properties on separate loans
4. **You need speed and simplicity** — 2nd lien is faster to close
5. **Your properties have very different LTVs** — cross-collateral averages them together

## 7. Home Equity Investment (HEI) Products

### Overview

Home Equity Investments (HEIs) are shared-equity products where a company provides cash in exchange for a share of the property's future appreciation (or depreciation). No monthly payments.

### Major HEI Companies and Rental Property Eligibility

| Company | Primary Residence | Investment Property | Max Amount | Term | Cost Structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Point** | Yes | **No** (primary residence only) | Up to $500K | Up to 30 years | Share of appreciation (typically 25–75% of gain) |
| **Hometap** | Yes | **No** (primary residence only) | Up to $600K | 10 years | Share of appreciation (typically 15–25% of home value) |
| **Unison** | Yes | **No** (primary residence only) | Up to $500K | Up to 30 years | Share of appreciation + origination fee |
| **Unlock** | Yes | **No** (primary residence only) | Up to $500K | Up to 10 years | Share of appreciation |

**Critical Finding: No major HEI company currently serves investment properties.** All require the property to be a primary residence. This is a significant gap in the market.

### Unison's "Hybrid" Product (2025)

Unison launched a hybrid home equity sharing loan that combines:
- A second mortgage with below-market interest rate
- An equity-sharing component
- Still primary-residence only

### Why HEI Companies Avoid Investment Properties

1. **Valuation Complexity**: Investment property values depend on income approach, not just comps
2. **Higher Volatility**: Rental properties have more value volatility
3. **Occupancy Risk**: Vacancy affects both income and property value
4. **Regulatory Concerns**: Different consumer protection rules for investment properties
5. **Adverse Selection**: Investors are more sophisticated and likely to "game" the appreciation split

### HEI vs DSCR 2nd Lien: Cost Comparison (Hypothetical)

If an HEI were available for investment properties:

| Factor | HEI (Hypothetical) | DSCR 2nd Lien |
|---|---|---|
| **Monthly Payment** | None | $373/mo ($40K at 9.5%) |
| **Upfront Cost** | Origination fee (~3–5%) | ~2–3% origination |
| **Exit Cost** | Share of appreciation (15–40% of gain) | None — just payoff balance |
| **5-Year Total Cost** | $40K invested × 25% share × (appreciation) | $22,380 in payments + $3K closing = ~$25K, but you still owe ~$33K principal |
| **If Property Appreciates 20%** | You owe $40K + 25% × $80K gain = **$60K** | You owe **$33K** remaining balance |
| **If Property Depreciates 10%** | You owe $40K − 25% × $40K loss = **$30K** | You still owe **$33K** remaining balance |

**Bottom Line**: HEI products share both upside and downside. For appreciating properties, a 2nd lien is cheaper. For depreciating properties, HEI provides downside protection. But since HEIs aren't available for investment properties, this comparison is academic — DSCR 2nd liens are the only game in town.

## 9. Investor Decision Framework

### Decision Tree: How to Access Equity from a DSCR Property

```
START: Need cash from investment property equity
│
├── Do you have an existing low-rate 1st lien (<7%)?
│   ├── YES →
│   │   ├── Need ≤$350K?
│   │   │   ├── YES → Closed-End 2nd Lien (Angel Oak, SWMC, Griffin)
│   │   │   └── NO → Need $350K–$3M?
│   │   │       ├── YES →
│   │   │       │   ├── Need revolving flexibility? → SWMC DSCR HELOC (1st lien)
│   │   │       │   └── Need lump sum? → Cross-collateral (if 2+ properties)
│   │   │       └── NO → Cash-out refi may be only option
│   │   └── Have 5+ properties? → Cross-collateral blanket likely better
│   │
│   └── NO (1st lien already at 7%+) →
│       ├── Cash-out refi likely better — you're not preserving a rate advantage
│       └── But check: does new refi have prepay penalty? If yes, run the numbers
│
├── What CLTV will the new structure create?
│   ├── ≤75% → Most 2nd lien products available
│   ├── 75–80% → Fewer options (Sun West, Griffin, LoanStream)
│   └── >80% → LoanStream only (90% CLTV) — very expensive
│
└── What's your time horizon?
    ├── <3 years → 2nd lien almost always wins (lower closing costs, preserve rate)
    ├── 3–7 years → Run the blended rate comparison
    └── 7+ years → Consider cash-out refi if you can get a better rate on full balance
```

### Key Rules of Thumb

1. **If your 1st lien rate is 1%+ below current market, 2nd lien is almost always better** for amounts ≤$350K
2. **Closing costs on a 2nd lien are typically 50–75% less** than a full cash-out refi
3. **Blended rate matters more than 2nd lien rate** — calculate the weighted average of your existing 1st + new 2nd
4. **CLTV >80% dramatically reduces options** — most DSCR 2nd liens cap at 75%
5. **Cross-default is the hidden risk** of cross-collateralization — one bad property can trigger default on all
6. **HEI products do NOT work for investment properties** — this is a confirmed market gap
7. **Sun West's DSCR HELOC is the most flexible product** but requires replacing your existing 1st lien
8. **LoanStream's 90% CLTV** is the most aggressive but newest and least tested

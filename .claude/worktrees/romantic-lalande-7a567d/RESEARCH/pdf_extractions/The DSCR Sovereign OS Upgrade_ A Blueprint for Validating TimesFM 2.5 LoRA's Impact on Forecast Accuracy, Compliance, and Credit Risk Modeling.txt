Blueprint for Validating TimesFM 2.5

Technical Feasibility and Forecast Enhancement
The evaluation of the TimesFM 2.5 LoRA upgrade within the DSCR Sovereign OS
framework begins with a rigorous assessment of its technical implementation and its
capacity to enhance forecast accuracy. The proposed strategy represents a sophisticated
application of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) tailored specifically for the
challenges of financial time-series forecasting 4 . The core decision to adopt LoRA over
alternative approaches like In-Context Fine-Tuning (ICF) or full fine-tuning is
strategically sound, balancing performance gains against operational cost and risk 21 87.
ICF, while requiring zero training data and computational resources, offers only good
few-shot performance and lacks the ability to learn deep, domain-specific patterns from
accumulated data . Conversely, full fine-tuning, though capable of marginal gains,
introduces significant risks of overfitting and a loss of the base model's valuable cross-
domain generalization capabilities, alongside substantially higher infrastructure costs 59.
LoRA emerges as the optimal "sweet spot," allowing the vast knowledge embedded in the
200-million-parameter TimesFM 2.5 foundation model to be augmented with minimal
trainable parameters, thus preserving its generalization power while adapting it to the
nuances of property rent histories 35139.
The proposed LoRA architecture, which targets only the attention mechanism projections
(q_proj and v_proj), is a well-founded choice grounded in empirical evidence from
financial benchmarking studies 7 . These modules are critical for determining how the
model weighs and aggregates information across the input sequence; modifying them
allows the adapters to learn DSCR-specific relationships without perturbing the
foundational mechanics of the transformer backbone 3 . This surgical approach
minimizes the risk of catastrophic interference, where extensive fine-tuning can overwrite
crucial pre-trained knowledge 19. The selection of a rank (r) of 16 for production is a
standard practice that provides sufficient capacity to capture complex patterns in

Anti-Leakage Compliance and Temporal Generalization
A cornerstone of any credible forecasting model in finance is its adherence to strict anti-
leakage protocols, ensuring that predictions are based solely on information available at
the time of the decision. The proposed LoRA upgrade blueprint demonstrates a mature
understanding of this principle through its explicit and disciplined covariate handling
strategy. The fundamental rule is strictly enforced: future covariates must derive only
from deterministic, decision-time-forward curves, such as the SOFR forward rates
generated by the NSS/Hull-White engine, and not from realized future data 132. For all
other exogenous variables, such as vacancy rates or occupancy indices, the model is
supplied with past-only information, simulating a real-world scenario where future values
are unknown 22. This clear distinction between predictable future drivers and
unpredictable past-only drivers is non-negotiable for preventing lookahead bias and
ensuring the model's out-of-sample performance is not artificially inflated 24. By
adhering to this discipline, the upgraded TimesFM 2.5 will learn genuine predictive
relationships rather than simply memorizing correlations between covariates and future
outcomes.
Beyond static anti-leakage rules, the proposal places significant emphasis on validating
the model's ability to generalize across different economic and geographic conditions.
This is achieved through a rigorous temporal cross-validation strategy that explicitly
avoids random shuffling of data points, which would violate the inherent temporal order
of time-series data 23. Instead, the dataset is split chronologically, for instance, by
vintage year, to create distinct train and validation sets 24. This methodology is crucial
for testing the model's resilience and adaptability, particularly its ability to perform under
new macroeconomic regimes. The specific requirement to validate generalization from
the pre-2022 low-rate environment to the post-2022 high-rate era is a powerful stress test
93. Such a transition represents a significant structural break in market dynamics, and a
model capable of navigating it demonstrates robustness and a deeper understanding of
underlying causal factors rather than superficial pattern matching 82. This is further
reinforced by the inclusion of geographic hold-out tests, where coastal markets or Sun
Belt regions are completely excluded from the training set 82. This ensures the model
learns nationally relevant patterns and does not become overly specialized to regional
idiosyncrasies, thereby enhancing its applicability across the entire portfolio. This
combination of strict covariate discipline and multi-faceted cross-validation forms a
robust defense against overfitting and establishes a strong foundation for the model's
reliability in a dynamic and uncertain market.

Integration into the DSCR Sovereign OS Credit Risk
Framework
The successful integration of the upgraded TimesFM 2.5 into the DSCR Sovereign OS
hinges on a modular and logical architecture that clearly separates the forecasting task
from the final credit decision-making process. The proposed design correctly positions
TimesFM not as a classifier, but as a dedicated "cash-flow engine" responsible for
generating rich, forward-looking risk features 12. Its sole responsibility is to predict
future rent and NOI paths, including their uncertainty through P10 and P90 quantiles,
conditioned on available information at the decision timestamp 107. This specialized role
allows the system to leverage the state-of-the-art forecasting capabilities of the
foundation model without burdening it with the complexities of supervised classification.
The outputs from TimesFM—the mean, P10, and P90 rent forecasts over a 12-month
horizon—then serve as primary inputs to a suite of separate, supervised models designed
for distinct risk and profitability objectives 45. This decoupled architecture enhances
modularity, auditability, and maintainability, as each component can be developed,
validated, and updated independently according to its specific purpose 12.
This modular approach directly supports the calculation of key credit risk parameters
within the PD/LGD/EAD framework. The TimesFM-generated rent paths are used to
construct the corresponding Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) paths over various
horizons (e.g., 12, 36, 60 months). From these simulated paths, a Probability of Default
(PD) model can calculate the likelihood of the DSCR falling below a critical threshold,
such as 1.0, providing a forward-looking measure of default risk 11. Similarly, the
distributions of future rents inform the estimation of Loss Given Default (LGD) and
Exposure at Default (EAD) by modeling potential cash flows and collateral values at the
time of distress 9 44. The combination of these three pillars—PD, LGD, and EAD—
enables the calculation of Expected Credit Loss (ECL), a cornerstone of modern credit risk
management under standards like CECL 43 81. Furthermore, the same cash-flow engine
powers profitability analysis. By feeding the rent and NOI distributions into a Monte
Carlo simulation, the system can estimate the expected return on equity (ROE) or capital
loan-to-value (CLTV) for a given deal, factoring in interest margins, fees, funding costs,
and expected losses 42 47. This allows the DSCR Sovereign OS to define a "good
customer" not merely as one who meets a static DSCR threshold, but as a borrower who
satisfies minimum risk criteria (e.g., PD < x, ECL < y) while also offering a positive and
competitive expected ROE 12. This multi-dimensional definition aligns credit decisions
more closely with the institution's ultimate business objectives.

Pathway to Regulatory Defensibility and Model
Governance
While the technical implementation of the TimesFM 2.5 LoRA upgrade and its integration
into the DSCR Sovereign OS represent a significant advancement, achieving full
regulatory defensibility requires the establishment of a comprehensive model governance
framework. A well-built forecasting and decision pipeline is only as strong as its
validation and oversight mechanisms. The first pillar of this framework is a rigorous
validation process that goes beyond simple backtesting. It must include formal statistical
tests for calibration, which assess whether the predicted probabilities match observed
frequencies (e.g., loans predicted with a 5% PD should default at approximately a 5%
rate) 10. Discrimination tests, such as ROC/AUC and KS statistics, are also essential to
measure the model's ability to rank borrowers by risk 125. Critically, these validation
exercises must be performed across multiple segments defined by key risk drivers, such as
vintages, geographic regions, and property types, to demonstrate that the model performs
consistently and does not exhibit unexpected degradation in specific cohorts 161.
Performance must be monitored continuously for signs of drift, ensuring the model
remains accurate as market conditions evolve 50.
The second pillar concerns fairness and explainability, which have become paramount in
the regulation of AI systems in finance, particularly under instruments like the EU AI Act
30157. Although TimesFM itself operates on income data, the downstream classifiers built
upon its outputs (for PD and approval) must be constructed with fairness as a primary
design constraint. This involves using models, such as tree-based ensembles (e.g.,
XGBoost, LightGBM), that support monotonicity constraints 45. For example, a constraint
can be applied to ensure that an increase in DSCR or borrower reserves does not lead to
an increase in the predicted probability of default 49. Furthermore, the feature set for
these classifiers must be carefully curated to exclude protected attributes (e.g., race,
gender) and their proxies, a core tenet of fair-lending regulations 31. To satisfy demands
for transparency, the system must also generate local explanations for individual
decisions. Techniques like SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) can be used to attribute
a specific applicant's predicted PD or approval probability to the contributions of each
input feature, providing the necessary justification for adverse action notices and
enabling auditors to understand the logic behind automated decisions 50. All aspects of
this governance framework—from data lineage and model documentation to validation
results and explanation logs—must be meticulously recorded in a centralized Evidence
Vault to provide a complete and auditable trail of the model's lifecycle and performance
161.

## 1. DSCR Baseline (Reference Point)

All comparisons below are measured against this baseline:

| Parameter | DSCR Loan (2025 Range) |
|---|---|
| **Rate Range** | 6.25% – 9.50% |
| **Best Rate (DSCR 1.50+, 720+ FICO, ≤70% LTV)** | 6.25% – 7.00% |
| **Standard Rate (DSCR 1.20-1.30, 700 FICO, 75% LTV)** | 7.25% – 8.25% |
| **Weak Rate (DSCR <1.0 or no-ratio)** | 8.50% – 9.50% |
| **Max LTV (Purchase)** | 75% – 80% (85% at Kiavi w/ 700+ FICO; 90% at Angel Oak w/ 740+ FICO) |
| **Max LTV (Cash-Out)** | 70% – 75% |
| **Documentation Required** | Appraisal, lease or rent comp, entity docs, personal credit — NO income docs |
| **Qualification Metric** | Property DSCR ≥ 1.0 (best pricing ≥ 1.25; some lenders go to 0.75 or no-ratio) |
| **Min FICO** | 620 (Griffin/Easy Street) to 700 (Lima One) |
| **Prepayment Penalty** | 3-2-1 or 5-4-3-2-1 step-down |
| **Term** | 30-year fixed, 5/1 & 7/1 ARM, IO options |
| **Closing Speed** | 2 – 4 weeks |
| **LLC Vesting** | Yes, standard |
| **Property Count Limit** | None |

**DSCR's Sweet Spot:** Stabilized rental properties where rent covers debt service, self-employed investors who can't document income conventionally, scaling portfolios beyond 10 financed properties.

## 2. Bank Statement Loans

### What It Is

Bank statement loans qualify borrowers based on business or personal bank statement deposits rather than tax returns or property cash flow. The lender calculates qualifying income from 12–24 months of bank statements, typically using gross deposits minus a business expense factor (50%–80% of deposits count as qualifying income for business accounts; 100% for personal accounts).

### Key Parameters

| Parameter | Bank Statement Loan |
|---|---|
| **Rate Range** | 6.75% – 10.50% |
| **Typical Rate** | 7.50% – 9.25% |
| **Rate vs DSCR** | +0.50% to +1.50% above comparable DSCR |
| **Max LTV (Purchase)** | 75% – 80% |
| **Max LTV (Cash-Out)** | 70% – 75% |
| **Max LTV (Investment Property)** | 70% – 75% (lower than primary) |
| **Documentation Required** | 12–24 months business OR personal bank statements, business license or CPA letter, entity docs, appraisal |
| **Min FICO** | 600 – 660 |
| **Qualification Metric** | DTI based on bank statement income (not property DSCR) |
| **Prepayment Penalty** | 3-2-1 or 5-4-3-2-1 step-down |
| **Term** | 30-year fixed, ARM options |
| **Property Types** | Primary, secondary, AND investment properties |

### How It Works

1. Borrower provides 12–24 months of bank statements (business or personal)
2. Lender sums all deposits and applies an expense factor:
   - **Business statements:** Typically 50% expense ratio → qualifying income = 50% of deposits (varies 20%-50% by lender)
   - **Personal statements:** 100% of deposits count (after excluding transfers)
3. Qualifying income is used to calculate DTI (typically max 43-50%)
4. Property must still appraise, but qualification is borrower-income-based, not property-income-based

### When Bank Statement Beats DSCR

| Scenario | Why Bank Statement Wins |
|---|---|
| **Property DSCR < 1.0 but borrower has strong business income** | Bank statement qualifies on borrower income, not property cash flow. A $200K/yr business owner can qualify even if the property rents don't cover the debt. |
| **New purchase with below-market rent** | DSCR uses appraised rent or current rent — if current rent is low, DSCR qualification fails. Bank statement doesn't care about the rent at all. |
| **Owner-occupied investment (house hack)** | DSCR is for investment properties only. Bank statement works for primary residence + investment. |
| **Mixing property types** | Bank statement qualifies you as a borrower, not the property — so you can finance primary, secondary, AND investment properties under one program. |
| **Building credit history for DSCR** | Finance with bank statement now, refinance to DSCR after rents stabilize to 1.25+ DSCR. |

### When DSCR Beats Bank Statement

| Scenario | Why DSCR Wins |
|---|---|
| **Property DSCR ≥ 1.25** | DSCR rate is 0.50-1.50% lower — no reason to pay the bank statement premium |
| **Borrower has no consistent bank deposits** | Erratic or seasonal deposits create income calculation problems |
| **Privacy preference** | DSCR requires no personal financial disclosure; bank statement exposes all deposit activity |
| **Scaling portfolio (5+ properties)** | DSCR has no DTI accumulation; bank statement DTI stacks with each new loan |

### Rate Comparison: Bank Statement vs DSCR (Investment Property)

| Borrower Profile | DSCR Rate | Bank Statement Rate | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 720 FICO, 75% LTV, DSCR 1.30 | 7.25% | 8.25% | +1.00% |
| 680 FICO, 80% LTV, DSCR 1.10 | 8.25% | 9.25% | +1.00% |
| 660 FICO, 75% LTV, DSCR 0.90 | 9.00% (or decline) | 9.50% | +0.50% (but bank stmt APPROVES) |

> **Key Insight:** When DSCR < 1.0, the bank statement rate premium shrinks because the DSCR alternative is either a "no-ratio" pricing tier (8.50-9.50%) or a decline. The bank statement loan may actually be **cheaper** than a DSCR no-ratio product for sub-1.0 properties.

## 3. Asset Depletion / Asset-Based Loans

### What It Is

Asset depletion loans qualify borrowers by converting liquid assets into imputed monthly income. The lender divides total qualifying assets by the loan term (typically 360 months for a 30-year loan) to create a monthly "income" figure. This imputed income is used for DTI qualification.

**Formula:** Qualifying Monthly Income = (Total Liquid Assets × 70%) ÷ Loan Term in Months

### Key Parameters

| Parameter | Asset Depletion Loan |
|---|---|
| **Rate Range** | 6.50% – 9.00% |
| **Typical Rate** | 7.25% – 8.50% |
| **Rate vs DSCR** | +0.25% to +1.00% above comparable DSCR |
| **Max LTV (Purchase)** | 70% – 75% |
| **Max LTV (Cash-Out)** | 65% – 70% |
| **Documentation Required** | 2–3 months statements for ALL qualifying accounts (bank, brokerage, retirement), appraisal, entity docs |
| **Qualifying Assets** | Checking, savings, brokerage (stocks/bonds), CD, money market, retirement accounts (often at 70% haircut) |
| **Non-Qualifying Assets** | Cryptocurrency (some lenders), real estate equity, business value, art/collectibles |
| **Min FICO** | 640 – 680 |
| **Qualification Metric** | DTI based on imputed asset income |
| **Prepayment Penalty** | Step-down typical |
| **Term** | 30-year fixed, ARM options |
| **Min Assets** | Typically $500K+ in liquid assets to qualify meaningfully |

### How It Works — Worked Example

```
Borrower has:
- Checking/Savings:    $200,000
- Brokerage (stocks):  $1,500,000
- 401(k):              $800,000 (at 70% haircut = $560,000 qualifying)
                                 
Total Qualifying Assets = $200,000 + $1,500,000 + $560,000 = $2,260,000
Apply 70% factor (conservative): $2,260,000 × 70% = $1,582,000

Imputed Monthly Income = $1,582,000 ÷ 360 months = $4,394/month

If proposed PITI = $2,200/month:
  Front-end DTI = $2,200 / $4,394 = 50% (tight but may qualify)
  If borrower also has rental/other income, DTI improves
```

### When Asset Depletion Beats DSCR

| Scenario | Why Asset Depletion Wins |
|---|---|
| **High net worth, low property rent** | Property DSCR may be 0.80 because rents are low, but the borrower has $3M in assets. Asset depletion qualifies them easily at a better rate than DSCR no-ratio. |
| **Retiree / no employment income** | DSCR fails if property rent doesn't cover debt. Asset depletion creates income from wealth. |
| **Recently sold business** | Large liquidity event but no current income stream. DSCR may work if rents cover, but asset depletion provides a cleaner path. |
| **Multiple low-DSCR properties** | Each property individually may not qualify for DSCR, but combined assets support all loans via asset depletion. |
| **Luxury / low-cap-rate markets** | In markets like San Francisco or Manhattan, rents rarely cover debt at current rates. Asset depletion bridges the gap. |

### When DSCR Beats Asset Depletion

| Scenario | Why DSCR Wins |
|---|---|
| **Property DSCR ≥ 1.25** | DSCR is 0.25-1.00% cheaper and requires zero asset documentation |
| **Borrower has modest liquid assets** | Asset depletion doesn't work well below $500K — imputed income is too low |
| **Assets are tied up in illiquid investments** | PE, VC, RE equity don't count for asset depletion |
| **Want maximum privacy** | Asset depletion requires full disclosure of all accounts; DSCR requires none |

### Rate Comparison: Asset Depletion vs DSCR

| Scenario | DSCR Rate | Asset Depletion Rate | Winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| $2M liquid, property DSCR 1.30 | 7.25% | 7.75% | DSCR (by 0.50%) |
| $2M liquid, property DSCR 0.90 | 9.00% (no-ratio) | 8.00% | **Asset Depletion** (by 1.00%) |
| $5M liquid, property DSCR 0.75 | Decline / 9.50% | 7.50% | **Asset Depletion** (by 2.00%) |
| $500K liquid, property DSCR 1.25 | 7.50% | 8.50% | DSCR (by 1.00%) |

> **Key Insight:** Asset depletion is the "high net worth, low rent" solution. The rate advantage flips when DSCR < 1.0 because the DSCR no-ratio pricing is punitive. With $2M+ in assets, asset depletion can be **1-2% cheaper** than DSCR for sub-1.0 DSCR properties.

## 4. No-Doc / No-Ratio Loans

### What It Is

No-doc loans (also called no-ratio or stated-income-stated-asset in their modern non-QM form) require minimal documentation — neither borrower income nor property cash flow is verified. The lender relies primarily on credit score, LTV, and property value. Modern no-doc products are non-QM (not the pre-2008 subprime version).

### Key Parameters

| Parameter | No-Doc / No-Ratio Loan |
|---|---|
| **Rate Range** | 7.50% – 12.00% |
| **Typical Rate** | 8.50% – 10.50% |
| **Rate vs DSCR** | +1.25% to +3.00% above comparable DSCR |
| **Max LTV (Purchase)** | 60% – 75% |
| **Max LTV (Cash-Out)** | 55% – 65% |
| **Documentation Required** | Appraisal, credit report, entity docs — that's it. No income, no bank statements, no assets, no rent roll. |
| **Min FICO** | 650 – 700 (higher than DSCR because risk is higher) |
| **Qualification Metric** | Credit score + LTV + property value (no DSCR calculation, no DTI) |
| **Prepayment Penalty** | Step-down or flat penalty common |
| **Term** | 30-year fixed, ARM, interest-only options |
| **Property Types** | Investment properties, some primary/secondary |

### How It Differs from DSCR

| Dimension | DSCR Loan | No-Doc Loan |
|---|---|---|
| **Underwriting basis** | Property cash flow (rent / PITI) | Credit + equity position only |
| **Income verification** | None (but rent must support debt) | None (no rent test either) |
| **Rent roll required** | Yes (or rent comp) | No |
| **DSCR calculation** | Yes — must meet minimum | No — not calculated |
| **LTV maximum** | 75-80% | 60-75% (lower) |
| **Rate premium** | Baseline non-QM | +1.25% to +3.00% above DSCR |
| **Best for** | Cash-flowing properties | Properties with zero/unknown rental income |

### When No-Doc Beats DSCR

| Scenario | Why No-Doc Wins |
|---|---|
| **Maximum privacy, minimal documentation** | No income, no bank statements, no rent roll. Only credit + appraisal. If you don't want ANY financial disclosure, this is the product. |
| **Vacant land or unrented property** | DSCR requires rental income evidence. No-doc doesn't care if the property is vacant. |
| **Properties with no comparable rents** | Unique properties (rural, mixed-use, special purpose) where rent comps don't exist. DSCR can't calculate; no-doc doesn't need to. |
| **International investor with US credit only** | No US income, no US bank accounts, but has a US credit profile and cash for down payment. |
| **Extremely fast close needed** | Fewer docs = faster underwriting. Some no-doc lenders close in 10-14 days. |

### When DSCR Beats No-Doc

| Scenario | Why DSCR Wins |
|---|---|
| **Property DSCR ≥ 1.0** | DSCR is 1.25-3.00% cheaper. No reason to pay no-doc premium. |
| **LTV needed above 75%** | No-doc maxes at 60-75% LTV; DSCR goes to 80-85% |
| **Want best long-term rate** | The no-doc rate premium costs $2,500-6,000+/year on a $300K loan |

### Rate Comparison: No-Doc vs DSCR

| Loan Amount | DSCR @ 7.50% (30yr) | No-Doc @ 10.00% (30yr) | Annual Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| $200,000 | $1,398/mo | $1,755/mo | $4,284/yr |
| $350,000 | $2,447/mo | $3,071/mo | $7,488/yr |
| $500,000 | $3,496/mo | $4,387/mo | $10,692/yr |

> **Key Insight:** No-doc is the privacy product. You pay 1.25-3.00% for the privilege of disclosing nothing. For high-net-worth individuals who value privacy above rate, it's worth it. For everyone else, it's too expensive.

## 5. Debt Yield Loans

### What It Is

Debt yield loans qualify based on the property's Net Operating Income (NOI) relative to the loan amount, rather than DSCR (which is NOI relative to debt service). Debt yield is the lender's primary risk metric for commercial real estate — it answers: "If we foreclose, what yield does the property produce on our loan amount?"

**Formula:** Debt Yield = Net Operating Income (NOI) ÷ Loan Amount

### Key Parameters

| Parameter | Debt Yield Loan |
|---|---|
| **Rate Range** | 6.50% – 9.50% |
| **Typical Rate** | 7.00% – 8.75% |
| **Rate vs DSCR** | Comparable to slightly higher (+0.00% to +0.50%) |
| **Min Debt Yield** | 8% – 10% (most lenders require 9-10% minimum) |
| **Max LTV** | 70% – 80% (but constrained by debt yield floor) |
| **Documentation Required** | Operating statement (T-12 or T-3), rent roll, appraisal, property financials, entity docs |
| **Min FICO** | 640 – 700 |
| **Qualification Metric** | Debt yield ≥ 8-10% (NOT DSCR) |
| **Prepayment Penalty** | Yield maintenance, defeasance, or step-down |
| **Term** | 5, 7, 10-year terms with 25-30yr amortization (balloon at term) |
| **Property Types** | 5+ unit multifamily, mixed-use, commercial |
| **Loan Size** | $500K – $50M+ |

### Debt Yield vs DSCR: The Math

```
Property: 10-unit apartment building
NOI: $120,000/year
Requested Loan: $1,200,000
Interest Rate: 7.50%
Amortization: 30 years

DSCR Calculation:
  Annual Debt Service = $1,200,000 × 0.0839 (7.5%/30yr factor) = $100,668
  DSCR = $120,000 / $100,668 = 1.19 ← Below most lenders' 1.25 minimum

Debt Yield Calculation:
  Debt Yield = $120,000 / $1,200,000 = 10.0% ← Meets the 9-10% floor

RESULT: Property FAILS DSCR but PASSES debt yield.
Maximum DSCR-based loan: $120,000 / (1.25 × 0.0839) = $1,143,000
Maximum debt yield loan: $120,000 / 0.09 = $1,333,000
  → Debt yield allows $190,000 MORE loan than DSCR
```

### When Debt Yield Beats DSCR

| Scenario | Why Debt Yield Wins |
|---|---|
| **5+ unit commercial property** | Debt yield is the standard underwriting metric for commercial/multifamily. Lenders prefer it over DSCR for larger properties. |
| **Higher leverage on cash-flowing commercial** | Debt yield often allows higher loan amounts than DSCR when rates are high (because DSCR includes the interest rate in its denominator; debt yield does not). |
| **Rising rate environment** | As rates rise, DSCR shrinks for the same loan amount. Debt yield is rate-agnostic — same NOI, same debt yield regardless of rate. |
| **Bridge/CMBS lending** | CMBS and bridge lenders use debt yield as their primary sizing metric. If you're pursuing CMBS, debt yield is the language they speak. |
| **Interest-only periods** | Debt yield works well with IO periods because it's based on NOI, not debt service. DSCR during IO periods can be artificially high, masking risk. |

### When DSCR Beats Debt Yield

| Scenario | Why DSCR Wins |
|---|---|
| **1-4 unit residential investment** | Debt yield is not commonly offered for SFR/small residential. DSCR is the standard product. |
| **30-year fixed desired** | Debt yield loans typically have 5-10 year terms with balloons. DSCR offers 30-year fixed. |
| **No balloon risk desired** | Debt yield loans require refinancing at term. DSCR is fully amortizing. |
| **Smaller loans (<$500K)** | Debt yield lenders typically don't go below $500K. DSCR starts at $50-75K. |
| **Want non-recourse** | Both can be non-recourse, but DSCR non-recourse is more standardized for small-balance. |

### Rate Comparison: Debt Yield vs DSCR (5+ Unit Property)

| Scenario | DSCR Rate | Debt Yield Rate | LTV Allowed | Loan Amount Diff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10-unit, NOI $120K, 75% LTV | 7.75% | 7.50% | DSCR: $1,058K / DebtYield: $1,143K | +$85K with debt yield |
| 20-unit, NOI $300K, 75% LTV | 7.50% | 7.25% | DSCR: $2,646K / DebtYield: $2,857K | +$211K with debt yield |
| 5-unit, NOI $55K, 75% LTV | 8.00% | 8.25% | DSCR: $484K / DebtYield: $550K | +$66K with debt yield |

> **Key Insight:** For 5+ unit properties, debt yield almost always allows a **larger loan amount** than DSCR because it's not penalized by the interest rate in its denominator. The higher the rate environment, the bigger the debt yield advantage.

## 6. Seller Financing

### What It Is

Seller financing (also called owner carry-back, purchase-money mortgage, or installment sale) is when the property seller acts as the lender. The buyer makes payments directly to the seller instead of (or in addition to) a traditional mortgage. Terms are negotiated directly between buyer and seller.

### Key Parameters

| Parameter | Seller Financing |
|---|---|
| **Rate Range** | 3.50% – 9.00% (negotiated; typically at or below market) |
| **Typical Rate** | 5.00% – 7.50% |
| **Rate vs DSCR** | Can be **1.00% to 4.00% LOWER** than DSCR |
| **Max LTV** | Whatever buyer and seller agree (often 70-90%) |
| **Documentation Required** | Promissory note, deed of trust/mortgage, purchase agreement — no lender underwriting |
| **Min FICO** | Seller decides (often no credit check) |
| **Qualification Metric** | Seller's discretion (can be none) |
| **Prepayment Penalty** | Negotiable (often none) |
| **Term** | Typically 3-10 years (balloon), sometimes 15-30 years amortized |
| **Balloon** | Common — seller wants their equity back within 3-10 years |
| **Property Types** | Any — no lender restrictions |
| **Closing Speed** | 1-2 weeks (no lender underwriting) |
| **Closing Costs** | Minimal — no origination, no lender title, no appraisal required |

### Common Seller Financing Structures

| Structure | Description | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| **Full carry-back** | Seller finances 100% of purchase price | Seller wants income stream; buyer has no down payment |
| **Partial carry-back** | Seller carries a 2nd lien (10-20%) behind a 1st mortgage | Buyer needs higher LTV than 1st lender allows |
| **Wrap-around** | Seller's existing mortgage stays in place; buyer pays seller who pays original lender | Seller has below-market rate they want to keep |
| **Subject-to** | Buyer takes over seller's existing payments | Seller is distressed; buyer wants low-rate assumption |
| **Land contract / Contract for deed** | Seller retains title until paid in full | Seller wants maximum security |

### When Seller Financing Beats DSCR

| Scenario | Why Seller Financing Wins |
|---|---|
| **Seller offering carry-back financing** | If a seller is motivated and offers carry-back terms at 5-6%, that's 1.5-3.5% below DSCR rates. On a $400K loan, that's $6,000-14,000/year savings. |
| **Property doesn't qualify for DSCR** | Unrentable property, unique property, or property with title/environmental issues that no lender will finance. Seller doesn't care. |
| **Need maximum LTV** | Seller may finance 90-100% LTV. No institutional lender will. |
| **Speed is critical** | Close in 1-2 weeks. No underwriting, no appraisal contingency. |
| **Below-market rate via wrap-around** | If seller has a 3.5% mortgage from 2021, they can wrap at 5.5% and pocket the 2% spread. Buyer gets 5.5% when market is 7.5%. |
| **Credit challenges** | Seller may not check credit at all. DSCR requires 620-700 FICO. |
| **Minimal closing costs** | Save $8,000-15,000 in origination, lender title, appraisal, and underwriting fees. |
| **Tax advantages for seller** | Seller defers capital gains via installment sale (IRS §453). Mutual benefit. |

### When DSCR Beats Seller Financing

| Scenario | Why DSCR Wins |
|---|---|
| **Seller wants all cash** | No carry-back available. Must use institutional financing. |
| **Long-term fixed rate needed** | Seller financing typically balloons in 3-10 years. DSCR gives 30-year fixed. |
| **Refinance risk** | At balloon, you must refinance or pay off. If rates are higher then, you're stuck. |
| **No seller relationship** | You're dealing with an individual, not a regulated institution. Dispute resolution is harder. |
| **Due-on-sale clause risk** | If wrap-around, the original lender may call the loan due if they discover the transfer. |
| **Need institutional servicing** | Seller may not escrow taxes/insurance, may not report to credit bureaus. |

### Rate Comparison: Seller Financing vs DSCR

| Scenario | DSCR Rate | Seller Financing Rate | Monthly Diff on $350K |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motivated seller, wrap-around | 7.50% | 5.50% | $420/mo savings ($5,040/yr) |
| Standard carry-back | 7.50% | 6.50% | $210/mo savings ($2,520/yr) |
| Seller wants premium rate | 7.50% | 8.50% | -$213/mo (DSCR is cheaper) |

> **Key Insight:** Seller financing is the ONLY DSCR-adjacent product that can be **cheaper** than DSCR. When a motivated seller offers carry-back at 5-6%, the savings are massive. But it comes with balloon risk and no institutional protection.

## 7. Hard Money Loans

### What It Is

Hard money loans are short-term, asset-based loans from private lenders, typically used for fix-and-flip, value-add, or bridge financing. They're based primarily on the property's after-repair value (ARV) and the deal's profitability, not on borrower income or property cash flow.

### Key Parameters

| Parameter | Hard Money Loan |
|---|---|
| **Rate Range** | 8.00% – 15.00% |
| **Typical Rate** | 10.00% – 12.50% |
| **Rate vs DSCR** | +2.00% to +6.00% above DSCR |
| **Max LTV (As-Is)** | 60% – 70% |
| **Max LTV (ARV)** | 65% – 75% (after-repair value) |
| **Documentation Required** | Purchase contract, scope of work, ARV estimate, entity docs — minimal income docs |
| **Min FICO** | None to 600 (many lenders don't check) |
| **Qualification Metric** | Deal economics (ARV, rehab budget, exit strategy) |
| **Prepayment Penalty** | 3-6 months interest minimum |
| **Term** | 6 – 24 months |
| **Amortization** | Interest-only (most common) |
| **Points/Origination** | 2 – 5 points |
| **Property Condition** | Can finance distressed, non-rent-ready properties |
| **Closing Speed** | 5 – 14 days |

### Hard Money vs DSCR: The Rate Gap

| Loan Amount | DSCR @ 7.50% (30yr) | Hard Money @ 11% (IO) | Monthly Diff | Annual Diff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $200,000 | $1,398/mo | $1,833/mo | $435/mo | $5,220/yr |
| $350,000 | $2,447/mo | $3,208/mo | $761/mo | $9,132/yr |
| $500,000 | $3,496/mo | $4,583/mo | $1,087/mo | $13,044/yr |

> **Total cost of 12-month hard money on $350K:**
> - Interest: $38,500
> - Points (3): $10,500
> - Total: **$49,000 for 12 months of capital**
> - Compare to DSCR year-1: $29,364 (interest only portion)
> - **Hard money costs ~$20,000 MORE for the same 12-month period**

### When Hard Money Beats DSCR

| Scenario | Why Hard Money Wins |
|---|---|
| **Property not rent-ready** | DSCR requires stabilized, rent-ready property. Hard money finances distressed/rehab properties. |
| **Fix-and-flip (not a hold)** | You're selling, not holding. 6-12 month hard money is the right product. |
| **Need to close in under 2 weeks** | Hard money closes in 5-14 days. DSCR needs 2-4 weeks. |
| **Value-add strategy** | Buying below market, adding value, refinancing at higher appraised value. Hard money → DSCR refi is the standard playbook. |
| **Credit too damaged for DSCR** | Hard money lenders often don't check FICO. DSCR requires 620-700. |
| **Bridge before DSCR refi** | Stabilize property first, then refinance into DSCR at better rate and longer term. |

### The Bridge-to-DSCR Strategy (Most Common Use Case)

```
STEP 1: ACQUIRE WITH HARD MONEY
  Purchase: $250,000 (distressed property)
  Rehab:    $50,000
  Loan:     $225,000 @ 11%, 12-month IO = $2,063/mo
  Points:   $6,750 (3 points)

STEP 2: REHAB & STABILIZE (3-6 months)
  - Complete renovations
  - Place tenant at $2,200/mo rent
  - Property now appraises at $350,000

STEP 3: REFINANCE INTO DSCR
  DSCR loan at 75% LTV = $262,500 @ 7.75%, 30yr
  Monthly payment: $1,873/mo (vs $2,063/mo hard money IO)
  
  RESULTS:
  ✓ Payment drops by $190/mo
  ✓ Convert short-term IO to 30-year amortizing
  ✓ Cash out $262,500 - $225,000 = $37,500 extra
  ✓ DSCR qualifies because rent ($2,200) covers PITI ($1,873) at 1.17 DSCR
  
  TOTAL HARD MONEY COST: ~$31,500 (interest + points for 6 months)
  VALUE CREATED: $100,000 (appraisal increase from $250K to $350K)
  NET GAIN: $68,500 minus rehab costs
```

### When DSCR Beats Hard Money

| Scenario | Why DSCR Wins |
|---|---|
| **Property is already rent-ready** | No reason to pay 8-15% when 6.25-9.5% is available. |
| **Long-term hold** | Hard money is 6-24 months. If you're holding 5+ years, DSCR is the right product. |
| **Property cash flows** | If DSCR ≥ 1.0, you qualify for DSCR directly. Skip the hard money step entirely. |

## 9. Decision Matrix: Which Product Wins in Each Scenario

### Scenario 1: Property DSCR < 1.0, Borrower Has Strong Business Income

| Product | Verdict | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| DSCR (no-ratio) | Runner-up | Available at 8.50-9.50%, but expensive for sub-1.0 properties |
| **Bank Statement** | **WINNER** | Qualifies on your $200K+/yr business income, not the property's weak rent. Rate 7.50-9.25% — often cheaper than DSCR no-ratio. |
| Asset Depletion | Viable | If you also have $1M+ liquid, asset depletion may be cheaper than bank statement |
| No-Doc | Overpriced | 8.50-12% — you have income, so prove it and get bank statement pricing |
| Debt Yield | N/A | Not relevant for 1-4 unit residential |
| Seller Financing | Viable | If seller offers terms at 5-6%, that beats everything |
| Hard Money | Wrong tool | You're holding, not flipping |

**Best path:** Bank statement loan at 7.50-9.25% — cheaper than DSCR no-ratio, qualifies on borrower income, 30-year term.

---

### Scenario 2: High Net Worth, Low Property Rent

| Product | Verdict | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| DSCR (no-ratio) | Expensive | 8.50-9.50% for no-ratio; you're paying for what your assets already prove |
| Bank Statement | Suboptimal | Requires deposit activity; wealthy borrowers may not have regular deposits |
| **Asset Depletion** | **WINNER** | $3M in assets = $5,833/mo imputed income. Qualifies at 7.25-8.50% — significantly cheaper than DSCR no-ratio for sub-1.0 properties. |
| No-Doc | Overpriced | 8.50-12% — your assets can prove income more cheaply |
| Debt Yield | Viable | If property is 5+ units with strong NOI relative to loan amount |
| Seller Financing | Viable | If seller offers below-market carry-back |
| Hard Money | Wrong tool | Short-term, expensive, wrong for long-term hold |

**Best path:** Asset depletion at 7.25-8.50%. High net worth borrowers with low-rent properties save 1-2% vs DSCR no-ratio.

---

### Scenario 3: Need Maximum Privacy, Minimal Documentation

| Product | Verdict | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| DSCR | Good but not perfect | No income docs, but still requires rent roll, appraisal, entity docs |
| Bank Statement | Fails | Requires 12-24 months of bank statements — the opposite of privacy |
| Asset Depletion | Fails | Requires full disclosure of all accounts and assets |
| **No-Doc** | **WINNER** | Only credit report + appraisal. No income, no bank statements, no rent roll, no asset disclosure. Maximum privacy. |
| Debt Yield | Fails | Requires operating statements and rent rolls |
| Seller Financing | Also winner | No documentation at all if seller doesn't require it — but depends on seller |
| Hard Money | Viable | Minimal docs, but short-term and expensive |

**Best path:** No-doc loan at 8.50-10.50% if you need institutional financing. Seller financing if a motivated seller is available and doesn't require documentation.

---

### Scenario 4: 5+ Unit Commercial Property

| Product | Verdict | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| DSCR | Viable | Works for 5-10 units, but rate pricing and loan sizing may be suboptimal |
| Bank Statement | Suboptimal | Qualifies on borrower income, not property economics — wrong framework |
| Asset Depletion | Suboptimal | Same issue — borrower-based, not property-based |
| No-Doc | Suboptimal | Too expensive for commercial; LTV too low |
| **Debt Yield** | **WINNER** | Standard commercial underwriting metric. Allows higher loan amounts than DSCR in high-rate environments. More lenders available at 5+ units. |
| Seller Financing | Viable | For smaller multifamily, sellers may offer carry-back |
| Hard Money | Bridge only | Use only as bridge to permanent debt yield or CMBS financing |

**Best path:** Debt yield loan at 7.00-8.75%. Allows 5-15% more loan than DSCR at the same property economics, and is the standard product for 5+ unit properties.

---

### Scenario 5: Seller Offering Carry-Back Financing

| Product | Verdict | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| DSCR | Overpaying | If seller offers 5.5% carry-back, DSCR at 7.5% costs $2,500+/yr more |
| Bank Statement | Overpaying | Same — seller financing is cheaper |
| Asset Depletion | Overpaying | Same |
| No-Doc | Overpaying | Same |
| Debt Yield | Overpaying | Same |
| **Seller Financing** | **WINNER** | 5-6% rate, 70-100% LTV, minimal closing costs, fast close. If seller is motivated and terms are favorable, this beats every institutional product. |
| Hard Money | Wrong tool | You already have financing from the seller |

**Best path:** Seller financing at negotiated rate. Accept the carry-back, then evaluate DSCR refinance at balloon date (3-10 years) if rates improve.

**Critical risk management:** Always negotiate:
- Maximum balloon term (10 years minimum ideal)
- Rate cap or conversion option
- Subordination clause (allows you to refinance the 1st lien without seller's permission)
- Late payment grace period
- Prepayment penalty waiver

### Scenario 6: Short-Term Bridge Before DSCR Refi

| Product | Verdict | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| DSCR | Can't qualify | Property isn't stabilized yet — no tenant, no rent, no DSCR |
| Bank Statement | Possible but slow | 3-5 week close; property may not be insurable/rent-ready |
| Asset Depletion | Possible but slow | Same timing issue |
| No-Doc | Possible but expensive | 8.50-12% for a bridge is very expensive; also 2-3 week close |
| Debt Yield | Not applicable | Property has no NOI yet |
| Seller Financing | Viable if available | If current owner will carry during stabilization |
| **Hard Money** | **WINNER** | Purpose-built for this scenario: 5-14 day close, finances distressed properties, interest-only during rehab, exit via DSCR refinance. |

**Best path:** Hard money bridge → DSCR refinance. The standard BRRRR strategy.

**Cost analysis for 6-month hard money bridge on $300K:**
- Interest @ 11% IO: $16,500
- Points (3): $9,000
- Misc fees: $2,500
- **Total bridge cost: ~$28,000**
- DSCR refi savings: $28,000 is the "cost of doing business" to create a DSCR-qualifying property that cash flows $15,000-30,000/year.

## 1. Who Services DSCR Loans?

### The Short Answer: It Depends — And It Changes

DSCR loans occupy a gray zone in the servicing ecosystem. Unlike agency-conforming loans (Fannie/Freddie) with standardized servicing infrastructure, DSCR loans are non-QM products with fragmented servicing arrangements.

### Do DSCR Lenders Service Their Own Loans?

**Usually not.** The most common model is:

| Model | Description | Prevalence |
|-------|-------------|------------|
| **Originate-to-Distribute** | Lender originates, immediately sells loan into a securitization trust; servicing rights are sold or subserviced | Most common |
| **Portfolio Retention** | Lender keeps the loan on its own balance sheet and services it directly | Rare for DSCR |
| **Hybrid** | Lender originates, retains servicing rights but uses a subservicer for operations | Growing |

Most DSCR lenders are **originators, not servicers**. They generate loans, pool them into Non-QM mortgage-backed securities (MBS), and sell the pools to institutional investors (insurance companies, pension funds, private debt funds, REITs). The servicing is typically:

- **Sold** to a third-party servicer at or shortly after closing
- **Subserviced** through a specialist firm that handles day-to-day operations

### Major DSCR / Non-QM Loan Servicers

Based on industry research, the following firms are prominent in the non-QM/DSCR servicing space:

**Master Servicers / Primary Servicers:**
- **Carrington Mortgage Services** — Both a top non-QM lender and active servicer
- **NewRez / Shellpoint Mortgage Servicing** — Major non-agency servicing platform
- **Pennymac** — Expanded into non-QM correspondent lending and servicing (2025 launch)
- **Specialized Loan Servicing (SLS)** — Known for non-agency and subprime servicing
- **Selene Finance** — Non-agency and scratch-and-dent loan servicing
- **PHH Mortgage / NewRez** — Large-scale subservicing

**Subservicers (Behind-the-Scenes Operators):**
- **FCI Lender Services** — Securitization servicing specialist
- **Mr. Cooper (Nationstar)** — Large-scale subservicing
- **Walter Investment / Ditech** — Legacy non-agency servicing (distressed)

**Special Servicers (Default & Workout):**
- **LNR Partners** — CMBS and non-agency special servicing
- **Midland Loan Services (PNC)** — Structured finance special servicing
- **KeyBank Real Estate Capital** — Multifamily and CRE special servicing

### What Happens When a Loan Is Securitized?

When a DSCR loan is pooled into a Non-QM MBS (often called a "PLS 2.0" or "Private Label Securities 2.0" deal):

1. **The loan is transferred** to a securitization trust (a REMIC)
2. **A master servicer is appointed** by the trust to oversee the pool
3. **A primary servicer** (sometimes the original lender, often a third party) handles borrower-facing operations
4. **A special servicer** is designated to handle defaults, modifications, and foreclosures
5. **The borrower may not even know** the loan has been securitized — they just see a different company name on their payment portal

**Key insight for investors:** The servicer you deal with at closing may NOT be the servicer you deal with 6 months later. This is normal but creates operational friction.

## 2. Servicing Transfer Rules

### How Often Are DSCR Loans' Servicing Transferred?

**Frequently.** Non-QM/DSCR servicing rights are traded actively in the secondary market. Unlike agency servicing (which tends to be more stable), non-QM servicing is viewed as a higher-yielding, higher-risk asset class. This means:

- Servicing can be sold **multiple times** during the life of a loan
- Transfers are especially common after securitization, when the trust re-allocates servicing
- Distressed or underperforming servicing portfolios are bought and sold by specialty servicers

### Federal Notice Requirements (RESPA Regulation X)

Under **12 CFR 1024.33** (RESPA Regulation X), both the transferor and transferee servicer must provide written notice:

| Requirement | Timeline | Details |
|-------------|----------|---------|
| **Transferor Notice** | Not less than 15 days before effective date | Must include: effective date, new servicer name/address, statement that terms cannot change |
| **Transferee Notice** | Not more than 15 days after effective date | Same information plus new payment instructions |
| **Joint Notice** | At least 15 days before transfer | Both servicers can send a single combined notice |

### What the Notice Must Include

- The **effective date** of the transfer
- The **name and address** of the new servicer
- A **toll-free phone number** for the new servicer
- A statement that **the transfer does not affect any term or condition** of the mortgage contract other than servicing
- Information about **where to send payments** during the 60-day transition period

### Can Servicing Transfers Affect Loan Terms?

**No — but the experience can feel like it does.**

- **Rate**: Cannot change. The note rate is contractual.
- **Payment amount**: Cannot change (unless ARM adjustment is scheduled).
- **Escrow**: The new servicer must honor existing escrow arrangements but may analyze and adjust escrow calculations differently.
- **Payment portal/website**: Will change. This is the #1 source of confusion.
- **Customer service quality**: May vary dramatically. Non-QM servicers vary widely in competence.

### The 60-Day Safe Harbor Period

During the 60-day period following a servicing transfer:
- **No late fees** can be charged if you send your payment to the old servicer by mistake
- The old servicer must **forward the payment** to the new servicer
- You cannot be reported as **delinquent** to credit bureaus for payment misrouting during this window

### Practical Advice for DSCR Borrowers

1. **Watch for transfer letters** — they look like junk mail but contain critical information
2. **Set up payment alerts** with both old and new servicer during transition
3. **Keep records** of all payments during transfer periods
4. **Verify auto-pay** is re-established with the new servicer
5. **Do NOT stop making payments** because you're confused about where to send them

## 3. Escrow & Impound Accounts

### Do DSCR Lenders Require Escrow for Taxes and Insurance?

**It varies by lender — but most DSCR loans offer escrow waivers.**

Unlike agency-conforming loans (where escrow is often mandatory, especially above 80% LTV), DSCR loans treat escrow differently because:

1. DSCR loans are classified as **business-purpose credit**, not consumer credit
2. The borrower is typically an **LLC**, not an individual homeowner
3. Many investors **prefer to manage their own tax and insurance payments** for cash flow control

### Typical DSCR Escrow Policies

| Lender Policy | Details |
|---------------|---------|
| **Escrow Waived** | Most common for DSCR. Borrower pays taxes and insurance directly. Often requires minimum credit score (e.g., 720+) and/or LTV below 75%. |
| **Escrow Required** | Some lenders require escrow for all loans; others require it when LTV exceeds a threshold (typically 70-80%) |
| **Escrow Optional** | Borrower can choose. Waiving escrow may carry a small rate premium (0.125-0.250%) |

**Example — Plaza Home Mortgage DSCR Program:**
> "Escrow/impound accounts may be waived when the following requirements are met: Minimum credit score of 720. Minimum [LTV threshold]."

### What Happens If You Don't Escrow?

| Aspect | With Escrow | Without Escrow (Waived) |
|--------|-------------|------------------------|
| **Monthly payment** | Higher (includes 1/12 of annual taxes + insurance) | Lower (P&I only) |
| **Cash flow control** | Servicer manages disbursements | Borrower manages — must budget for large annual/semi-annual bills |
| **Risk of missed payments** | Low — servicer pays on your behalf | High — if you forget to pay property taxes, a tax lien can take priority over the mortgage |
| **Lender risk** | Lower — taxes and insurance are always current | Higher — borrower could let coverage lapse or taxes go delinquent |
| **Rate impact** | Typically no premium or slightly lower | May carry 0.125-0.250% rate premium |

### How Does Escrow Affect PITIA?

PITIA = **P**rincipal + **I**nterest + **T**axes + **I**nsurance + **A**ssessments

- **With escrow**: Your monthly payment IS PITIA (all five components rolled into one)
- **Without escrow**: Your monthly payment is PI only. You must separately budget for T, I, and A

**Critical DSCR calculation point:** When qualifying for a DSCR loan, the lender calculates DSCR using PITIA — regardless of whether you escrow. Waiving escrow does NOT change the DSCR formula, only your monthly cash outflow pattern.

### Escrow Cushion Rules

If escrow is established, the servicer is generally permitted to maintain a **cushion of up to 2 months** of escrow payments, per RESPA guidelines. However, business-purpose loans may not be subject to the same RESPA escrow analysis requirements as consumer loans.

## 4. Payment Processing & Grace Periods

### Typical Grace Periods for DSCR Loans

| Feature | DSCR / Non-QM | Conventional Agency |
|---------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Payment due date** | 1st of month | 1st of month |
| **Grace period** | Typically 15 days | Typically 15 days |
| **Late fee trigger** | After 15th of month | After 15th of month |
| **Credit bureau reporting** | 30+ days late | 30+ days late |

**Most DSCR loans follow the standard mortgage convention:**
- Payment is due on the 1st
- Grace period runs through the 15th
- Late fees apply after the 15th
- Payments 30+ days past due are reported to credit bureaus

### Late Fee Structures

| Fee Type | Typical Range |
|----------|--------------|
| **Late fee percentage** | 4-5% of the overdue principal and interest |
| **Minimum late fee** | Varies by lender; often $25-$50 |
| **Maximum late fee** | Capped by state law in some jurisdictions |

**Example:** On a $2,500/month P&I payment that is 16 days late:
- At 5% late fee: $125 penalty
- At 4% late fee: $100 penalty

### How Does Late Payment Affect DSCR Status?

This is a critical and often misunderstood point:

1. **DSCR is calculated at origination** — it's not continuously monitored post-close
2. **However**, if you are seeking to refinance or acquire additional DSCR loans, recent late payments will:
   - Appear on your credit report (30+ days late)
   - Disqualify you from most DSCR programs (most require no more than 1x30 in the past 12 months)
   - Force you into "scratch and dent" programs with significantly higher rates
3. **If the loan is securitized**, the special servicer monitors pool-level DSCR metrics. Widespread delinquency triggers can affect the entire deal structure.

### Key Difference from Conventional Loans

| Aspect | DSCR Loans | Conventional Loans |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Payment portal quality** | Often inferior; non-QM servicers have less polished tech | Generally excellent |
| **Auto-pay reliability** | May glitch during servicing transfers | More stable |
| **Customer service** | Limited hours; less trained staff | More robust |
| **Payment application** | May be slower (especially during transfers) | Generally efficient |
| **Escrow analysis** | Less standardized | RESPA-governed annual analysis |

**Practical tip:** Always pay DSCR loans via **ACH or wire** with confirmation numbers. Paper checks in non-QM servicing environments can be delayed.

## 5. Default & Foreclosure Process

### What Happens When a DSCR Borrower Defaults?

The default process for DSCR loans follows state law but has some distinctive characteristics compared to conventional loans:

### Timeline from Missed Payment to Foreclosure

| Day | Event |
|-----|-------|
| **Day 1** | Payment due (1st of month) |
| **Day 16** | Grace period ends; late fee assessed |
| **Day 30** | Payment reported 30 days late to credit bureaus |
| **Day 30-60** | Servicer begins outreach (calls, letters) |
| **Day 60** | Payment reported 60 days late; demand letter may be sent |
| **Day 90** | Payment reported 90 days late; Notice of Default (NOD) may be recorded |
| **Day 90-120** | Formal foreclosure process begins |
| **Day 120+** | Foreclosure proceedings (timeline varies dramatically by state) |

**For hard money / DSCR hybrid lenders** (like Capital Fund 1): If a loan is in default for **2 consecutive months**, the loan may move directly into foreclosure proceedings — faster than conventional timelines.

### Judicial vs. Non-Judicial Foreclosure

**This varies by state, not by loan type.** DSCR loans follow the same state foreclosure law as any other mortgage:

| State Type | Process | Typical Timeline |
|------------|---------|-----------------|
| **Judicial foreclosure states** | Lender must file lawsuit; court oversees process | 12-18 months (or longer) |
| **Non-judicial foreclosure states** | Power-of-sale clause allows foreclosure without court | 2-6 months |
| **Hybrid/opt-in states** | Either process available | Varies |

**Key states for DSCR investors:**
- **Texas**: Non-judicial; fast (as little as 60 days from NOD to sale)
- **Florida**: Judicial; slow (12-18+ months)
- **California**: Non-judicial (with additional borrower protections); 4-6 months
- **New York**: Judicial; very slow (18-36 months)
- **Ohio**: Judicial; 6-12 months

### Does DSCR Default Differ from Conventional?

| Aspect | DSCR / Non-QM | Conventional Agency |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Loss mitigation options** | Limited; no agency mandates | Extensive (FHA, VA, GSE programs) |
| **Foreclosure speed** | Can be faster (especially hard money) | Often delayed by modification requirements |
| **Deficiency judgments** | More likely to be pursued (LLC borrower) | Varies by state; sometimes waived |
| **Credit impact** | Severe; future DSCR qualification impacted | Severe but FHA/VA pathways remain |
| **Special servicer role** | Active; may pursue strategic foreclosure | Standard servicer protocols |

### Non-QM / DSCR Delinquency Data

Current delinquency rates show DSCR/Investor loans performing worse than agency but within expected ranges:

| Loan Type | 60+ Day Delinquency Rate |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Agency conventional** | ~1.9% (Q1 2026) |
| **Non-QM Full Doc** | ~0.76% |
| **DSCR/Investor** | ~2.92% |
| **Bank Statement** | ~3.99% |
| **Non-QM 2023 vintage** | ~6.02% (highest) |
| **Non-QM 2021 vintage** | ~1.94% (lowest) |

**Source:** RiskSpan Non-Agency Mortgage Market Update, S&P Global

**Key finding:** DSCR/Investor delinquency rates are roughly **3-4x** agency rates, even within the same FICO bucket. The 2023 vintage is performing the worst due to:
- Adverse selection (stronger borrowers refinanced out)
- Higher rates at origination (2023 peaks)
- Lower proportion of full-doc underwriting
- Thin cash flow margins on properties

## 6. Loan Modification & Forbearance

### Can DSCR Loans Be Modified?

**Yes, but options are significantly more limited than agency loans.**

DSCR loans are business-purpose credit originated outside the QM/agency framework. This means:

- **No statutory obligation** for the servicer to offer modifications
- **No HAMP, HARP, or other government programs** apply
- Modifications are purely **contractual and discretionary** — governed by the pooling and servicing agreement (PSA) if the loan is securitized

### Typical DSCR Modification Options

| Option | Availability | Details |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Rate reduction** | Rare but possible | Usually requires special servicer approval; more likely if loan is in a PSA that permits it |
| **Term extension** | Possible | Extending from 30 to 40 years reduces P&I; but may not be permitted by PSA |
| **Forbearance** | Case-by-case | Temporary payment pause or reduction (3-6 months typical) |
| **Repayment plan** | More common | Spread missed payments over 6-12 months on top of regular payments |
| **Principal forbearance** | Very rare | Portion of principal set aside (not forgiven) to reduce payments |
| **Short sale / deed-in-lieu** | Available | May be preferred by servicer over foreclosure in some cases |

### How Did DSCR Lenders Handle COVID Forbearance?

**Critical distinction:** The CARES Act forbearance mandate applied only to **federally-backed mortgages** (Fannie, Freddie, FHA, VA, USDA). DSCR loans were **NOT covered** because they are:

1. Non-agency (not backed by GSEs or federal agencies)
2. Business-purpose (not consumer primary residences)

However, many DSCR lenders and servicers offered **voluntary forbearance** during COVID:

- Typically **3-6 months** of payment pause
- Missed payments were usually **added to the end of the loan** (not forgiven)
- Some servicers required a **lump-sum repayment** at the end of forbearance
- Forbearance terms varied widely — there was no standardization

**Important:** If your DSCR loan is in a securitization trust, the PSA governs what modifications the servicer can make. Many PSAs limit modifications to **5% of the pool balance** without triggering bondholder consent requirements.

### Navigating DSCR Loan Workout Options

1. **Contact the servicer immediately** — do not wait until you're 90 days late
2. **Document everything** — financial hardship, rent roll declines, vacancy issues
3. **Understand the PSA constraints** — ask if your loan is in a securitization and what modifications are permitted
4. **Consider the special servicer** — if your loan is in a deal that has transferred to a special servicer, that entity has authority to modify
5. **Engage a workout attorney** — DSCR modifications are commercial negotiations, not consumer rights exercises

## 7. Prepayment Process

### Overview: Paying Off a DSCR Loan Early

DSCR loans are **designed for long-term holds**, and the prepayment penalty (PPP) structure reflects this. Understanding the payoff process is critical for investors planning exits via sale or refinance.

### How to Request a Payoff Statement

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| **1. Contact servicer** | Call, use online portal, or send written request |
| **2. Specify payoff date** | You must tell them the exact date you plan to pay off — the payoff amount is date-specific |
| **3. Request two-date payoffs** | Best practice: get payoff as of closing date AND closing date + 7 days, plus per-diem interest |
| **4. Review the statement** | Verify: principal balance, accrued interest, prepayment penalty, recording fees, any other charges |
| **5. Wire funds** | Most DSCR servicers require wire transfer (not check) for payoffs |
| **6. Get confirmation** | Request a release of lien / satisfaction of mortgage within 30-60 days |

**Timeline:** Most servicers provide payoff statements within **5-7 business days** of request. The statement is typically valid for **10-30 days** (check the expiration date).

### Prepayment Penalty Structures

This is the most complex and financially significant aspect of DSCR loan payoff.

#### Step-Down (Declining) Penalty

The most common structure. Penalty percentage decreases each year:

| Year | 5-4-3-2-1 | 5-4-3-3-3 | 3-2-1 |
|------|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Year 1 | 5% | 5% | 3% |
| Year 2 | 4% | 4% | 2% |
| Year 3 | 3% | 3% | 1% |
| Year 4 | 2% | 3% | 0% |
| Year 5 | 1% | 3% | 0% |
| Year 6+ | 0% | 0% | 0% |

**Key insight:** The **5-4-3-3-3** structure (with a floor) is increasingly common and costs significantly more if you exit in years 4-5 compared to a true 5-4-3-2-1.

**Example — $400,000 loan payoff in Year 2:**
- 5-4-3-2-1: $16,000 penalty
- 5-4-3-3-3: $16,000 penalty (same)
- Fixed 5%: $20,000 penalty

**Example — $400,000 loan payoff in Year 4:**
- 5-4-3-2-1: $8,000 penalty
- 5-4-3-3-3: $12,000 penalty (50% more!)
- Fixed 5%: $20,000 penalty

#### Fixed Percentage Penalty

A flat rate (commonly 5%) applies for the entire penalty window regardless of when you pay off.

- **Advantage:** Simplicity — no ambiguity about cost
- **Disadvantage:** No reward for holding longer within the penalty window
- **Typical use:** Lower-rate loans where the fixed penalty offsets the rate discount

#### Yield Maintenance

The most complex structure, typically found in CMBS and some DSCR products:

**Formula concept:** Lender calculates a fee that makes them whole for lost interest income.
- Uses present value of remaining payments multiplied by the difference between your loan rate and current Treasury yields
- **When rates are below your loan rate:** Yield maintenance is expensive
- **When rates are above your loan rate:** Yield maintenance can be cheaper than percentage-based penalties

**Warning:** If your loan includes yield maintenance, **always get an official quote from the servicer** before making exit decisions. Your estimate will almost certainly be wrong.

#### Defeasance

Rare in standard DSCR loans but found in CMBS:
- You don't pay off the loan — you replace the property as collateral with Treasury securities
- Total costs: $55,000-$165,000 in professional fees alone (not counting securities)
- Typically only makes sense for large loan balances ($2M+)

### Hard vs. Soft Prepayment Penalties

| Type | Refinance | Sale | Common? |
|------|-----------|------|---------|
| **Hard penalty** | Triggers fee | Triggers fee | Very common in DSCR |
| **Soft penalty** | Triggers fee | Does NOT trigger fee | Less common; usually costs higher rate |
| **Lockout period** | Cannot prepay at all | Cannot prepay at all | Rare in DSCR; common in CMBS |

**Investor consideration:** If you plan to sell the property before the prepay period expires, a **soft penalty** loan saves significant money — but expect to pay 0.25-0.50% higher rate.

### Partial Prepayment Provision

**Most DSCR loans allow partial prepayments up to 20% of the original principal balance per year without triggering the penalty.** This means:
- You can pay down up to 20% of the original loan amount each year penalty-free
- This reduces the balance that the penalty percentage applies to if you later pay off entirely
- Check your specific promissory note — this provision varies by lender

### What Happens at the End of the Prepay Period?

Once the prepayment penalty period expires:
- You can **refinance or sell freely** with no penalty
- The loan continues amortizing normally
- Some DSCR loans convert to **adjustable rates** after an initial fixed period (5/1, 7/1, 10/1 ARM structures)
- If your loan has an ARM component, plan your exit strategy around the first rate adjustment

## 8. Assumability

### Are DSCR Loans Assumable?

**Generally NO — DSCR loans are NOT assumable.**

This is a significant difference from some government-backed loan programs:

| Loan Type | Assumable? | Mechanism |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **VA loans** | Yes | With VA and lender approval |
| **FHA loans** | Yes | With lender creditworthiness approval |
| **USDA loans** | Yes | With lender approval |
| **Conventional (agency)** | Generally no | Due-on-sale clause prohibits |
| **DSCR / Non-QM** | **Generally no** | Due-on-sale clause; business-purpose structure |

### Why DSCR Loans Are Not Assumable

1. **Due-on-sale clause**: Virtually all DSCR mortgages include a due-on-sale (alienation) clause requiring full repayment upon transfer of the property. This prevents assumption.

2. **Business-purpose classification**: DSCR loans are business-purpose credit, not consumer credit. They lack the regulatory framework that makes FHA/VA assumptions possible.

3. **LLC borrower structure**: Most DSCR loans are made to LLCs. A transfer of LLC membership interests might technically avoid the due-on-sale clause, but lenders increasingly include **due-on-transfer provisions** that capture membership changes.

4. **Securitization constraints**: If the loan is in a securitization trust, the PSA typically prohibits assumptions because they change the credit profile of the pool collateral.

### Are There Any Exceptions?

- **Some DSCR lenders** may permit assumption on a case-by-case basis with full underwriting of the new borrower
- **Transfer of LLC membership** rather than the property deed may be a gray area — but lenders are getting smarter about catching this
- **Subject-to** transactions (where the buyer takes title "subject to" the existing mortgage without lender approval) are technically possible but risky — the lender can call the loan due

### Is This a Disadvantage vs. Government-Backed Loans?

**Yes and no:**

| Consideration | Impact |
|---------------|--------|
| **In a rising rate environment** | Non-assumable is a disadvantage — you can't offer a buyer a below-market rate |
| **In a stable/declining rate environment** | Less relevant — new financing is equally attractive |
| **For investment properties** | Less impactful than for primary residences — buyers are investors, not owner-occupants |
| **Due-on-sale enforcement** | DSCR lenders enforce aggressively (unlike some agency lenders who may not) |

**Bottom line:** If assumability is important to your exit strategy (e.g., selling a property with a below-market 3% rate), DSCR loans are the wrong product. If you're focused on long-term holds and rate is not a selling point, this is a non-issue.

## 9. Year-End Tax Reporting

### Do DSCR Lenders Issue 1098 Forms?

**Yes.** DSCR loan servicers are required to issue Form 1098 (Mortgage Interest Statement) when $600 or more in mortgage interest is received during the calendar year.

### Key Details for DSCR Borrowers

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Form** | IRS Form 1098, Mortgage Interest Statement |
| **Threshold** | $600+ in interest paid during the year |
| **Issuance deadline** | January 31 of the following year |
| **Delivery** | Mailed to the address on file; some servicers provide electronic access |
| **SSN/EIN requirement** | The 1098 must include the borrower's SSN (for individuals) or EIN (for LLCs) |

### How Is Interest Reported for LLC-Owned Properties?

This is where DSCR borrowers face complexity:

#### Single-Member LLC (Disregarded Entity)

- The LLC is a "disregarded entity" for tax purposes
- The 1098 **should be issued to the individual owner's SSN**
- Interest is reported on **Schedule E** of the individual's Form 1040
- The IRS EIN Letter or Form W-9 must be on file with the lender so the 1098 can be properly issued

#### Multi-Member LLC (Partnership)

- The LLC files Form 1065 (partnership return)
- The 1098 may be issued to the **LLC's EIN** or to the individual guarantor
- Interest expense passes through to members on **Schedule K-1**
- Each member reports their share on their individual Schedule E

**Common problem:** Some servicers issue 1098s with SSN fields that don't accommodate EINs, or vice versa. The BiggerPockets community reports frequent issues with 1098 forms asking for SSNs when the payer is an LLC.

**Solution:** Ensure your lender/servicer has the correct tax identification information (W-9) at closing. If the 1098 is incorrect, request a corrected form immediately.

### What Tax Documents Do DSCR Borrowers Receive?

| Document | When | Purpose |
|----------|------|---------|
| **Form 1098** | January 31 | Reports mortgage interest paid ($600+ threshold) |
| **Annual escrow statement** | Annual | If escrow is established; shows disbursements and balances |
| **Form 1099-S** (if property sold) | At sale | Reports gross proceeds from sale |
| **Year-end interest statement** | January-February | Some servicers provide supplemental detail beyond 1098 |

### Schedule E Reporting for Rental Properties

DSCR borrowers with investment properties report on **Schedule E (Form 1040), Supplemental Income and Loss**:

| Schedule E Line Item | What to Report |
|---------------------|----------------|
| Line 12: Mortgage interest | Amount from Form 1098 |
| Line 16: Taxes | Property taxes paid (from escrow statement or personal records) |
| Line 9: Insurance | Property insurance premiums |
| Line 18: Other expenses | HOA fees, property management, maintenance, etc. |

**Key distinction from Schedule A:**
- **Schedule A** (Itemized Deductions): For primary residence mortgage interest
- **Schedule E** (Rental Income): For investment property mortgage interest — this is where DSCR interest goes

### Special Considerations

1. **Business-purpose vs. consumer**: DSCR loans are business-purpose. Interest is reported as a **business expense on Schedule E**, not as a personal deduction on Schedule A. This is actually advantageous — Schedule E deductions are not subject to the $10,000 SALT cap or the mortgage interest limitation that applies to Schedule A.

2. **Multiple properties**: If you have multiple DSCR loans on different properties, each property gets its own Schedule E entry. You should receive a separate 1098 for each loan.

3. **Interest-only periods**: During an interest-only period, 100% of payments are interest (fully deductible). Once amortization begins, only the interest portion is deductible — principal payments are not.

4. **Prepayment penalties**: Prepayment penalties are generally **deductible as interest expense** on Schedule E in the year paid.

5. **Late fees**: Late payment fees may or may not be deductible — consult your tax advisor. The IRS generally treats them as a cost of financing (deductible) rather than a penalty (not deductible).

## 10. Investor Action Checklist

### At Closing

- [ ] Confirm **who will service your loan** (original lender or third party)
- [ ] Obtain **servicer contact information** (phone, website, mailing address)
- [ ] Set up **online payment portal access** immediately
- [ ] Establish **auto-pay/ACH** for monthly payments
- [ ] Confirm **escrow arrangement** (waived or established)
- [ ] File **W-9 or EIN Letter** with servicer for 1098 purposes
- [ ] Review **prepayment penalty schedule** and mark expiration date in calendar
- [ ] Understand **due-on-sale clause** provisions

### First 90 Days Post-Close

- [ ] Verify **first payment** is properly credited
- [ ] Confirm **property tax and insurance** are either in escrow or you've set reminders
- [ ] Watch for **servicing transfer notice** — it may arrive within weeks
- [ ] If servicing transfers: set up new portal, re-establish auto-pay, note 60-day safe harbor
- [ ] Review **closing disclosure** against first statement for any discrepancies

### Ongoing Management

- [ ] Monitor for **servicing transfer notices** (they look like junk mail — don't discard)
- [ ] Keep **12 months of payment records** accessible
- [ ] Track **prepayment penalty countdown** if planning early exit
- [ ] Review **annual escrow analysis** (if applicable)
- [ ] Confirm **1098 receipt** each January
- [ ] If rates drop significantly, calculate **refinance breakeven** including prepay penalty
- [ ] If property becomes vacant, contact servicer **before** you miss a payment

### In Distress

- [ ] Contact servicer **immediately** upon anticipated payment difficulty
- [ ] Request **loss mitigation application** (even though DSCR options are limited)
- [ ] Understand whether your loan is **securitized** (affects modification options)
- [ ] Consider **property sale** before foreclosure (especially if prepay penalty is expired)
- [ ] Engage **real estate attorney** experienced in non-QM/commercial workouts
- [ ] Document all communications with servicer in writing

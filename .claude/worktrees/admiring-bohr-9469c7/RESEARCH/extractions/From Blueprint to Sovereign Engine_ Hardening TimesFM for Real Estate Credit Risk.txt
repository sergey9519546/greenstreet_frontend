The Log-Return Trap and the Superiority of Native
Normalization
The application of foundation models like TimesFM to real estate finance forecasting
requires a departure from standard quantitative finance practices that may prove
detrimental in a probabilistic, long-horizon context. One such practice, forecasting log-
returns and reconstructing the original series through a cumulative product, represents a
significant architectural trap . This method, while seemingly elegant, introduces a critical
mathematical flaw related to error compounding over time. The variance of a sum of
random variables is additive; therefore, as the forecast horizon extends, the uncertainty
associated with the cumulative sum of predicted log-returns grows proportionally . When
this summed distribution is exponentiated to return to the original scale of rent or cash
flow, the tails of the resulting distribution become severely distorted. The right tail (e.g.,
P90) explodes toward unrealistically high values, while the left tail (e.g., P10) collapses
toward zero . For a real estate credit engine tasked with calculating Debt Service
Coverage Ratios (DSCR) under stress scenarios, this renders Monte Carlo simulations
meaningless, as the output distribution no longer reflects plausible future outcomes but
rather a mathematical artifact of the forecasting method itself 29165.
TimesFM's internal architecture provides a more robust and mathematically sound
alternative to manual log-transforms: Reversible Instance Normalization (RevIN) . RevIN
is a normalization technique designed specifically for time-series forecasting that
addresses the core problem of scale and variance propagation 98. During the forward
pass, RevIN scales each time series instance in the batch to have a mean of zero and a
standard deviation of one. After the Transformer decoder generates its predictions in this
standardized latent space, a reversible denormalization step is applied to map the outputs
back to the original data domain 98. This entire process is learned end-to-end as part of
the model. By normalizing the data before it enters the attention layers, RevIN ensures
that the model operates on a consistent scale, preventing features with larger magnitudes
from dominating the learning process. Critically, because the reconstruction is a learned,

===== PAGE 2 =====

continuous function rather than a simple exponential of a summed distribution, it avoids
the pathological tail behaviors associated with naive log-return reconstruction 45.
Relying on TimesFM's native RevIN is therefore not merely a convenience but a
fundamental upgrade that aligns the model's mechanics with the statistical properties
required for reliable risk assessment in CRE finance 98. Studies evaluating TimesFM for
financial forecasting have noted the use of log-transforms for stabilizing training,
particularly for volatile assets like cryptocurrencies where rapid price drops can cause
numerical instability 139. However, this is a training-level preprocessing choice intended
to stabilize gradients during optimization, distinct from the final reconstruction method
for generating a coherent, bounded probabilistic forecast suitable for risk analysis 46.
The goal in a production credit engine is to produce a statistically valid distribution of
potential outcomes, not just a point estimate stabilized during training.

Patching, Covariate Routing, and Attention Masking
For TimesFM to perform reliably in a production setting, its interaction with the input
data pipeline must be meticulously engineered. Two foundational aspects of this
interaction—the patching mechanism and the routing of covariates—are critical points of
failure if not handled correctly. The first issue arises from the model's decoder-only
architecture, which processes time series data in fixed-size patches 44161. In the main
TimesFM model, an input patch length (input_patch_len) of 32 is used, meaning the
model expects to receive sequences of 32 consecutive time steps at a time 44. If the
available historical context for a real estate property—such as 24 months of rent
payments—is shorter than this patch size, the data pipeline will be forced to heavily zero-
pad the input to meet the model's expectations . This padding dilutes the attention
mechanism, as the self-attention layers will treat the padded zeros as legitimate data
points, causing the model to attend to non-existent information and degrading forecast
accuracy . The solution is twofold. First, the context_len parameter in the data
pipeline should be aligned to be a multiple of the chosen patch_len (e.g., 32, 64, or 96
months) . Second, if sufficient historical data is genuinely unavailable, a more invasive
architectural adjustment may be necessary: modifying the patch_len hyperparameter
within the TimesFM configuration itself. For instance, reducing patch_len to 12 could
be strategically beneficial, as it would align the patch boundaries with annual seasonality,
a dominant pattern in many real estate markets . Regardless of the approach, it is
imperative to implement an explicit attention_mask tensor in the data loader . This
binary mask informs the Transformer which parts of the input sequence are actual data
and which are padding, allowing the attention mechanism to ignore the padded regions
entirely. Without this mask, the model remains susceptible to the "no data as zero rent"
fallacy, treating padding as genuine inactivity .
The second critical area is the proper handling of covariates, which has become
increasingly important with newer versions of TimesFM (2.0+) . These versions

===== PAGE 4 =====

introduce separate input channels for past_covariates and future_covariates,
reflecting a deeper understanding of the causal structure of time series data. Mixing these
two types of information into a single tensor silently corrupts the model's cross-attention
mechanism, leading to subtle forms of lookahead bias where future information
inadvertently influences predictions for past time steps . The upgrade requires a strict
separation of concerns in the data loading and collation logic. past_covariates
should contain all variables that are known up to and including the last time step of the
context window. This includes lagged features like trailing three-month delinquency
flags, historical vacancy rates, and seasonal indicators based on the context period .
Conversely, future_covariates are variables whose values are known for the entire
forecast horizon. This category includes deterministic external factors such as the SOFR
forward curve, planned tax hikes, and crucially, MSA-level macroeconomic anchors like
the Zillow Rent Index (ZRI) or local vacancy rates . Properly segregating these inputs is
not merely a best practice but a functional requirement for the model to operate as
intended. Frameworks aiming to bridge TSFMs with covariate-aware forecasting
emphasize the importance of surgically integrating contextual information, reinforcing
the need for a clean and explicit interface between the data pipeline and the model 19
129. The evolution of the data contract, from simple tuples to a structured dictionary
format, formalizes this separation and prevents silent failures that are difficult to debug .
This disciplined approach ensures that the model's powerful attention mechanisms are
directed only at relevant information, preserving temporal causality and improving the
overall reliability of the forecasts.
Injecting Cross-Sectional Anchors and Managing Spatial
Dependencies
A significant limitation of applying a standard Time Series Foundation Model (TSFM) like
TimesFM to real estate finance is its inherent "spatial blindness" . When processing data
for a property, the model treats its time series in isolation, completely unaware of its
geographic neighbors or the broader market conditions that govern its performance. It
sees a duplex in Austin and another in Miami as two entirely separate universes, forcing
it to relearn basic economic gravity for every single asset from scratch . This is a profound
weakness for commercial real estate, where property values and rental income are
heavily influenced by local market dynamics such as job growth, supply of new housing,
interest rate shocks, and aggregate demand 141162. A shock in one Metropolitan Statistical
Area (MSA), such as a spike in vacancy due to a large corporate campus closing, will

===== PAGE 5 =====

inevitably affect nearby properties. A model that fails to recognize this cross-sectional
dependency will produce inaccurate forecasts and a poor understanding of systemic risk.
To overcome this, the concept of injecting "Cross-Sectional Anchors" is a powerful
architectural enhancement .
The proposed solution is to feed MSA-level macroeconomic indicators, such as the Zillow
Rent Index (ZRI) or the official MSA vacancy rate, as a future covariate for every property
located within that specific MSA . By placing these macro-level signals in the
future_covariates channel, the attention mechanism is given a global signal that it
can attend to when making predictions for any property in that MSA. This allows the
model to learn a crucial relationship: "when the MSA vacancy rate spikes, this specific
property's rent will likely follow suit, regardless of its own idiosyncratic history." This
technique effectively provides the model with a "macro anchor," grounding its local
predictions in the reality of the broader economic environment . This approach is central
to the field of Spatio-Temporal Foundation Models (STFMs), which aim to explicitly
model both temporal and spatial correlations 47 49. Research in this area shows that
incorporating contextual and spatial features significantly improves forecasting
performance and generalization capabilities 129. Furthermore, large-scale spatiotemporal
datasets like HouseTS are specifically designed to unify regional socioeconomic variables
with granular property-level data, underscoring the importance of this cross-sectional
information 42. The effectiveness of this method can be rigorously tested through a
Geographic Hold-Out Test: train the model on properties from 90% of MSAs and evaluate
its performance on the remaining 10% . A successful implementation will show a
significant reduction in metrics like Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) on the hold-
out MSAs compared to a baseline model without the macro anchors, demonstrating
improved out-of-sample geographic generalization . This upgrade transforms the model
from a collection of isolated predictors into a cohesive system that understands the
interconnected nature of real estate markets, a prerequisite for building a truly sovereign
credit engine.
Achieving Mathematical Rigor in Probabilistic
Forecasting
For a forecasting model to be considered production-grade in the context of real estate
finance, its probabilistic outputs must be mathematically coherent and suitable for
downstream risk analysis. A primary challenge in this domain is the phenomenon of

===== PAGE 6 =====

"quantile crossing," which often arises when using the standard Pinball loss function for
quantile regression . The Pinball loss optimizes each desired quantile (e.g., P10, P50,
P90) independently, without enforcing any relationship between them. As a result, in
highly volatile or thin markets, it is common to observe illogical predictions where a
higher quantile is estimated to be lower than a lower one—for example, predicting a P90
value less than the P50 median . Such a scenario is mathematically impossible for a true
cumulative distribution function (CDF) and catastrophically breaks any downstream
application that relies on ordered quantiles, such as Monte Carlo simulations for stress-
testing DSCR or constructing meaningful confidence bands 96143. The output ceases to
represent a valid probability distribution, invalidating its use for regulatory-grade risk
assessment 163.
To address this, two primary solutions are proposed, with a clear preference for the more
sophisticated approach. The first is a regularization-based fix: adding a non-crossing
penalty term to the loss function. This term penalizes violations of monotonicity, for
instance, by adding a value proportional to max(0, q_low - q_high) whenever a
lower quantile q_low is predicted to be greater than a higher quantile q_high . While
effective, this is a heuristic correction that adds another hyperparameter (the penalty
weight lambda) to tune and may not guarantee perfect enforcement, especially with
complex interactions 71. The preferred and more robust solution is to abandon the
prediction of discrete, independent quantiles altogether and instead adopt a
Distributional Parameterization approach . In this framework, the model's output head
is configured to predict the parameters of a specific parametric probability distribution,
such as the Student-T distribution, characterized by location (μ), scale (σ), and degrees
of freedom (ν) . The model is then optimized using the Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL)
loss function. The key advantage of this method is that quantiles derived analytically
from a fitted distribution are inherently ordered by definition. This guarantees
mathematical consistency for the entire predictive distribution, eliminating the possibility
of quantile crossing and providing a solid foundation for further statistical analysis 67.
This approach is favored for several reasons. First, it is theoretically more elegant and
robust. Second, it provides a complete probability model, not just a few summary
statistics, which is invaluable for advanced analytics. Third, having a parametric form
facilitates analytical validation and reporting, which is crucial for meeting regulatory
requirements outlined in guidelines for loan origination and monitoring 132. This shift
from discrete quantiles to a full distributional forecast elevates the model's output from a
simple prediction to a statistically rigorous characterization of uncertainty, a cornerstone
of any credible credit risk engine.

Enhancing Robustness with Regime-Aware Adapters
and Handling Missing Data
A truly sovereign credit engine must be adaptable to changing economic environments
and resilient to the messy realities of real-world data. Two critical enhancements for
achieving this are the development of regime-specific adapters and the intelligent
handling of missing data periods, often referred to as "dark months." The first challenge
stems from the fact that economic regimes—such as the low-interest-rate environment
preceding 2022 versus the high-rate, inflationary environment since then—exhibit
fundamentally different patterns of behavior . Training a single Low-Rank Adaptation
(LoRA) module on a mixed dataset spanning these disparate eras results in a "mushy"
model that averages out the characteristics of each regime. This averaged model will be
suboptimal for forecasting in either environment, as it fails to specialize in the unique
dynamics of each period . The solution is to train multiple, specialized LoRA adapters,
each fine-tuned on data from a single, homogeneous regime. For example, one adapter
could be trained exclusively on data from 2015–2021, capturing the low-rate regime
dynamics, while another is trained on 2022–present data, specializing in the high-rate
regime . At inference time, a lightweight gating mechanism can then dynamically blend
the weights of these adapters. This gating function could be a simple heuristic based on a
current macroeconomic indicator, such as the SOFR rate or CPI; for instance, if SOFR is
above a certain threshold (e.g., 4.0%), the model places more weight on the high-rate
adapter . This approach is a form of a pragmatic Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) system,
allowing the model to adapt its behavior to the prevailing economic conditions without
requiring costly continuous retraining 21185. This directly addresses the challenge of
structural breaks in time series, which are shifts in model parameters caused by external
shocks like financial crises or major policy changes 31 32.
The second enhancement addresses the pervasive issue of missing data. In property
management, months may be "dark" due to units being offline for renovation, tenant
turnover, or simple data entry errors . Naively feeding these gaps as null or imputing
them with a simple mean or zero value is disastrous. The model will interpret a
renovation month as a catastrophic market crash, learning incorrect and brittle
relationships . The correct approach is to employ a sophisticated feature engineering
strategy. Instead of simply imputing the raw rent value (e.g., using forward-fill), the data
pipeline should introduce a binary is_missing flag as an additional covariate for each
time step . This flag acts as a signal to the model, informing it whether the observed rent
value is genuine or an imputed placeholder. The model can then learn a more nuanced
behavior: during periods flagged as missing, it automatically widens the P10-P90
confidence bands to reflect the increased uncertainty about the true underlying rent

===== PAGE 8 =====

level . This technique makes the model explicitly aware of data quality and its impact on
predictability. It moves beyond passive acceptance of noisy data and instead builds a
model that can reason about the confidence of its own predictions in the face of
incomplete information. This combination of regime-aware adaptation and intelligent
missing data handling creates a model that is not only more accurate but also more
robust and interpretable, capable of navigating the complexities of real-world CRE data
while maintaining its performance across diverse and challenging economic conditions.
Production Optimization for Real-Time Underwriting
Performance
Transforming a state-of-the-art research model like TimesFM into a production-grade
credit engine requires addressing the final and most critical barrier: inference latency.
While the HuggingFace Trainer API provides a flexible environment for development
and experimentation, its native Python-based execution is far too slow for real-time
applications like an automated underwriting API, which may need to process thousands
of loan applications simultaneously . The latency of a single forecast, even if acceptable in
isolation (e.g., ~200ms), becomes prohibitive when multiplied by the number of parallel
requests and the complexity of downstream tasks like running 10,000-path Monte Carlo
simulations for each applicant . This performance bottleneck necessitates a dedicated
optimization workflow focused on accelerating inference on GPU hardware.
The industry-standard pathway for achieving this is to export the trained PyTorch model
to the Open Neural Network Exchange (ONNX) format and then compile it using a high-
performance inference runtime like NVIDIA's TensorRT 25 26. ONNX serves as a
universal intermediate representation, decoupling the model from the specific deep
learning framework in which it was trained. Once in ONNX format, TensorRT can
perform a series of aggressive optimizations tailored to the specific GPU architecture
(e.g., A10G or A100) 57 58. These optimizations include kernel fusion, precision
calibration (typically to FP16 to halve memory footprint and bandwidth requirements),
tensor core utilization, and graph transformations that simplify the computation graph
57. The expected outcome of this process is a dramatic reduction in latency, with a
decrease from approximately 200ms per property down to around 15ms, enabling the
kind of real-time performance required for interactive underwriting workflows . This
speedup makes it feasible to run extensive risk simulations not just once, but as part of an
iterative, real-time feedback loop with the borrower.

===== PAGE 9 =====

However, this optimization process is not a black box and must be executed with rigorous
validation to ensure that performance gains do not come at the cost of accuracy. The final
success of the productionization phase hinges on three key metrics. First is latency, with
a target of under 20ms per property for batch size 1, ensuring responsiveness . Second is
throughput, aiming for hundreds of properties per second to handle peak loads . Third,
and most critically, is accuracy parity. A cryptographic hash or a statistical test must
prove that the outputs of the TensorRT-compiled engine are numerically identical to the
original PyTorch model 24. The maximum absolute difference between the two outputs
−3
must be below a stringent tolerance, typically on the order of 1e , to prevent the
introduction of hidden biases that could compromise regulatory compliance 154. This
commitment to accuracy parity is essential for creating a defensible and trustworthy
"Sovereign OS" that delivers both institutional-grade reliability and real-time performance
163.

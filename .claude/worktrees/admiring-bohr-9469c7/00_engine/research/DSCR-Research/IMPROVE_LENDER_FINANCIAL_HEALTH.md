# Lender Financial Health & Stability Assessment for DSCR Lenders

**Research Date:** March 2026  
**Purpose:** Provide investors and brokers with a framework for evaluating which DSCR/non-QM lenders are safe to work with and which might exit the market  
**Status:** CRITICAL GAP — This research fills a major void in DSCR lender due diligence

---

## Executive Summary

The non-QM/DSCR lender landscape has experienced significant turbulence since 2022, with high-profile closures (Sprout Mortgage, First Guaranty Mortgage) and major structural changes (Pacific Western Bank/Deephaven merger, Kiavi acquisition by Figure). Non-QM securitization issuance reached $40 billion in 2024 (up 34% YoY per Morningstar DBRS), yet rising DSCR default rates in 2023–2024 vintages (30+ DQ at 6.9% for 2023 DSCR vintage per Fitch) threaten lender stability. This document provides a comprehensive framework for assessing lender financial health, including a 0–100 stability scoring system.

## 3. Warehouse Line Risk

### 3.1 How DSCR Lenders Fund Their Loans

DSCR/non-QM lenders typically fund loans through **warehouse lines of credit** — short-term revolving credit facilities provided by banks and financial institutions. The cycle works as follows:

1. **Lender originates loan** using warehouse line funds
2. **Warehouse bank holds the mortgage note** as collateral
3. **Lender sells the loan** into the secondary market (securitization or whole loan sale)
4. **Proceeds repay the warehouse line**, freeing capacity for new originations

For non-QM/DSCR products, advance rates typically range from **80–90%** (vs. 95–98% for agency loans), requiring more substantial equity from the lender.

### 3.2 What Happens If a Warehouse Lender Pulls a Line

| Scenario | Impact |
|----------|--------|
| **Line not renewed at term** | Lender must find alternative warehouse provider or cease originations |
| **Advance rate reduced** | Lender needs more capital per loan, reducing volume capacity |
| **Line frozen mid-cycle** | Loans in pipeline cannot be funded; borrowers lose rate locks |
| **Covenant violation triggers** | Warehouse lender can seize collateral (the mortgage notes) |

**Critical Quote (Ginnie Mae):** "A typical Mortgage Banker will cease to operate without a warehouse line of credit."

**Critical Quote (Brookings Institution):** "When the term of the warehouse line expires, the non-bank must negotiate a new contract with the warehouse lender. This is known as 'rollover risk.'"

### 3.4 Non-QM Warehouse Risk Factors

- **Longer dwell time:** Non-QM loans stay on warehouse lines longer (~60–90 days vs. 30–45 days for agency loans) per Inside Mortgage Finance, increasing warehouse lender risk exposure
- **Limited warehouse providers:** Fewer banks are willing to provide warehouse lines for non-QM products, creating concentration risk
- **Rising rate environment risk:** When rates rise, existing loans on warehouse lines may be worth less than their funding cost, creating margin calls
- **Flagstar precedent:** In 2022, Flagstar's warehouse division required advance approval for non-QM mortgages — a signal that warehouse providers can quickly tighten terms

### 4.3 Does Securitization Volume Indicate Stability?

**Yes, with caveats:**

| Pro | Con |
|-----|-----|
| Regular securitization = consistent secondary market access | High volume doesn't mean high quality |
| Demonstrates institutional investor demand for the lender's product | A lender can securitize and still fail (FGMC securitized before bankruptcy) |
| Provides capital recycling (funds new originations) | Securitization windows can close abruptly in market stress |
| Rating agency oversight adds discipline | "Bigger" doesn't mean "safer" — Sprout was a major originator |

### 4.4 What Happens When a Lender Can't Securitize

When securitization markets freeze or a lender's loan quality deteriorates:

1. **Loans pile up on warehouse lines** — increasing carrying costs and reducing origination capacity
2. **Margin compression** — the lender must hold loans at a loss or sell at deep discounts
3. **Warehouse covenant violations** — extended dwell time triggers warehouse lender scrutiny
4. **Forced selling** — distressed loan sales at significant discounts
5. **Capital depletion** — losses erode the thin equity base most non-QM lenders operate on
6. **Potential failure** — this is exactly what happened to Sprout and FGMC in 2022

**Key Insight:** Higher default rates in 2023–2024 vintages could lead to reduced investor appetite for DSCR-backed RMBS, potentially creating a negative feedback loop for DSCR lenders who depend on securitization.

## 6. Early Warning Signs of Lender Distress

### 6.1 Operational Warning Signs

| Warning Sign | Severity | Explanation |
|--------------|----------|-------------|
| **Rate increases out of market** | HIGH | If a lender's rates are consistently 50–100+ bps above competitors, they may be trying to slow volume or compensate for capital costs |
| **Slower closings** | HIGH | Delays in closing may indicate warehouse line issues, staffing problems, or capital constraints |
| **Reduced AE communication** | MEDIUM | Account executives going dark or being unresponsive often precedes layoffs or shutdown |
| **Guideline tightening** | MEDIUM | Sudden guideline changes may indicate warehouse lender pressure or risk management concerns |
| **Staff layoffs** | HIGH | Layoffs (especially in operations/sales) are a strong leading indicator of financial distress |
| **Executive departures** | HIGH | C-suite or senior management departures, especially CFO or COO, signal internal problems |
| **Delayed securitizations** | HIGH | If a regular issuer hasn't securitized in an extended period, loan quality or market access may be deteriorating |
| **Warehouse line changes** | CRITICAL | Any change in warehouse line terms (reduced advance rates, shorter terms, new covenants) is a major red flag |

### 6.2 Financial Warning Signs

| Warning Sign | Severity | Explanation |
|--------------|----------|-------------|
| **Financial reporting delays** | HIGH | If a public lender delays earnings or a private lender stops sharing financials, something is wrong |
| **High employee turnover** | MEDIUM | Broad-based turnover suggests organizational instability |
| **Fixed asset auctions** | HIGH | Selling equipment or office space indicates cash flow problems |
| **Questionable accounting practices** | CRITICAL | Any hint of accounting irregularities is a deal-breaker |
| **Frequent management changes** | MEDIUM | Instability at the top filters down to operations |
| **Vendor payment delays** | HIGH | If appraisers, title companies, or other vendors report late payments, the lender is in trouble |

### 6.3 Market-Level Warning Signs

| Warning Sign | Severity | Explanation |
|--------------|----------|-------------|
| **Rising DSCR default rates** | HIGH | If the broader DSCR market shows stress, all DSCR lenders face headwinds |
| **Warehouse provider exits non-QM** | CRITICAL | When warehouse banks pull back from non-QM (like Flagstar in 2022), it signals sector-level risk |
| **Securitization market freeze** | CRITICAL | If non-QM RMBS issuance stalls, DSCR lenders lose their primary exit strategy |
| **Regulatory scrutiny increase** | MEDIUM | New CFPB enforcement actions or state licensing issues signal coming industry pressure |

## 8. What Happens to Borrowers When a DSCR Lender Fails

### 8.1 Existing Funded Loans

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Do I still have to pay my mortgage?** | YES — the loan obligation survives lender bankruptcy |
| **What happens to my loan?** | The loan will typically be **sold to another lender or servicer** as part of bankruptcy proceedings |
| **Are my original terms preserved?** | YES — the new servicer must honor the original terms (rate, payment, schedule, prepay penalty) |
| **What about my escrow?** | Escrow funds are protected and transfer to the new servicer |
| **Does my payment change?** | No — the payment amount, interest rate, and schedule remain the same |
| **What about prepay penalties?** | Prepay penalties from the original loan agreement remain in force |

### 8.2 Loans in Progress (Pipeline)

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **What happens to my rate lock?** | This is the **highest-risk scenario** — rate locks may NOT be honored if the lender fails before closing |
| **Will my closing be delayed?** | Very likely — the lender's shutdown will disrupt all loans in the pipeline |
| **Can I move my loan to another lender?** | Possibly, but you'll need to re-lock at current market rates, which may be higher |
| **Who pays for re-locking?** | The borrower typically bears this cost; there is no established protection mechanism |

### 8.3 Specific DSCR Borrower Risks

| Risk | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Rate lock loss** | Unlike agency loans where the GSEs provide some backstop, DSCR rate locks have no such protection |
| **Appraisal expiration** | If the lender fails mid-process, the appraisal may expire before a new lender can use it |
| **Due diligence costs** | Borrowers may lose money spent on inspections, appraisals, and other due diligence |
| **Purchase contract risk** | If the loan doesn't close on time, the borrower may lose their purchase contract and earnest money |
| **Limited borrower protections** | DSCR/investment property loans have fewer consumer protections than owner-occupied residential mortgages |

### 8.4 Mitigation Strategies for Borrowers

1. **Always have a backup lender** — get pre-approved with at least 2 DSCR lenders
2. **Never let rate locks expire** — track lock expiration dates meticulously
3. **Include financing contingencies** — in purchase contracts, ensure financing contingencies cover lender failure
4. **Monitor your lender** — watch for the warning signs in Section 6
5. **Prioritize Tier 1 and Tier 2 lenders** — use the stability scoring system above

## 10. Recommendations

### 10.1 For Investors/Borrowers
1. **Use the Stability Scoring System** — evaluate every DSCR lender before committing
2. **Always maintain 2+ lender relationships** — never depend on a single DSCR lender
3. **Monitor lender health quarterly** — check for the warning signs in Section 6
4. **Prioritize publicly traded or transparent lenders** — Angel Oak's NYSE listing provides the best visibility
5. **Be cautious with opaque private lenders** — LendSure and similar lenders warrant extra due diligence

### 10.2 For Brokers
1. **Diversify lender panels** — don't concentrate pipeline with any single DSCR lender
2. **Track securitization activity** — lenders who haven't securitized recently may be struggling
3. **Watch warehouse line news** — if a major warehouse provider exits non-QM, all DSCR lenders face risk
4. **Monitor default rates** — rising DSCR defaults will eventually impact lender stability
5. **Communicate warning signs to clients** — if a lender shows distress indicators, proactively advise borrowers

### 10.3 For the DSCR Platform
1. **Integrate the Stability Score** into the lender recommendation engine
2. **Build automated monitoring** — track warehouse line changes, securitization filings, regulatory actions
3. **Create a "Lender Health Dashboard"** — real-time monitoring of key stability indicators
4. **Develop backup lender matching** — automatically suggest alternatives if a primary lender shows distress
5. **Add pipeline protection features** — alerts when lender health scores decline while borrower loans are in process

## 2. "What is a DSCR loan?" — the explainer (paste-ready)

**Heading:** What is a DSCR loan?

A DSCR loan — short for debt-service-coverage-ratio — is a mortgage for investment
property that qualifies you on the property's rental income, not your personal income.

A traditional mortgage looks at *you*: your pay stubs, your tax returns, your
debt-to-income ratio. A DSCR loan looks at the *property*: does the rent cover the loan
payment? If it does, you can qualify — even without US credit history or a US paycheck.

That's why DSCR loans are popular with real estate investors, self-employed business
owners, and foreign nationals. They fall under "non-QM" (non-qualified mortgage)
lending — loans built for serious borrowers who don't fit the traditional bank box.
The property's income tells the story.

**Heading:** Why investors choose DSCR

Because you qualify on the property and not your salary, your personal income never
caps how many properties you can own. Find a deal that cash-flows, and you can keep
building your portfolio — one door, then the next.

## 3. How DSCR is calculated (teach the math simply)

**Heading:** How lenders calculate DSCR

The formula is simple:

**DSCR = Net Operating Income (NOI) ÷ Total Debt Service**

- **Net Operating Income (NOI):** the rent left over after operating costs like
  management, maintenance, and utilities.
- **Total Debt Service:** the full monthly payment — principal, interest, taxes,
  insurance, and HOA (lenders shorten this to PITIA).

What the result means:
- **1.0** — the property breaks even; rent exactly covers the payment.
- **Above 1.0** — positive cash flow; the property earns more than it costs to own.
- Most programs want **1.0 or higher**, and **1.2+** gives you the strongest terms.

**Heading:** A quick example

Say you buy a rental and tenants pay **$3,600/month**, with no extra operating
expenses, while your total monthly payment is **$2,700**.

**DSCR = $3,600 ÷ $2,700 = 1.33**

A 1.33 ratio means the property earns 33% more than it costs to carry — a healthy
cushion. That's good for you (real cash flow) and good for the lender (lower risk).
With a DSCR loan, the lender is on your side: when the property wins, you both win.

## 4. How to improve your DSCR (helpful = trustworthy)

**Heading:** Five ways to strengthen your DSCR

If your ratio is close but not quite there, small moves add up:

1. **Raise the rent** to match market — or run it as a short-term rental where allowed.
2. **Trim operating costs** like management fees or service contracts.
3. **Put more down** — a smaller loan means a smaller payment.
4. **Refinance to a lower rate** to cut the monthly payment.
5. **Clear small property debts** that drag on cash flow.

Even minor changes can tip a file from "not yet" to "approved." Our team will show you
the shortest path to qualifying.

## 5. When a DSCR loan makes sense (set expectations)

**Heading:** Is a DSCR loan right for your strategy?

DSCR loans are built for **rental properties** — both kinds:

- **Long-term rentals.** Leased to tenants on annual agreements. Steady, predictable
  cash flow; great for investors who want stability.
- **Short-term / vacation rentals (Airbnb, VRBO).** Higher income potential, more
  seasonal. Because nightly income varies by location and property, we underwrite STR
  income using verified market data and projections — not best-case guesses.

If you're buying a property you'll live in, a DSCR loan isn't the fit — these are for
income property. (Ask us and we'll point you the right way.)

## 6. Terms worth knowing before you sign (educate, reduce friction)

**Heading:** The options that shape your loan

DSCR loans aren't one-size-fits-all. Ask about:

- **Loan term.** 30-year and 15-year are most common; the term changes your monthly
  payment and lifetime interest.
- **Fixed vs. adjustable rate.** A fixed rate never changes — easiest to budget. An ARM
  starts lower, then can adjust later; we'll model the worst-case reset before you sign.
- **Prepayment penalty.** Many DSCR loans reward holding the property a few years; the
  penalty terms vary, so know yours up front.
- **Loan-to-value (LTV).** Most DSCR programs lend up to ~75–80% of the property's
  value, so plan for roughly 20–25% down. Less leverage = stronger file.
- **Property type.** Single-family and small multi-unit (up to 4 units) are standard;
  larger and mixed-use have their own programs.
- **Business entity.** DSCR loans are made to a US business entity (usually an LLC), not
  an individual — see the foreign-national section below.

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

| Metric | Standard Timeline | Fast-Close Timeline | Conventional Comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Total Days to Close** | 21–45 days (avg 30) | 10–15 days | 30–45 days |
| **Application to Submission** | 1–3 days | Same day | 1–3 days |
| **Submission to Initial Review** | 1–3 days | Same day | 2–5 days |
| **Initial Review to Condition Sheet** | 5–10 days | 1–3 days | 7–14 days |
| **Condition Clearance** | 5–15 days | 2–5 days | 7–14 days |
| **Clear to Close to Funding** | 3–7 days | 1–2 days | 3–7 days |
| **Closing Costs (% of loan)** | 2%–5% | 2%–5% (same) | 3%–6% |
| **Key Bottleneck** | Appraisal (5–14 days) | Appraisal (still bottleneck) | Appraisal + income docs |

**Key Insight:** DSCR loans close at roughly the same speed as conventional loans for the standard process. The advantage is that DSCR requires **no income verification** (no W-2s, no tax returns), which eliminates the most common conventional delay. Fast-close DSCR lenders (Dominion Financial, Tidal Loans, Lendmire) can achieve 10–15 day closings through AI underwriting and parallel processing — something conventional lenders rarely match.

### Phase 1: Application & Document Submission (Days 1–3)

**What happens:** Borrower formally submits loan application with purchase contract and property details. If pre-approved, this is mostly providing property-specific information.

**Documents required:**
- Fully executed purchase agreement
- Property address and legal description
- Expected rental income documentation (existing leases or market rent analysis)
- Entity documents (Articles of Organization, Operating Agreement, EIN letter)
- Updated bank statements showing funds for down payment + closing costs + reserves
- Proof of insurance binder (or in-progress quote)

**Who does what:**
| Role | Action |
|---|---|
| **Borrower/Investor** | Gathers and submits all documents |
| **Loan Officer/Broker** | Reviews completeness, submits to lender |
| **Processor** | Opens file, validates documents, creates checklist |

**How it differs from conventional:** No W-2s, no tax returns, no pay stubs required. Entity documents replace personal income docs. This is a major time saver.

---

### Phase 2: Appraisal Ordering (Days 3–7)

**What happens:** Lender orders property appraisal from a licensed independent appraiser via an Appraisal Management Company (AMC). The appraisal determines two critical things:
1. **Market value** of the property (affects LTV ratio)
2. **Market rent** (determines DSCR ratio)

**Cost:** $400–$800 (SFR) | $800–$1,500 (small multifamily)

**Timeline by market:**
| Market Type | Turnaround |
|---|---|
| Small/rural markets | 5–7 days |
| Major metros (Phoenix, Atlanta, Dallas) | 7–14 days |
| Complex/multifamily | 14–21 days |

**Who does what:**
| Role | Action |
|---|---|
| **Processor** | Orders appraisal through AMC |
| **Appraiser/AMC** | Inspects property, prepares report with value + rent analysis |
| **Underwriter** | Reviews appraisal when received (Phase 5) |

**⚠️ This is the #1 bottleneck in DSCR closings.** Appraisal cannot be parallelized — it's on the critical path. BiggerPockets community consensus: "The largest determining factor is the speed of the appraiser."

**How to expedite:**
- Request rush appraisal (adds $200–$400 fee, saves 3–5 days)
- Use lenders with in-house AMC relationships
- Some fast-close lenders (Dominion) use AI-driven AVM for initial valuation, conventional appraisal in parallel

---

### Phase 3: Title Search & Insurance (Days 3–10) — **PARALLEL**

**What happens:** Title company conducts thorough ownership history search, looking for:
- Outstanding liens (tax liens, mechanic's liens, judgment liens)
- HOA violations or unpaid assessments
- Defects in chain of title
- Easements and encumbrances

Once clear, title company issues a **title commitment** (promise to insure).

**Cost:**
| Item | Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Lender's title insurance (required) | $500–$1,500 |
| Owner's title insurance (recommended) | $500–$2,000 |
| Title search fee | $200–$400 |

**Who does what:**
| Role | Action |
|---|---|
| **Processor** | Opens title order with title company |
| **Title Company** | Conducts search, resolves issues, issues commitment |
| **Underwriter** | Reviews title commitment for deal-breakers |

**Common title issues that cause delays:**
- Unreleased liens from previous owner
- Boundary disputes
- Missing assignments in chain of title
- Unpaid property taxes

---

### Phase 4: Insurance Binding (Days 5–15) — **PARALLEL**

**What happens:** Borrower must secure property insurance before closing. Lender requires proof that property is insured for at least the loan amount.

**Required coverage:**
- Hazard insurance (fire, wind, covered perils)
- Flood insurance (if in FEMA flood zone) — adds $500–$3,000+/year
- Liability coverage (typically included in property insurance)
- Wind/hail coverage (coastal areas — separate policy may be required)

**Cost:** $1,200–$3,000/year (SFR rental), varies significantly by location

**Who does what:**
| Role | Action |
|---|---|
| **Borrower** | Gets quotes from 3+ insurance agents, selects policy |
| **Insurance Agent** | Provides binder with mortgagee clause to title company |
| **Underwriter** | Verifies coverage amounts and mortgagee clause |

**⚠️ Insurance is often underestimated as a delay source.** Investors buying in coastal, wildfire, or flood zones may struggle to find affordable coverage quickly.

---

### Phase 5: Underwriting Conditions (Days 10–25)

**What happens:** After receiving appraisal, title commitment, and insurance binder, the underwriter reviews everything and issues a **condition sheet** (list of items that must be cleared before CTC).

**Common DSCR underwriting conditions:**

| Condition | Typical Resolution Time | Platform Can Help? |
|---|---|---|
| Updated bank statements (sufficient funds) | 1–2 days | ✅ Pre-verify funds |
| LLC good standing verification | 1–3 days | ✅ Auto-verify entity status |
| Lease agreement review (existing tenants) | 2–5 days | ✅ Validate lease vs market rent |
| Insurance verification (mortgagee clause) | 1–2 days | ✅ Insurance checklist |
| Appraisal follow-up questions | 3–7 days | ⚠️ Limited |
| Property condition/repair requirements | 5–15 days | ❌ Physical inspection needed |
| Flood certification | 1–2 days | ✅ Auto-check FEMA maps |
| Entity foreign registration | 3–7 days | ✅ Alert early if out-of-state |

**Who does what:**
| Role | Action |
|---|---|
| **Underwriter** | Reviews all docs, issues condition sheet, clears conditions |
| **Processor** | Tracks conditions, follows up with borrower |
| **Borrower** | Responds to conditions within 24–48 hours (best practice) |
| **Loan Officer/Broker** | Escalates delays, coordinates between parties |

**Best Practice:** Respond to ALL conditions within 24–48 hours. Every day of delay extends closing timeline.

---

### Phase 6: Clear to Close (Days 20–35)

**What happens:** All conditions satisfied → underwriter issues "Clear to Close" (CTC). This is the milestone — the loan is fully approved.

**After CTC:**
- Closing team (title company or closing attorney) prepares closing documents
- Includes: mortgage note, deed of trust, settlement statement, all disclosures
- Loan goes to doc drawing department

**Who does what:**
| Role | Action |
|---|---|
| **Underwriter** | Issues CTC |
| **Processor** | Confirms CTC, coordinates closing date |
| **Title Company/Closing Agent** | Prepares closing documents |
| **Closing Agent/Notary** | Scheduled for signing appointment |

---

### Phase 7: Closing Disclosure (3 Business Days Before Closing)

**What happens:** Federal law requires borrower receives Closing Disclosure (CD) at least **3 business days** before closing date. This document details every cost, credit, and adjustment.

**Key items to verify on CD:**
- Loan amount and interest rate match agreement
- Monthly payment breakdown (PITIA)
- All closing costs accurately reflected
- Prepayment penalty terms
- Cash due at closing matches expectations
- DSCR ratio stated correctly

**⚠️ CRITICAL:** If there are material changes to the CD after issuance, the 3-day clock resets, delaying closing.

**Who does what:**
| Role | Action |
|---|---|
| **Lender** | Prepares and sends CD |
| **Borrower** | Reviews CD, raises questions within 24 hours |
| **Loan Officer/Broker** | Walks borrower through CD, answers questions |

---

### Phase 8: Signing / Closing (Day of Closing)

**What happens:** Borrower signs all final documents.

**Signing options:**

| Method | Availability | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **In-person** | All states | At title company/attorney office, 45–60 min |
| **Remote Online Notarization (RON)** | 40+ states ✅ | Digital signing via video call — ideal for out-of-state investors |
| **Power of Attorney** | Most states | Trusted representative signs on borrower's behalf |

**Documents signed:**
- Mortgage note (promissory note)
- Deed of trust / mortgage
- Settlement statement (HUD-1 or Closing Disclosure)
- Affidavits and declarations
- Wire transfer authorization
- IRS forms (W-9 for entity)

**Who does what:**
| Role | Action |
|---|---|
| **Borrower** | Signs documents, wires funds |
| **Closing Agent / Notary** | Administers signing, notarizes documents |
| **Title Company** | Collects and holds funds in escrow |

---

### Phase 9: Funding & Recording (1–3 Days After Signing)

**What happens:** Lender wires loan proceeds to title company. Title company disburses funds to seller, pays off existing liens, distributes closing costs, and records deed/mortgage with county.

**Funding types by state:**

| State Type | Funding Timeline | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Table funding states** | Same day as signing | Funds disperse at closing table |
| **Escrow states** | 24–72 hours after signing | Common in CA, AZ, OR, WA |

**Post-closing:**
- Borrower receives recorded deed (may take 2–4 weeks)
- Loan may be sold/transferred to servicer (normal — doesn't change terms)
- Title insurance policy issued
- Set up mortgage payment with servicer (auto-debit available)
- Coordinate tenant handoff if property has existing renters
- Begin tenant placement if vacant (critical for DSCR!)

### Top 5 Delays — Detailed

#### 1. Appraisal Delays (Most Common & Most Impactful)

**Why it happens:**
- Appraiser shortage in hot markets
- Complex properties require more time
- AMC scheduling bottlenecks
- Appraiser asks for additional comps

**How to avoid:**
- Order appraisal Day 1 (don't wait for full doc submission)
- Request rush appraisal ($200–$400 extra)
- Use lenders with strong AMC relationships
- Avoid unusual properties (mixed-use, condotels, rural)
- Pre-check value with AVM or comps analysis

**Platform opportunity:** Integrate AVM (Automated Valuation Model) to give borrowers early warning about potential appraisal gaps. This won't replace the appraisal but helps borrowers prepare for the worst case.

#### 2. Missing Documentation

**Why it happens:**
- First-time investors don't know what's needed
- Entity documents aren't in order
- Bank statements are outdated (>30 days old)
- Operating agreement doesn't authorize signing member

**How to avoid:**
- Use lender-specific document checklist BEFORE applying
- Have entity documents ready before making offers
- Keep bank statements current (within 30 days)
- Verify Operating Agreement authorizes the signing member

**Platform opportunity:** Lender-specific document checklists with auto-validation. Alert when docs are missing or stale.

#### 3. Title Issues

**Why it happens:**
- Unreleased liens from previous owners
- Judgment liens against seller
- Boundary disputes or encroachments
- Missing assignments in chain of title
- Unpaid property taxes

**How to avoid:**
- Order title work immediately upon contract execution
- Request preliminary title report during due diligence period
- Work with title companies experienced in investment properties
- For REO/foreclosure properties, expect more title complications

**Platform opportunity:** Early title order prompt. Partnership with title companies for expedited service.

#### 4. Insurance Procurement

**Why it happens:**
- Investors don't know they need landlord/investment property insurance
- Coastal/flood zone properties are hard to insure
- Insurance agents unfamiliar with LLC-named policies
- Mortgagee clause requirements delay binder issuance

**How to avoid:**
- Start getting insurance quotes BEFORE going under contract
- Use insurance agents experienced with investment properties
- Have LLC information ready when requesting quotes
- Know if property is in flood zone before offering

**Platform opportunity:** Insurance marketplace with investment-property-specialized agents. FEMA flood zone auto-check.

#### 5. Entity Document Problems

**Why it happens:**
- LLC not in good standing (missed annual filings)
- Operating Agreement doesn't authorize borrowing
- LLC registered in wrong state
- EIN doesn't match entity name
- Foreign entity registration not filed

**How to avoid:**
- Form LLC and get EIN BEFORE making offers
- Verify good standing with Secretary of State
- Ensure Operating Agreement authorizes member to borrow
- If LLC is out-of-state, file foreign registration early (3–7 days)
- Use entity name consistently on all documents

**Platform opportunity:** Entity formation wizard. Good standing auto-check. Foreign registration requirement detector.

## 7. PRE-APPROVAL VS. PRE-QUALIFICATION FOR DSCR

### Definitions

| | Pre-Qualification | Pre-Approval |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | High-level estimate of borrowing power | Formal underwriting review of borrower profile |
| **Documentation** | Basic info (FICO, entity, target property type) | Full documentation (entity docs, bank statements, credit pull) |
| **Credit Pull** | Soft pull or no pull | Hard credit pull ✅ |
| **Validity** | Indefinite (informational) | 60–90 days typically |
| **Commitment** | None — informational only | Conditional commitment subject to property |
| **Time to Get** | Minutes to hours | 2–5 business days |
| **Strength with Sellers** | ❌ Weak — no real proof | ✅ Strong — shows you're serious |
| **Days Saved at Close** | 0–3 days | **5–10 days** |

### Do DSCR Lenders Offer Pre-Approvals?

**Yes, most major DSCR lenders offer some form of pre-approval:**

| Lender | Pre-Approval Available? | Type | Validity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kiavi** | ✅ Yes | Full pre-approval with credit pull | 60–90 days |
| **Visio Lending** | ✅ Yes | Pre-qualification → pre-approval | 90 days |
| **Lima One Capital** | ✅ Yes | Pre-approval letter available | 60 days |
| **Griffin Funding** | ✅ Yes | Pre-approval with soft/hard pull | 60–90 days |
| **Ridge Street Capital** | ✅ Yes | Pre-approval for qualified borrowers | 60 days |
| **Dominion Financial** | ⚠️ Limited | Express process (fast enough not to need pre-approval) | N/A |
| **Angel Oak** | ✅ Yes (via broker) | Broker-submitted pre-approval | 60–90 days |

### Pre-Approval Process for DSCR

1. **Submit borrower information** (FICO, entity details, investment experience)
2. **Hard credit pull** (lender runs tri-merge credit report)
3. **Entity verification** (LLC good standing, EIN confirmation)
4. **Reserve verification** (bank statements showing sufficient funds)
5. **Issue pre-approval letter** (subject to property appraisal and title)

**Pre-approval covers the BORROWER side.** The PROPERTY side (appraisal, title, insurance, DSCR calculation) still happens after going under contract.

### How the Platform Can Provide Instant Pre-Qualification

| Step | Platform Action | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1. User enters basic info | FICO estimate, entity status, target loan amount | 2 minutes |
| 2. Soft credit check | Pull credit without hard inquiry | 30 seconds |
| 3. Entity verification | Auto-check Secretary of State records | 10 seconds |
| 4. Lender matching | Match borrower profile to eligible lenders | Instant |
| 5. DSCR estimate | User enters target rent → calculate DSCR | 1 minute |
| 6. Generate pre-qualification letter | PDF with estimated borrowing power | Instant |
| **Total** | | **~5 minutes** |

**Note:** Pre-qualification is NOT pre-approval. The platform should clearly label it as an estimate and guide users toward formal pre-approval with a specific lender.

### Critical Path Analysis

```
CRITICAL PATH (Cannot be parallelized):
Day 1-3:   Application → Doc submission
Day 3:     Appraisal ordered (START critical path)
Day 3-17:  Appraisal in progress (5-14 days)
Day 17-25: Underwriting review + conditions (depends on appraisal)
Day 25-28: Clear to close + CD (3-day regulatory wait)
Day 28-29: Signing
Day 29-32: Funding + recording

PARALLEL PATH (Can run simultaneously with appraisal):
Day 3-10:  Title search + commitment
Day 3-15:  Insurance binding
Day 1-2:   Flood certification
Day 1-7:   Entity verification
```

**Maximum parallelization saves 10–15 days vs. sequential processing.** Most efficient lenders run all parallel items simultaneously from Day 3.

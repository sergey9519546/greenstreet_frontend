## 1. Beeline trailer — probable structure (decoded)

A 75-second fintech "trailer" for investors almost always follows this beat map, and
Beeline's style points squarely at it:

1. **Cold open / tension (0–8s).** A relatable pain, shot like a movie. The old way:
   paperwork avalanche, a banker saying "no," endless waiting. Often comedic/exaggerated.
2. **Logo sting / promise (8–14s).** Brand mark slams in with a one-line manifesto
   ("There's a faster way to build your portfolio").
3. **The turn (14–35s).** The product as hero. Fast cuts: tap phone → instant quote →
   "approved." Energetic, kinetic typography, big numbers on screen.
4. **Proof / scale (35–55s).** Properties stacking up, a map lighting up, cash flow,
   "investors in X states," social proof. The dream made concrete.
5. **The vibe / identity (55–68s).** Pure brand mood — confident music peak, the
   lifestyle the loan unlocks (keys, doors, a portfolio empire).
6. **Resolution / CTA (68–75s).** Logo, tagline, URL, one button. "Start in minutes."

**Craft signatures to borrow:**
- Cut on the beat of the music; the track drives the edit, not the other way around.
- Big, bold, animated type stating numbers and claims (DSCR, "minutes," "no W-2s").
- One idea per shot. Trailers are a series of single punches.
- Tension → release structure. Earn the product reveal with a problem first.
- A confident, slightly cheeky voice — but earned by clarity, not noise.

## 2. Translate, don't copy: GreenStreet's own voice

Beeline = irreverent, jokey, millennial. If you simply copy that tone you become a
me-too. Your North Star (`GREENSTREET_NORTH_STAR.md`) points somewhere more powerful
and more ownable:

> **Beeline sells "fast and fun." You sell "the door that was closed is open."**

Keep from Beeline: the kinetic trailer craft, the speed, the boldness, the
problem→product→dream arc, music-driven cutting.

Change for GreenStreet: trade the jokes for **aspiration + possibility + trust**. Your
emotional core is the investor — especially the foreign national — who was told "no"
and now hears "yes." That's cinematic in a richer way than comedy. Think *confident,
warm, global, cinematic* — closer to an Apple/airline "possibility" film than a meme.

Brand world to lean on: **the forest you already planted** — programs named Maple, Oak,
Birch, Cedar. Green = growth. Street/map = property and place. "Plant a portfolio."
Use a map-and-growth visual language, not skylines-and-handshakes.

## 4. Higgsfield production pack

**Reality of your budget:** Plus plan, **211 credits**. Video clips cost meaningfully
more than images. 211 credits is enough for **a handful of hero video shots or one
short (~15–20s) teaser**, not a fully rendered 9-shot 75s film. Plan in tiers:

- **Tier A (proof, cheap):** generate 2–3 hero *still* images to lock the look. Cheapest.
- **Tier B (teaser):** turn the 3 best stills into 5s video clips → a ~15s teaser.
- **Tier C (full trailer):** all 9 shots as video + music + edit → needs a credit top-up.

**Recommended workflow (consistency-first):**
1. **Lock the look with images first.** Generate each shot as a still (cheaper, faster
   iteration). Use a consistent style line in every prompt so shots match.
2. **Animate the keepers.** Feed each approved still into an image-to-video model as the
   `start_image` so the motion matches the look you approved.
3. **Assemble** in your editor with a music track; cut on the beat. (The repo already
   has `explainer-reel.mp4` — we can match or replace its style.)

**Model guidance (confirm via `models_explore` at run time):**
- Stills / brand frames: a high-quality image model (e.g., the marketing/commercial
  image model for ad-grade frames; a portrait model for the human shots).
- Image-to-video: a start-frame animation model for clean, controlled motion; a
  multi-shot/identity model if we need the same person across shots.
- Always run `get_cost: true` first on each generation to preflight credits.
